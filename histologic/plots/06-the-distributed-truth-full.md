# The Distributed Truth: Full Plot Outline (v2)

**Status:** CURRENT. Revision 2, Sept 2026. It replaces the Nov 2025 plot. It is consistent with the short stories, the rewritten *The Correction*, the drafted *The Lost Hour*, `plots/00-FOUNDATION-MANUSCRIPT.md`, `characters/` and `worldbuilding/`.
**Genre:** a political thriller and conspiracy mystery. A war of facts, a betrayal nobody commits, and a revolution that has to outrun its own sponsors.
**Structure:** Prologue + 30 chapters + Epilogue, in four parts. About 110,000 words (about 3,400 words per chapter).
**Timeline:** Tuesday, October 30, 2108 – Thursday, February 14, 2109. Epilogue on Friday, March 15, 2109.
**Consistency rule:** this book must agree with Books 1–5. Books 7–9 will be rewritten to fit it.

## High Concept

A year after the Lost Hour, seventeen minutes vanish from the life-logs of Chronos, a nation that records every moment of every life. Chronos's Judge finds the Lost Hour's technique in the wound and names Veridica. By the autumn of 2108 the two nations are fighting a **war of facts**: contradictory histories broadcast into each other's border districts, fact storms spreading inland, a lakeshore population that no longer knows which pier is real. The Continuity survivors know whose hand made the gap. They chose silence in Book 5, and the silence has become a war.

Veridica's government offers Marcus Chen, the nation's most trusted restorer, a way to end the war in a month: **Operation KINDRED**. Befriend Chronos's senior historian, **Julian Kovač**, a lonely man who admires him, and take the reconciliation key that lets Veridica "restore" Chronos's record from the true source. It is Protocol R-2 turned outward, with Marcus as the attachment figure.

Marcus refuses, and he publishes: his own orders, and fragments of *The Foundation of Certainty* that start arriving from two different senders, one of whom is a friend of Old Songs whom everyone has believed dead for forty years. The war stops. The government confesses first and offers a remedy: the **Concordance**, a national index of every citizen's journal. It is exactly what the heroes wanted, and it is **the trap**. The last part of the book is a race to keep "many hands" from becoming one record again.

**The question the book asks:** if every victory can be designed, is there any way to win that the designers can't use? The book's answer is partial and hard-won: **you can't stop them riding your revolution, but you can refuse to give it a center.**

## The Manuscript Thread

Book 6 reveals, and only reveals (`00-FOUNDATION-MANUSCRIPT.md` §5):
- the **Compiler's Introduction** (pp. 2–4, no mark), recognised by Old Songs as the voice of **Elise Vance**;
- **Principle Three** (pp. 13–17, mark YP·02, unread). Kira recognises her stolen fact in it, and KINDRED's technical annex;
- **Principle Five** (pp. 23–28, mark JM·08, unread). For the Volkovs it is their father's death, planned on paper. For Sokolov it is her job;
- a **strip of the table of contents**, showing only the lines for Principles Four to Six with their page numbers. It identifies Singh's page 30 as **Principle Six**, but shows none of the other headings;
- the **Compiler's Warning** (handwritten, March 2066, Vance's copy only), the catalyst for publication;
- **Vance herself**, who confesses.

**Two delivery routes, two senders (this is the engine of Twist 2):**
1. **Vance's route.** The Introduction, the table-of-contents strip and the Warning come from the **Compiler's own copy**: no copy numeral, and the Warning is handwritten. They are hidden in the bindings of old songbooks that a patient on Tanaka's ward asks her to take "to the old gentleman who sings on your broadcasts". Tanaka carries them across the lake without knowing.
2. **The other route.** Principle Three and Principle Five arrive as **anonymous deposits at the Commission's public testimony intake**, which Kovač runs. They are typed with the chipped *e* and carry the copy numeral **X**. (Singh's page was XI.) Kovač notices that the intake log records a deposit **but no visitor walked through the intake that day** (a Twist 2 clue).

**Rules held:** nobody names Principles One, Two or Four, the Register, the Annex or the heirs. Nobody decodes a mark. Nobody knows whose copy X or XI is. Characters may guess (Marcus notes that XI came from the Ministry's own correspondence), but they may not know.

## The Four Twists

### Twist 1: The target was also the hunter. (Ch 8.)
Marcus has gone some way into KINDRED, telling himself he is only studying it. When he finally resolves to refuse, he tells Julian the truth face to face in the garden outside the shielded conference rooms, where Julian's Chronos interface will record every word. Julian goes white and then laughs, because he has his own orders. **Chronos's Administration sent him to do the same thing to Marcus: Directive MERIDIAN**, to win the trust of Veridica's restorer and turn him. Both nations ran R-2 on the same two men at the same table. The confession is in Chronos's permanent record the moment it is spoken. Neither man can take it back, and that is the point: the refusal becomes a **double publication**.
- **Clues:**
  - Julian asks Marcus the same questions Marcus was trained to ask him (Ch 6).
  - Julian's aide steers them into shielded rooms and Julian steers them out (Ch 6).
  - Kovač: "Chronos never sends him anywhere. Why now?" (Ch 7).
  - Julian knows the name of Marcus's desk (Ch 6).

### Twist 2: The remedy was ready before the disease was announced. (Ch 24.)
Publication works. The war stops. Within a day First Minister Maitland **confesses first** and offers the **Concordance**: every citizen's journal registered, indexed and weighed by The Judge as testimony. It is the Doctrine made universal, it is popular, and the Commission is to run it. Then Vance, found in Ch 21, says she sent **only three things** (the Introduction, the strip and the Warning). She did not send Principles Three and Five. Maya finds that the Concordance's architecture was **drafted by the Technical Director's office weeks before the publication** it answers, and that the anonymous X deposits entered the intake **from inside the Ministry network**.

**Someone fed them the fragments, in the order that made publication inevitable, and had the remedy waiting.** Page 30 says it: *"Arrange to be the one who restores it."* The Concordance restores the record by absorbing the journals into it. It creates a list of everyone who remembers, and digitised journals can be edited from the true source (Principle Three, which the heroes themselves just published). Kira: "They didn't stop our publication. They finished it."
- **Clues:**
  - The X pages arrive exactly when needed: Three the week KINDRED's annex is questioned, Five the week the border must be argued (Ch 9–10).
  - No visitor came through the intake (Ch 9).
  - Maitland's speech is too polished for a twelve-hour turnaround (Ch 17).
  - Wei is pleased by the publication (Ch 15, 17).
  - Reeves, visited by Maya, recognises the Concordance at a glance: "Give them a trusted index and see what grows" (Ch 23).
  - The Kestrel falsifier was paid by a Concordance outreach officer (Ch 19).

### Twist 3: The Compiler has been on Tanaka's ward for two years. (Ch 21.)
**Dr. Elise Vance** is alive at 72. She is Tanaka's long-term patient, **"Mrs. M. Kerr"**, in the teaching hospital's **sealed cognitive ward**, the only place in Chronos where a person's life-log is clinically sealed and nobody records them. The "tall, stooped" hospital director who has protected Tanaka since 2106 for reasons he never explained is **her son, Dr. Soren Vale**. She asked him to, because the escapees are proof that her design can be survived. She chose the alias for Anselm Kerr, the mentor she and Mikhail shared at the Archive, who was corrected in 2067. When Old Songs hears the full ward name, he knows.

The reunion of the woman who built the prison and the man who survived it is the emotional heart of the book.
- **Clues:**
  - Tanaka's case notes: "Patient M.K., 72: lucid beyond her chart; tremor, right hand; asks after 'the old man who sings'" (Ch 3).
  - The eleventh memorandum: the patient read it and said "Write a twelfth" (Ch 3; Book 5 Epilogue).
  - The songbooks come from "a patient" whose name Tanaka won't give, out of clinical confidentiality (Ch 5).
  - The pages smell faintly of the ward's disinfectant (Ch 5).
  - The patient knows the origin of the Chronos stabilisation technique too well (Ch 14).
  - The Manuscript's own voice note: Vance is "precise, with a hidden tremor".
- **Red herring:** Professor **Park Ji-won** (74), the Synthesis mediator at the Lakes Talks, who reminisces about "the old capital Archive". Old Songs half-believes she is Elise until he hears her voice (Ch 13): "No. Elise never said 'perhaps' in her life."

### Twist 4: Two hands, one on each side of the lake. (Ch 30.)
Nikolai finishes the forensic comparison he has worked on since Nov 2107. The Chronos Gap carries Wei's signature, the same backward re-sealing of the chain as the Lost Hour. But it couldn't have been done from outside: it needed a **Chronos-side counter-authorisation** with its own habits. The key that countersigned the Chronos Gap is the **senior reconciliation key of the Chronos Temporal Archive, which belongs to Julian Kovač.** Julian's own log (Ch 11) shows him asleep through those seventeen minutes, like four million other people, and a Chronos log can be a lie only if someone holds the true source.

Did Julian do it? Was his key used? Was the man who thanked Marcus for not using him the other hand all along? **The book doesn't answer.** The heroes realise, and can only *guess* (Principle Two is Book 7), that the war between the nations was **run by people on both sides working together**.
- **Clues:**
  - Julian's reconciliation key is the thing both states wanted (Ch 2, 6).
  - His log from Dec 13, 2107 shows seventeen minutes "asleep", when Chronos logs sleep, every breath of it (Ch 11).
  - He offers the key to Marcus without being asked (Ch 11).
  - Nikolai's months of work (Book 5 Epilogue; Ch 18).

**This twist sets up Book 7,** in which Marcus carries out the operation he refused and destroys Julian (`SERIES-OVERVIEW.md` §4).

## Structure

### Prologue: The Doubled Pier (Ruth Calloway, Tue Oct 30, 2108, night)
Kestrel Point at the end of the ferry season. A storm alert at 21:40, the third that week. The onset is sensation, never weather: a pressure behind the eyes, then the certainty that the old pier at the end of Harbour Street has always been there (Chronos) *and* was demolished in 2089 (Veridica). Wren, fourteen, walks towards it in the fog, onto boards her mind can see. Ruth catches her at the edge of the drop. In the kitchen afterwards Ruth opens the **Kestrel Book**, which the town began in the spring from a copy of Kira's method, and writes the entry with two neighbours countersigning in their own hands. The book is the only thing in town that agrees with itself. **Ending:** walking home, she passes a gray Border Security van parked at the end of the lakeshore road with its engine running and a mast folded on its roof. The storm came from *our* side.

### PART ONE: THE WAR OF FACTS (Nov 1 – Nov 22)
*Theme: the cost of silence; an offer that looks like mercy.*

**Ch 1: The Seventeen Minutes** (Kira, Thu Nov 1). A Commission session on the war's casualties, with Wei presenting the "attribution analysis" of the Chronos Gap. His conclusion is reasonable, kind and wrong: a Chronos fabrication, or Reeves's technique leaked. Kira sits with the knowledge of whose hand it was and the note she filed in January. Flashback to Jan 2108, when Chronos published its analysis: she recognised the seam in an hour and said nothing, because they had all agreed. Now there are thirty-one dead. After the session, Kovač stops her in the corridor with a quiet question about Adrian: "Has he written to you?" He hasn't. **Ending:** Marcus is called to the First Minister's office "tonight, alone", and Wei, passing, says pleasantly, "Good luck."

**Ch 2: KINDRED** (Marcus, Thu Nov 1, night). Maitland's office, with Chen Zhao and Wei present. The offer: talks in the neutral zone under Synthesis mediation, with Marcus as Veridica's delegate. The real task: befriend Julian Kovač, Chronos's senior historian, who holds the reconciliation key to Chronos's backup architecture. With it, Veridica's technicians can "restore" Chronos's account of the Gap from the true source, and the war ends in a month. Wei's technical annex is elegant. Maitland: "One man's trust, against four hundred casualties." Marcus recognises the method: this is R-2, with himself as the seed. He asks for time and gets a week. **Ending:** in the file, the target's photograph. The surname is Kovač.

**Ch 3: The Sealed Ward** (Tanaka, Mon Nov 5, Chronos). The war has revived "stabilisation", now as treatment for storm casualties, and Tanaka recognises Continuity under a Chronos name. Her director, Dr. Vale, blocks it again without explaining why. Her ward round includes **Patient M.K., 72**, long-term, in the sealed wing where nobody is logged. Her case notes: lucid beyond her chart; a tremor in the right hand; reads every memorandum Tanaka writes ("Write a twelfth"); asks after "the old man who sings on your broadcasts". **Ending:** M.K. gives her two old border songbooks, "for the old gentleman, from an admirer. Paper should go home." Tanaka puts them in her bag.

**Ch 4: The Kestrel Book** (Ruth, Wed Nov 7). How the book works: every entry countersigned, and disagreement written beside it in the other person's hand. The storm schedule, which Ruth has started to notice follows the ferry timetable. League of the Record leaflets appear under doors: *THE JOURNALS ARE CONTAMINATION.* Ruth goes to a Commission "listening session" in a church hall and meets Kira, who is quietly impressed. **Ending:** a Chronos broadcast rewrites Kestrel Point's founding. Half the neighbours now "remember" a Chronos town charter. Ruth opens the book. The book remembers otherwise, in twelve hands.

**Ch 5: The Compiler's Voice** (Old Songs, Fri Nov 9, the resistance farmhouse). Alexei brings the songbooks from Tanaka. In the binding of the second is a folded typescript: the **Compiler's Introduction** (*"…Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided by a public. It must be decided for one…"*). No numeral, and a chipped *e*. Old Songs reads it twice and stops humming. It is not the words. It is how the argument turns: a young woman at the Archive in 2063, on Thursdays, winning every argument by conceding the first point. **"Elise."** Tanaka, asked where the books came from, will only say "a patient", out of confidentiality. The pages smell faintly of the ward's disinfectant, but nobody knows the smell except Tanaka, and she isn't there. **Ending:** Old Songs writes the name on paper, which he has never done with anything he loved: *Elise Vance.*

**Ch 6: The Lakes Talks** (Marcus, Mon Nov 12 – Thu Nov 15). The talks open in a requisitioned hall in the neutral zone. The mediator is **Professor Park Ji-won** of the Synthesis Institute, who talks warmly of "the old capital Archive". Julian Kovač is gentle, precise and funny, and knows the name of Marcus's desk ("14-08, I think? I read your testimony"). He keeps asking the questions Marcus was trained to ask him. Julian's aide steers them into the shielded rooms, and Julian steers them out into the garden. Marcus, telling himself he is only studying KINDRED, goes on. On Thursday it is the Day of Testimony, and Julian asks to watch the readings on a screen with him. **Ending:** Julian: "In Chronos nobody forgets anything. You can't imagine how lonely it is." Marcus can. That is the problem.

**Ch 7: Aunt Helena** (Kovač, Sat Nov 17). Kovač sees the broadcast of the talks and understands in one frame. She goes to Marcus's flat. Julian is her brother's elder son, and Adrian's brother. He left for Chronos at nineteen, and the family split along the lake. "The state has used my family once. Adrian was sent to Kira. Now you are sent to Julian." She tells him about Julian's childhood, and about a boy who kept lists. And a question she can't answer: *"Chronos never lets Julian out of the Archive. Why send him to talks now?"* **Ending:** she gives Marcus the first letter Adrian has written her in three years. It is one line: *They're going to use him. Don't let them.*

**Ch 8: MERIDIAN** (Marcus, Thu Nov 22). **Twist 1.** Kira has refused to speak about KINDRED for a week. Elena has said one thing: "It always becomes real on one side." Marcus decides. In the garden, where Julian's interface records everything, he tells Julian the truth: the orders, the key, the week. Julian goes white, and then he laughs, helplessly, and takes out his own directive. **MERIDIAN:** win the restorer's trust, and turn him. The two of them sit on a bench in the fog, each holding the order to seduce the other. **Ending:** Julian: "It's in my log now. I can't take it back." Marcus: "Good. Neither can I."

### PART TWO: THE FRAGMENTS (Nov 26 – Dec 17)
*Theme: the design, on paper; the decision to publish by many hands.*

**Ch 9: The True Source** (Kira, Mon Nov 26). Kovač calls her to the intake. An anonymous deposit: five typed pages with the chipped *e*, copy numeral **X**. It is **Principle Three**: *"Maintain three backup systems that appear independent and are not… Should insiders reform it, it may be restored from the true source."* Kira reads the mechanism of her own stolen fact, and of Marcus's 0.58→0.73, and of KINDRED's annex: Veridica already knows how to reach Chronos's true source. Kovač, uneasy: the intake log records the deposit, **but no visitor passed the desk that day.** Kira files that under UNCHECKED. **Ending:** X, not XI. "Someone else has a copy."

**Ch 10: The Calibration Grounds** (Dmitri, Wed Nov 28). A second deposit, again copy X: **Principle Five**. *"Border regions of broadcast overlap should be preserved, not resolved: they are where a population's tolerance for contradiction may be measured, and raised."* Ivan Volkov's discrepancy, and his death as Test Subject 2471, planned on paper in 2066, twenty-four years before it happened. Dmitri takes the page to the border checkpoint and puts it in front of Sokolov. She reads her job, written in 2066. Two formal people, one bench, a long silence. Alexei wants to burn something. Nikolai wants to know who is sending the pages. **Ending:** Sokolov: "Then I am an instrument." Dmitri: "So was I. It is not a permanent condition."

**Ch 11: Seventeen Minutes Asleep** (Julian, Fri Nov 30, Chronos). **Julian's only POV chapter.** His life in Chronos: every moment logged, his flat, his work at the Archive, his loneliness, and his brother Adrian, whom he hasn't spoken to in years. Administrator Aubry debriefs him; she already has the garden confession in his log, and wants to use it. Julian decides to publish with Marcus instead. At home he scrolls his own log back to Dec 13, 2107, as every Chronos citizen has done: 03:12 to 03:29, *asleep*, heart rate steady, nothing. He has never thought about it. **Ending:** he writes to Marcus, offering what nobody asked him for: "If it helps, I can show you the reconciliation key. So you can see there is nothing behind it." *(A Twist 4 plant.)*

**Ch 12: The Third Fragment** (Isaiah, Mon Dec 3, SFW). Kira sends him the Introduction. Page 30 and the sung essay were one and two, so this is three, and his rule is satisfied. His SFW editors are delighted in a way that worries him: "The Histologic world confesses." A military attaché asks for "the full set, for assessment". Isaiah agrees to publish, but only on the same hour as Marcus and Julian, so no single nation owns it. **Ending:** he writes the headline in the parlour of his memory palace, and the aunt at the head of the table is watching him. *(SO·04 stays unspoken.)*

**Ch 13: The Table of Contents** (Old Songs, Wed Dec 5). A third songbook, through Tanaka, holds a **strip of a contents page**: three lines, IV, V and VI, with page numbers. Principle Six begins at page 29, which makes Singh's page 30 a page of **Principle Six**. Marcus, Kira and Old Songs sit with what that means: the Commission that restored the record is a principle, and they are its restorers. Old Songs listens to a broadcast of Professor Park and lets his half-hope go: "No. Elise never said 'perhaps' in her life." He begins, quietly, to hum something new, a round. **Ending:** Marcus: "We were always going to be the restorers." Old Songs: "Then stop restoring. Start copying."

**Ch 14: Stabilisation** (Tanaka, Fri Dec 7, Chronos). The Temporal Defence Administration overrides Dr. Vale, and the first "stabilisation" cradles arrive. Patient M.K. dictates, from memory, the clinical origin of the technique ("a paraphrase of something old, about compassion"), and Tanaka turns it into a devastating twelfth memorandum that stops the order again. Tanaka notes, in case-note form, that her patient knows too much, and doesn't ask. She is a clinician, and confidentiality is the one wall she built herself. **Ending:** M.K. gives her one more book "for the old gentleman. The last one, I think." Tanaka carries it.

**Ch 15: The Warning** (Marcus, Sun Dec 9). In the last binding is one handwritten page, dated **March 2066**, in a young woman's hand: **the Compiler's Warning.** *"Reformers will try to fix it. They will fail, because the design anticipates them… There is no reform path, only replacement. Distribute truth. Distribute verification. Accept uncertainty. Build by many hands."* The decision: not the Commission, and not one outlet. **Many hands.** Publication at 14:00 on Monday Dec 17, in three nations at once:
- Marcus's KINDRED orders and the fragments, released into every journal network, the Commission's public archive and on the Justice Center steps;
- Julian's MERIDIAN orders, released from his Chronos log;
- Isaiah in the SFW and Synthesis.

Elena backs him in the one conversation they've had about 2105 in three years ("I did this to you. I know what it costs on both sides"). Kira backs him without being asked. Wei, told as a courtesy, says, "Good." **Ending:** Marcus writes the entry for Monday in advance, and leaves the "Verification" line blank.

**Ch 16: Fourteen Hundred** (Ruth, Mon Dec 17). Publication day, seen from Kestrel Point. The fragments arrive on paper, in the ferry office, by the hands of a courier Ruth has never met. Wren reads Principle Five aloud to a kitchen full of neighbours. At the lakeshore road, the gray van's mast comes down and the van drives away: Sokolov has ordered every transmitter unit to stand down. On the Chronos side, the Judge reads the will of millions changing in their logs and determines, at 0.88, that corrective broadcasting "no longer serves the collective interest". **Ending:** the first night without a storm since May. Ruth writes: *Today the war stopped. Witnesses: seventeen.* Then she crosses out seventeen and writes *everyone*, and then crosses that out too, because it isn't how the book works, and writes *seventeen* again.

### PART THREE: THE CONCORDANCE (Dec 18 – Jan 9)
*Theme: the embrace; the remedy that is a cage.*

**Ch 17: The Remedy** (Marcus, Tue Dec 18). By noon the First Minister has **confessed first**. KINDRED is "an error of judgement, now ended". Maitland pays tribute to "our commissioners' courage" and announces the **Concordance**: every citizen's journal may be registered, indexed and weighed by The Judge as testimony, with the Commission to run it. The capital cheers. Chen Zhao is warm. Wei presents the architecture, which is beautiful. Marcus notices only that the speech was too well made for twelve hours' notice. **Ending:** the Commission's endorsement lies on the table for his signature. Kira: "Don't sign anything tonight."

**Ch 18: The League of the Record** (Nikolai, Sat Dec 22 – Mon Dec 24). Pell's League fills a square: burn the journals and restore the pure record. There is genuine fear under it (people who miss certainty), and professional organisers on top of it. Nikolai watches the League's traffic and finds its logistics come out of a Ministry "public order" budget line. It's a pattern, not proof. He files it beside his other unfinished work: the Chronos Gap countersignature, which he still can't read. **Ending:** Christmas Eve. A journal house in Kestrel Point burns. Alexei, who is there, gets Wren out through a back window. The Kestrel Book is in Ruth's coat.

**Ch 19: The False Entry** (Ruth, Thu Dec 27). Cross-checking after the fire, the Kestrel Book finds entries that don't hold: **Emmet Varley** has been inventing witnessed events, and countersigning them himself under two names. The network argues about exclusion or probation, and settles on probation, with his entries marked in the margin in red. Then Varley confesses the rest. A pleasant man from **Concordance outreach** paid him, "to show that unregistered books can't be trusted". **Ending:** Ruth understands that the League wants her book burned and the Concordance wants it registered, and that neither wants it *free*.

**Ch 20: The Crossing** (Alexei, Wed Jan 2, 2109). Old Songs has been reading Tanaka's case notes, which she finally shows Kira without names. The hospital, the sealed ward, the tremor. He insists on crossing: a dead man with no papers, into a nation that logs everyone. Alexei and Dmitri take him across the lake by the old smugglers' causeway in fog (real weather). Julian meets them on the far side and walks them through the hospital's service corridors under his own name, which is either courage or something else. **Ending:** the sealed-ward door, and Tanaka on the other side of it, furious and afraid: "You should not be here. Nobody should be here. That is the point of this room."

**Ch 21: Mrs. Kerr** (Tanaka, Thu Jan 3, Chronos). **Twist 3.** Tanaka, confidentiality finally broken by necessity, says the ward name aloud: **Margit Kerr.** Old Songs stops, because Kerr was their teacher. The woman in the bed turns her head and says, "Hello, Misha. You look terrible." It is **Elise Vance.** Dr. Vale in the doorway is her son. He protected Tanaka because his mother asked him to. Tanaka writes her case note in real time, because it is the only way she can stand it. **Ending:** Vance to Old Songs, quietly: "I sent you three things. Only three." Tanaka writes it down: *Patient states she sent three documents.* She counts the pages in her head. There were five.

**Ch 22: Two Old People** (Old Songs, Fri Jan 4). The reunion. **Old Songs's one speech of the book:** forty years of songs, Kerr peeling his apple, and the question he has carried since 2066: "Did you know what it was, when you were writing it?" Her answer: "Yes. That is what I have to live with. Not that I didn't know. That I did, and was frightened, and thought frightened was the same as wise." She tells him why she hid in the one unrecorded room in Chronos, and why she wrote the Warning in March 2066 and told no one. She withholds one page ("There is one page I will not give anyone yet"; Principle Seven, Book 8). She didn't send Three or Five: "Copy X isn't mine." She will not name any of the twelve: "Names would make you a court, and a court is one record." **Ending:** Chronos border directorate officers in the hospital lobby, asking for "Mrs. Kerr" by name. Somebody knew. Vale gets them out through the mortuary.

**Ch 23: The Drafts** (Maya, Mon Jan 7). At the Commission, Maya is assigned to build the Concordance's intake. The design documents' metadata shows they were drafted in the **Technical Director's office from early November**, weeks before the publication they answer. The X deposits entered the public intake from a terminal **inside the Ministry network**. On the 15th of December she had visited her father, and she remembers now what he said when she described the Concordance: *"Give them a trusted index and see what grows. I'd have loved that for Continuity."* **Ending:** she takes it to Kira, on paper.

**Ch 24: The Remedy Was Ready** (Kira, Wed Jan 9). **Twist 2.** Kira, Maya, Nikolai and Marcus lay it out:
- the X pages were fed in the order that made publication inevitable;
- the remedy was drafted before the disease was named;
- Page 30, now known as Principle Six: *"Arrange to be the one who restores it."*

The Concordance **restores the record by absorbing the journals**. It creates a register of every citizen who remembers, and digitised journals can be edited from the true source, the principle they themselves just published. The League is the extreme that makes the Concordance look moderate. Varley's pay came from its outreach budget. *They didn't use the publication to stop them. They used it to finish the design.* **Ending:** Kira: "They didn't stop our publication. They finished it." Marcus, very quietly: "Then we don't give it a center."

### PART FOUR: MANY HANDS (Jan 14 – Feb 14)
*Theme: refusing the index; a reply written by everyone; the second hand.*

**Ch 25: 0.58** (Marcus, Mon Jan 14). The **Concordance Act** comes before The Judge in a public session: should unregistered journals be classed as "unverified records", which makes keeping one relevant to contamination? There are three positions:
- **Pell:** burn them all.
- **Wei**, for the government: register them all, "for their own protection".
- **Marcus:** keep them free, on paper, unindexed, checked by the people who wrote them.

Marcus argues against the most popular reform in Veridica's history, and loses the room. On the chamber panel The Judge updates his contamination index live as he speaks: 0.49… 0.53… 0.58. **Ending:** the panel stops at **0.58**, the number he was once at before someone adjusted it. He looks across the chamber. Wei is looking at the panel too, with polite interest.

**Ch 26: The Night of Copies** (Dmitri, Fri Jan 18 – Sat Jan 19). Kira's **pairing method**: every journal is copied by hand into two others in different towns, so no single seizure matters and no index can hold them. Vance, now smuggled into the Veridican border zone, will give her confession the same way. She will dictate it to **twelve journal keepers in twelve towns on both sides of the lake at once**, over a patched voice line, and each writes it down in their own hand. Twelve is her choice, and she won't say why: "It is a number I owe." (A seed for the signatories, revealed in Books 7 and 9.) Chronos directorate agents and League men are hunting the old woman. Dmitri runs security on the farmhouse, formally and without contractions. Alexei ferries copies across the lake. Nikolai keeps the line open. There is one near-miss at the causeway. **Ending:** dawn. Twelve towns, twelve hands, one confession. Nobody holds the original, because there isn't one.

**Ch 27: The Compiler's Confession** (Vance, Mon Jan 21). **Vance's only POV chapter.** In the Veridican farmhouse where she will live now, by the stove, beside the man she once argued with on Thursdays:
- her confession read aloud in town squares on both sides;
- the 2060s, the seventeen contributors (no names), and the Introduction she wrote in winter 2064;
- the Warning, and the hidden page she won't give up yet;
- why a design that anticipates its reformers can't anticipate a reply it didn't write;
- her tremor, and the time she has.

Old Songs teaches her "Many Hands": *"One hand writes and one hand checks, / one hand holds the light; / one voice fails and one voice mends, / and many make it right."* It needs three voices. **Ending:** Kira comes in from the yard, off-key, and becomes the third.

**Ch 28: The Reply** (Kira, Mon Jan 28 – Mon Feb 4). **The New Foundation**, a reply by many hands that deliberately **mirrors the manuscript's structure**:
- an **Introduction** written collectively;
- **answers** to the Introduction, to Three, Five and Six, and to the Warning, each in a different hand. Kira writes the answer to Three: *"Keep copies no one can recall: on paper, in many houses."* Sokolov writes the answer to Five: *"A border is where two peoples compare notes, not where they are measured."* Marcus writes the answer to Six: *"Trust no restorer, including us."* Julian writes one for Chronos: *"Record less. Remember more."* Wren, fourteen, writes one: *"Teach children to check, not to believe."*
- **three numbered blanks** for Principles One, Two and Four, which nobody has seen: *"We will answer these when we know what they said."*
- an **open register** of blank pages, where anyone may sign;
- **a warning to itself:** *"If this ever becomes one record, burn it and start again."*

It is published nowhere in particular and copied everywhere. On Feb 4 The Judge rules on the Concordance Act: keeping an unregistered journal is **contamination-relevant at 0.41, below threshold**. It can't class a large, concordant part of the nation as contaminated when its testimony agrees with the registered books. The Concordance survives, but voluntary. Many register anyway, and many don't. **Ending:** Marcus's index on the Commission panel reads **0.44**. Kira: "I'd have liked it lower." Marcus: "I'd have liked it checkable."

**Ch 29: Thank You** (Marcus, Mon Feb 11). The last session of the Lakes Talks, now a formality, with the war over by determination. On the bench in the garden, Julian says: **"Thank you for not weaponising my loneliness."** Julian is Chronos's new liaison to the networks, and has started a journal in handwriting, which Chronos finds deeply strange. Back in the capital, Wei stops Marcus in the Commission corridor, pleasant as ever, and hands him a folded page of his journal. It is the Nov 15, 2107 entry (*"I find I want to see what they build"*), and under it a new one, dated Feb 4, 2109: *"They refused the index. That was not in the design either."* Marcus: "You sent the X pages." Wei: "Did I?" **Ending:** Marcus writes in CHECKED: *Feb 11, 2109. Commission corridor. Involved parties: Chen, M.; Wei, T. Event: he wanted us to see it. Verification: the page. Witnesses: none.* Then he goes to find one.

**Ch 30: Two Hands** (Nikolai, Thu Feb 14). **Twist 4.** Nikolai finishes the Chronos Gap forensics he started in Nov 2107. It is Wei's seam, re-sealed backwards, the same as the Lost Hour. But it needed a **Chronos-side countersignature**, with its own habits, from inside Chronos's primary. The countersigning key is the **senior reconciliation key of the Chronos Temporal Archive: Julian Kovač's.** Julian's log shows seventeen minutes asleep, and a Chronos log can lie only if someone holds the true source. Nikolai sits with it for a whole day, then goes to Marcus. **Ending:** Marcus at the window of the flat, looking west towards the lake. "Two hands," he says. "One on each side." He doesn't say the rest: *then the war was never between them.* He writes it under UNCHECKED, because he can't prove it. Yet.

### Epilogue: The Round (Fri Mar 15, 2109)
Rotating vignettes:
- **Kestrel Point:** the ferry season reopens. The old pier is only water, and everyone agrees. The Kestrel Book has forty-three contributors and three copies in three other towns.
- **The central plaza:** Old Songs and Vance sing "Many Hands" by the Lost Hour stone. First three voices, then a dozen, then a crowd that picks it up without knowing who wrote it.
- **Sokolov:** promoted to Major in February, faster than anyone is promoted. She notices.
- **Isaiah in the SFW:** his fragments are in a military briefing folder he wasn't meant to see. The heading: *HISTOLOGIC COORDINATION: STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS* (Book 7–8 setup).
- **Maya:** she visits Reeves. He has read the New Foundation and has only one note: "No index. Clever. They'll hate that."
- **Tanaka:** she starts a twelfth notebook.
- **Julian's letter:** in his handwriting, to Marcus: *"My brother wrote to me. First time in eleven years. I'm going to answer."*

**Ending:** Marcus at the window with the UNCHECKED list. *Julian?* He looks at the question mark for a long time and doesn't cross it out.

**Final line:** *"The design could anticipate every reformer. It could not anticipate everyone."*

## Themes
- **The cost of silence.** Book 5's wise decision has a body count. The book never says they were wrong, only that it cost.
- **Seduction as method.** R-2 turned outward. Two states ask two lonely men to manufacture each other's trust. The only honest move is to say it aloud where it can't be unsaid.
- **The designed victory, again, and what beats it.** You can't stop them riding your revolution. You can refuse to give it a center.
- **One record against many witnesses,** at national scale. The Concordance is the old single record wearing the new movement's clothes.
- **The designer's guilt.** Vance knew. "Frightened is not the same as wise."
- **Old wisdom:** a round cannot be sung by one voice.
