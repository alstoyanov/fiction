# Fact Storms

**Status:** CURRENT canon, Sept 2026. It is consistent with Story 03, *The Correction* (especially Ch 8, 20, 23, 24 and 26) and *The Lost Hour* (v3.1). It replaces everything that was in `worldbuilding/fact-storms/`.
**The one rule:** a fact storm is **not weather**. It is a **neural event**. The physical world never changes. Only people's **perception** and **memory** do.

---

## 1. What a Fact Storm Is
Every citizen's neural interface receives a constant broadcast of facts about where they are, and experiences those facts as things it simply *knows*. A fact storm happens when an interface receives **two or more contradictory broadcasts about the same place at the same time**. The mind is handed incompatible truths, each of which feels as solid as a memory, and it can't let go of any of them.

> *"Ashford Hospital. Founded 2081."* / *"Ashford Correctional Facility. Founded 2079."* / *"No structure. Agricultural land."* All three are known at once, with the same certainty. (Book 4, Ch 23.)

## 2. What It Feels and Looks Like
- **Onset:** always described as sensation, never as meteorology. It is a sudden *wrongness*, a headache, the feeling of two grounds under one pair of feet. Story 03's "like a pressure change" is a **simile** and must stay one.
- **Knowing:** the core of the experience. A person *knows* several incompatible histories of the place they're standing in.
- **Perception:** buildings "flicker" between their histories (rust-red and abandoned, then chrome and glass; a hospital, then a prison, then a field). A cell seems to disappear and a person is standing in wet grass under the stars. The eyes haven't changed. The brain keeps switching between truths it can't reconcile.
- **Colour and distortion:** Story 03 shows fog "turning strange colours: purple, green, colours that shouldn't exist". Treat these as perceptual, and use them sparingly.
- **Shared:** everyone in range receives the same broadcasts, so everyone sees similar things. That's why it feels objective.
- **Behaviour:** people scream, weep, laugh, freeze, circle, or sit down. Some are drawn to the history they *want* (Hayes weeps for a life in which she was a nurse, Book 4 Ch 23).
- **Physical effects:** migraine, nausea, seizures in the vulnerable, and in the old and frail, **cognitive failure and death** (Sofya Volkov, Story 03).
- **Accidents happen because people act on what they see.** A driver swerves around a building that isn't there, or drives off an embankment onto a road that exists in only one history (Book 4, Ch 26). The ground itself never changes.
- **Fog:** the Lake Erie fog is **real weather**. Storms happen *in* it, and it is not caused by them.

## 3. Who Is Most and Least Affected
| More vulnerable | Less vulnerable |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Minds with **gaps**: memory taken by correction. The storm pours into the holes (Marcus). | The **Volkov triplets**: the bond, calling each other's names in rhythm. They are the only stable thing in a storm, and people follow them. |
| The old, the ill, the chronically exposed ("border brain") | People anchored in **dull, procedural truth**: a fact entry recited flatly. *"The storm could make anything warm. It could not make anything dull."* |
| Strong believers (facts integrated most deeply) | People holding a **physical anchor**: a hand, the floor ("Put your hand on it. The floor is real." Tanaka) |
| — | **The fully emptied.** A mind with nothing left (Samuel) gives the storm nothing to find. The storm "goes through him like light through an empty room." |
| — | People inside **shielded** buildings or vehicles (section 6) |

## 4. The Worst Effect: False Memories That Stay
In a mind with gaps, the storm doesn't just cause flicker. It **fills the gaps** with warm, coherent material assembled from the contradictions and from what the person wants. Afterwards some of this **sets**. It becomes a memory that feels exactly like a real one, and it can't be told apart from inside. This is **storm-induced confabulation** (Tanaka's term).
- **Canon case:** Marcus's "field" with Kira (Book 4, Ch 23 and 28). He'd swear to it, and it never happened.
- **Consequence:** survivors have to **check their memories against other people's** (Marcus's CHECKED / UNCHECKED / HOPES lists). This feeds directly into Book 5's themes.
- **Not the same as seeding:** confabulation is individual and never identical across people. Forty identical false memories are **seeding** (section 8, and Book 5 Twist 1).

## 5. Natural or Manufactured?
### The official account
Storms are an unfortunate natural side effect of towers overlapping at borders. They happen when one nation enters a fact that contradicts the other's, and they therefore **follow the fact logs**. They build slowly as the broadcasts drift into conflict. Border residents learn to feel them coming.

### The truth (Story 03 and Book 4; the design is Principle Five)
- The overlap is **deliberately preserved**. The border zone is a **laboratory**, the "calibration grounds", *"where a population's tolerance for contradiction may be measured, and raised."*
- **Border Security's test programme** (Captain Sokolov) manufactures storms with **vehicle-mounted transmitter units**. A unit is driven into a district and broadcasts a false history of it on the local node's frequencies, on top of the true one.
- **How to recognise them:** manufactured storms **don't follow the fact logs**. They start **on the hour**, arrive at **full strength all at once** ("like a switch being thrown"), return to the same streets, and hit districts where neither factorepo has entered anything new for months. *"Less like a conflict and more like a schedule."* (Isaiah's investigation, Book 4, Ch 8: 206 deaths over nine years.)
- **Purpose:** measurement. According to Reeves, every border storm for fifteen years was a Project Continuity field trial: *"Where do you think we learned what a mind can bear?"* (Book 4, Ch 24.)
- **Victims in canon:**
  - The 2068 warehouse test: 43 dead (Ivan Volkov's notes).
  - **Test Subject 2471, Ivan Volkov:** died about 2090 of a cerebral haemorrhage, "lethal threshold confirmed", about a year after he was corrected.
  - **Sofya Volkov:** died in the hospital storm, Nov 2104.
  - **Mikhail Petrov's wife and daughter:** died in a 2074 storm.
  - The retired schoolteacher in a border town (Isaiah's first lead).

## 6. Protection and Institutions
- **Sirens** warn of storms in the border zone, and there are evacuation protocols.
- **Hospitals** have cognitive trauma wards, including a Border Security wing for burned-out agents.
- **Exposure limits** exist for border workers and are routinely ignored (Dmitri, Story 03).
- **Shielding:** correctional facilities can cut inmates off from outside broadcasts. At Ashford the **isolation comes up automatically** the moment the monitors detect a conflict above threshold. In Book 4 Kovač keeps it down by hand from B-4 by re-entering the override every four seconds. Border Security has **some shielded vehicles**, which are expensive and never enough of them.
- **Telemetry during storms:** interfaces fail erratically, and the Registry logs *cognitive failure: flatline*. This makes a spoofed flatline believable (Book 4's escape).

## 7. The Ashford Storm (Book 4): The First Storm Inside Veridica
- **Date:** night of Aug 1–2, 2106, beginning at 23:47.
- **Method:** Elena's resistance used a stolen Border Security unit on the frequencies of **Ashford's local node** (supplied by Nikolai), with the shielding held down from inside.
- **Content:** three histories of the building (hospital, prison, field).
- **Character:** only partly calibrated because the plan was rushed. It was meant to be one clean twenty-minute surge, and it came in **waves for about ninety minutes**.
- **Consequences:**
  - The lockdown was released.
  - The escapees' telemetry was spoofed to flatline.
  - Marcus's false field memory formed.
  - The pursuit crossed the lakeshore border zone during a real (manufactured) storm, and Hanna Marsh, Pavel Ionescu and Teo were killed.
- **Officially:** an "infrastructure malfunction in the eastern district broadcast node" (Ministry of Historical Integrity).

## 8. Related but Different: Seeding
**Continuity seeding** ("erase, then seed", Phase Two) also travels through the broadcast network. But it is **not a storm**:
- **Nothing flickers.** A person feels at most a faint, momentary wrongness (Marcus at 14:21, Book 5 Ch 3).
- **Nothing contradicts.** A single false memory is written in smoothly, and it is **identical** in everyone in range. That is how it gets caught: forty witnesses with the same wrong detail.
- **Who resists it:** the triplets' bond, anyone outside the node's range, and memory kept in rote or sung material (Old Songs).

The Lost Hour (Nov 15, 2106) combined **erasure** (the record) with **seeding** (the Ministry district node). It was **not** a fact storm.

## 9. Language and Culture
- **"Storm-touched":** someone affected by storms.
- **"Border brain":** chronic storm syndrome.
- **"Always one fact storm away from doubt":** border residents' self-description (Dmitri).
- **Storm survivors** are stigmatised and more likely to be corrected. Their contamination rises with exposure.

## 10. Writing Checklist
- Use no meteorological words (pressure fronts, winds, thunder, seasons) except as clearly marked similes.
- Every "change" is perception or memory. Physical consequences only come from people *acting* on what they perceive.
- Make manufactured storms feel **scheduled**, and let natural ones build.
- Give the reader a reason each time why a particular character is more or less affected.
- Keep seeding and storms distinct.
