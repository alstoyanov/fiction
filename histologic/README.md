# Histologic: A Science Fiction Series

## Overview
**Histologic** is set on a future Earth where history has become an exact science. Every event is recorded as a verified fact in a national **factorepo**. A supercomputer, **The Judge**, settles every dispute from those facts, and a neural interface in every citizen's head broadcasts the nation's history into memory, so that people simply *know* it.

Nations are no longer defined by language or land, but by which version of history they believe. These **histonations** contradict one another, and where their broadcasts overlap at the borders, people's minds come apart in **fact storms**. Anyone who doubts their nation's history is an outlaw, and outlaws are **corrected**.

> *"What if history became an exact science, and believing in your nation's version of history was mandatory?"*

Beneath it all lies a secret. The system was **designed**, including its wars, its weaknesses and even the ways it can be reformed, by the authors of a manuscript that officially never existed.

## Key Concepts
- **Factorepo:** every fact, with its time, place and people. Citizens believe it is immutable. Insiders can edit it through a hidden "true source".
- **The Judge:** decides disputes and computes probabilities about people. Above 60% "contamination" means corrective custody.
- **Neural interface:** installed at about age 8. It receives the broadcasts and reports telemetry. Removing it kills.
- **Fact storms:** contradictory broadcasts that break perception. They are officially natural and in fact manufactured.
- **Correction:** the white room, where a machine finds what you feel most strongly and rubs it away. It is kind, sincere and devastating.
- **The seven nations:** Veridica, Chronos, Memoria, Axiom and Synthesis (Histologic), and the SFW and CNT (non-Histologic).

## Themes
- **Truth vs. freedom:** can absolute truth coexist with human freedom?
- **Record vs. memory:** what happens when the record lies, and when memory can be written?
- **Belief vs. knowledge:** is belief genuine if someone else arranged its conditions?
- **One record vs. many witnesses:** the spine of the series.
- **Reform the system was designed to absorb:** the designed victory.

## Philosophy
- What is the relationship between truth and power?
- Can there be freedom without the freedom to be wrong?
- What do we lose when we eliminate ambiguity?
- Is perfect knowledge worth perfect surveillance?
- What happens when certainty becomes tyranny?

## Inspiration
Orwell's *1984* (control through information), Asimov's *Foundation* (history as a science), *The Matrix* (questioning reality), historical revisionism and propaganda, modern surveillance, and the power of shared narratives to create identity.
