## At a Glance
- **Full name:** Isaiah Okonkwo.
- **Age:** **38** in *The Correction*, **39** in *The Lost Hour* (born c. 2067).
- **Occupation:** investigative journalist for twenty years, with a byline at the *Capital Review* (harbour contracts, school closures). Then Project Continuity subject **C-7 WITNESS**, cell **7-E** (upper level). After Book 5 he is a writer in asylum.
- **Status after Book 4:** the **only Continuity subject recorded as alive.** He has been held in **Secure Care Unit 3** since 04:10 on Aug 2, 2106. Expelled from Veridica on **Nov 15, 2106, at 14:30**.
- **Status after Book 5:** granted asylum by the SFW (Dec 3, 2106), on conditions he understands without being told. By Nov 2107 he travels on a Synthesis passport.
- **Appearance:** very tall. He ducks under a cable run and stands "with his head slightly bowed, like a man in a church". Dark skin, graying at the temples, long hands usually folded on one knee. He walks into any room "as if he were arriving at a press conference". His coat, the one he was arrested in, has his notes sewn into its lining from Book 4's Epilogue on.

## Who He Is
A reporter who was arrested three days before publishing proof that the border storms follow a schedule. Inside Wing C he becomes the person who turns seven prisoners into a question ("If I am seven, someone is one"), keeps the number 2471 safe for three strangers, and then chooses to stay behind so that the story comes out as testimony rather than a fugitive's forgery. His defence is a memory palace; his weapon is the record itself, used exactly. He believes in witnessing, and he is the first to see that witnessing can be made into a weapon too: in Book 5 he is the one uneasy voice about the Doctrine everyone else is celebrating.

### The Memory Palace
Nine rooms. The **parlour** holds harmless, true, warm things (his aunt's table, his career, pepper soup). He feeds it to the machine; his session header reads *"SALIENCE MAP: STABLE (PARLOUR?)"*. The **kitchen** holds sources; the **study** his notes, filed by year; the **bedrooms** the people he interviewed, "their words laid out on the pillows". The **cellar** is under the kitchen, reached by narrow stairs "his aunt had always told him not to use", behind a door painted the exact dull cream of the kitchen wall. The dangerous things live there. Before Unit 3 he moves everything he loves into the cellar and leaves the rooms above bare. In Unit 3 he loses one thing: **the colour of the cellar door**. The door stays; its colour is gone.

## Voice Rules
1. **He argues like an editorial.** Reasons in order, every unnecessary word struck out. "If six of us escape and the evidence leaves with us, Veridica will call it forgery… If the evidence is published from *inside*… it is a witness account." "It was arithmetic, in the end. Most hard things are."
2. **He classifies and counts.** A taxonomy of any building: believers, jobholders, the disturbed, and the one he can't classify. A census of Wing C. The storm dictated aloud with timestamps: "Twenty-three fifty-one. Atrium, Wing C. Lockdown released… I count eleven."
3. **He hears belief in how people say words.** "When Moss said *healing*, she said it the way people say *God*… When Reeves said *Veridica*, he said it the way people say *the building I work in*."
4. **He never claims more than he knows.** "I do not know which districts. I would like someone to find out." "I don't know. Somebody should find out." His published pieces end on a question he admits he cannot answer.
5. **Courteous formality.** "Director," with an inclination of the head. "Mr. Chen." A measured voice. He uses contractions in ordinary speech, but never hurries.
6. **Dry understatement.** "They'll find a very nice parlour." "If I were a guessing man. Which, professionally, I am not."
7. **He files.** Every coded message carries a prefix, a sequence number and a date (*IO-19. 5 APRIL.*). Nothing is lost or read out of order.
8. **Physical restraint.** He folds his hands. He rarely touches anyone. The first time he touches one of the seven is his hand on Marcus's shoulder in Book 4 Ch 22.
9. **No borrowed wisdom.** He never quotes real-world maxims about journalism or history. His lines are his own and specific.

## Continuity Traps
- **He does not escape in Book 4.** He stays by choice, sends the archive, and is moved to maximum security.
- **He is never "officially dead".** He is the one subject the Registry counts as alive. His article says so deadpan.
- **Expelled into Chronos**, at the lakeshore checkpoint, at 14:30 on Nov 15, 2106, under Sokolov's escort. **Veridica and the SFW share no border.** He claims asylum at the **SFW mission in Chronos**.
- **The article's title** is "Continuity: A Witness Account from Wing C". Released Aug 5 via the Synthesis Institute for Historical Methods.
- **He found R-2 himself** while sending the archive, and withheld the names. It is the only time he edits the truth.
- **Notes in the coat** are newsprint margins written with a burned match. He never had paper in Unit 3.
- **Contamination 81%.** Arrived Oct 27, 2105.
- **Page thirty-one.** His procurement leak's key page is page 31, and so is Singh's torn page in Book 5. Either make the echo deliberate, and let him notice it, or renumber one.
- **The rule of three (settled, Sept 2026):** "One fragment is a curiosity. Two is a pattern. I'll wait for three." Two is already a pattern; he won't publish without a third. The plot file now matches.
- **No notebook in Books 4–5.** The palace replaced it. In Book 5 Ch 5 he is still writing in newspaper margins with the burned match he kept from Unit 3.
- **No real awards.**
