# Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov: The Rememberer

**Status:** CURRENT, Sept 2026. Rewritten to match the new *The Correction* and *The Lost Hour* (v3.1). Later books will be rewritten to fit this file.
**Related:** `plots/00-FOUNDATION-MANUSCRIPT.md` (the manuscript he remembers) and Book 4's `chapters-summaries/README.md`.

---

## At a Glance
- **Full name:** Mikhail "Misha" Petrov. Everyone, including the staff at Ashford, calls him "Old Songs".
- **Born:** March 2038. He is **68** in *The Correction* (Oct 2105 – Aug 2106) and in *The Lost Hour* (Nov 2106 – Jan 2107), and turns **69** before Book 5's epilogue (Nov 2107).
- **Former occupation:** junior archivist and folk-music collector at the capital's Historical Archive (2060s), then a teacher of pre-Histologic history and music.
- **Status after Book 4:** officially **deceased** in the Correction Registry, like the other escapees. In Book 5 he declines to be legally brought back to life.
- **Appearance:** thin and slightly stooped, with a long white ponytail, a full white beard and pale blue eyes that look a little beyond whatever is in front of them. His hands are gnarled from decades of instruments and dishwater. At Ashford he wore cell gray with a canvas apron. After the escape he wears whatever the resistance hands him.
- **Not related to** Dr. Yuri Petrov (an Architect in later books). Petrov is a common name. Say so on the page if the two ever meet.

## Who He Is
Old Songs is the only person alive who remembers the world before the record, and he has kept the memory the only way the record can't reach: in songs. He has been corrected many times over forty years and was never emptied, because what he loves is kept in rote, rhythmic material, and the machines skim over that. He lets everyone think he is harmless furniture. *"Furniture remembers everything that happened in the room."*

He is not a mystic and not a fool. He is a very old, very tired, very sharp archivist who decided long ago that the safest place for the truth is a tune an orderly would never think to question.

## Voice Rules
These rules apply to every book.

1. **He speaks rarely.** In Wing C he speaks only a handful of sentences in the whole book. Every sentence lands. He never makes speeches, except once per book, when it matters most (Book 5, Ch 30).
2. **He hums instead of talking.** Humming is his language: greeting, warning, comfort, refusal. The characters, and the reader, learn to read it.
3. **Words in a song are a sign of freedom.** In captivity he *hums*. He sings with words for the first time when the dead need naming (Book 4, Ch 26). After that he sings every evening. If he is ever captured again, he goes back to humming, and the reader should feel it.
4. **His songs end on a question.** Every tune he favours lifts at the end of the phrase "like a question". It is his signature, and the book's prose should describe it that way consistently.
5. **Dry wit, never whimsy.** *"They erased an hour. We used to erase centuries. At least they're getting more efficient."*
6. **No quoting of modern, copyrighted songs.** Everything he sings is either an **original song** from the songbook below or a **public-domain** piece (section 5). His "pre-Histologic" repertoire is folk, hymn and lullaby from long before the Fracture Years.

## The Songbook
All lyrics here are **original to the series** unless marked as public domain. When a song appears in a novel, quote only the lines the scene needs.

### 1. The Lakeshore Air
*A traditional border-zone folk tune, pre-Histologic. Wordless in Wing C. Every phrase lifts at the end like a question.*

**One tune, three sets of words.** As with many old folk airs, different words have been sung to it over the years: the **ferry verses** below (the grandmothers' version), the **lullaby** "Hush Now, the River Is Long" (song 2), and Mikhail's secret setting of the Journal passage, **"Given Gently"** (song 4). This is why, in Book 4, the same hummed tune is the Volkovs' mother's song (Ch 6) and brings back Marcus's mother's lullaby (Ch 11). And in Book 5 it turns out to have carried the manuscript the whole time (Ch 12).
- **Where:** the humming in Book 4 (Ch 1, 3, 4, 6, 11 and 14, plus the yes and no phrases). The tune the Volkovs' mother hummed. In Book 5 Ch 12 it turns out to carry the Journal passage.
- **The traditional words,** which grandmothers of the lakeshore sang:
  > *Down by the water the ferry is late,*
  > *lanterns are low on the pier;*
  > *who will remember the name of the boat*
  > *when there is no one left here?*
  >
  > *Down by the water the fog's coming in,*
  > *nobody sees the far shore;*
  > *who will remember the boat and the name*
  > *when there's no pier any more?*
- **The signals** (Book 4, Ch 14): **yes** is the rising third phrase ("the name of the boat"). **No** is the falling last phrase, sung downward. **Silence** means they're watching.

### 2. Hush Now, the River Is Long
*A pre-Histologic lullaby from the Great Lakes, sung **to the Lakeshore Air** by mothers who weren't supposed to know it. Marcus's mother sang it to him when he had a fever.*
- **Where:** Book 4, Ch 11 (hearing the Lakeshore Air hummed at the garden gate, Marcus gets back the first line) and Ch 28 (sung in full: Old Songs verse 1, Marcus verse 2, Old Songs verse 3). A possible Book 9 reprise.
  > *Hush now, the river is long,*
  > *longer than night and longer than song;*
  > *sleep till the morning comes round the bend:*
  > *the river is long, but it comes to an end.*
  >
  > *Hush now, the boatman is kind,*
  > *he carries the ones that we leave behind;*
  > *nothing is lost that the water has kept:*
  > *the river remembers the ones who have slept.*
  >
  > *Hush now, and if you should wake,*
  > *count all the stars for the river's sake:*
  > *one for the lost and one for the found,*
  > *and one for the river that carries us round.*
- **Hidden link (Book 4 Twist 2):** in Marcus's false "field" memory, Kira is *counting the stars*. Verse 3 may have been buried in him since childhood, and the storm may have built the memory from it. Never state this outright. Tanaka's "remembering or making it up" (Ch 28) covers it.

### 3. The Names
*A lament from the Fracture Years, when too many died for the records to keep. The singer puts the names of the dead into the third line, one verse per name. It has no fixed length. It is as long as the dead.*
- **Where:** Book 4, Ch 26 (Teo, Hanna Marsh, Pavel Ionescu, Samuel, the old man at the fence). Book 8, where it grows through the war. He also sings it silently every night for Anya, Katya and Kerr.
  > *Who went down to the water?*
  > *Name them, and they stay.*
  > *(name) went down to the water —*
  > *name them, and they stay.*
  >
  > *The record may forget them,*
  > *the song will not forget;*
  > *name them, and they stay.*

### 4. Given Gently
*Words from the* Journal of Post-Democratic Governance *(around 2063, the essay behind Principle Six, author Chen Zhao), set by Mikhail in 2063 to the Lakeshore Air. He chose the tune every mother in the border zone knew, so that the words would hide inside something no one would ever question.*
- **Where:** Book 5, Ch 12 (first sung aloud), Ch 30 (dictated to Kira) and the Epilogue (sung at the memorial). In Book 9 it is identified as Chen Zhao's essay.
  > *A people that has lost part of its past*
  > *will accept any past it is given —*
  > *provided it is given gently,*
  > *and by someone it trusts.*
- **Irony:** in 2063 he set it to the gentlest tune he knew, so that he would never forget how dangerous it was.

### 5. The Town That Stopped Its Clocks
*A comic, bitter ballad from pre-Histologic times about a town that lost an hour and argued forever about what happened in it. Each verse is a different witness, and each one is sure.*
- **Where:** Book 5, at the safe house after the forty witnesses (around Ch 13 or 19), and as the Epilogue closes.
  > *The baker swore the bell had rung,*
  > *the priest swore it was still;*
  > *the mayor swore he'd climbed the tower,*
  > *the tower swore he never will —*
  > *and every one of them was sure,*
  > *and every one was sworn;*
  > *and nobody could tell the hour*
  > *the day the clocks were gone.*

### 6. Many Hands (planned, Book 6)
*His first new composition in forty years: a **round**. One voice starts, a second enters, then a third. It cannot be sung alone. The form is the meaning.*
  > *One hand writes and one hand checks,*
  > *one hand holds the light;*
  > *one voice fails and one voice mends,*
  > *and many make it right.*

### 7. The Two Maps (planned, Book 7)
*A song about two nations drawing the same river in different places. He writes it in October 2109, after learning that the 2074 storm was scheduled (Book 7, Ch 13), and sings it whole for the first time to Marcus (Book 7, Ch 26). The last verse lifts like a question.*
  > *Two maps of the one river,*
  > *one for either shore;*
  > *one puts the ford by the willows,*
  > *one by the mill-house door.*
  >
  > *They fought for the ford forty winters,*
  > *and neither would give an inch;*
  > *the river ran on between them*
  > *and never once asked which.*
  >
  > *But who drew the maps for the fighting,*
  > *and sold them the ink and the quill —*
  > *and why is the hand in the corner*
  > *the same on both maps still?*

### 8. The Lakeshore Air, complete (planned, Book 9)
Its old verses and his hidden verses sung together, once, at the end.

## Riddles and Sayings
Use these rarely: one per book at most.
- *"What cage has no bars? What prison has no walls? What slavery calls itself freedom?"* (a system that controls through truth).
- *"What is lost when it's remembered, and found when it's forgotten?"* (a song's words, which you only notice once you stop trying to recall them).
- *"Twelve shepherds tend the flock. Who tends the shepherds?"* (hold for Book 8 or 9, the Architects).
- *"They built a machine to know everything. Wisdom isn't knowing everything. It's knowing what to keep."*
- *"A song dies with the singer, unless it has many singers."* (the thesis of his arc).

## Symbolism
- **The living record:** memory without a system. It is proof that truth can survive outside the factorepo, and a warning that one person's memory is not enough.
- **Songs as vaults:** rote material that the machines skim over. His survival explains the triplets' survival, and gives the Book 5 technique its human origin.
- **Many singers:** his arc runs from a song kept by one man to a round sung by thousands. That is the series' movement from a single record to distributed truth.
