# The Judge (and The Counselor): The Machine That Decides

**Status:** CURRENT, Sept 2026. Rewritten to match `basic-idea.txt`, the three short stories, the drafted *The Correction* and the *The Lost Hour* chapter summaries. The Judge's destruction and The Counselor are **provisional** (Books 8–9). Later books will be rewritten to fit this file.

## At a Glance
- **What it is:** a supercomputer with access to the whole factorepo of one histonation. It evaluates facts to settle disputes, derives new facts by deduction, and computes **probabilities about people**. Its determinations are final, and they become facts themselves.
- **One per nation.** *"Each Histonation has its own Judge that has access to its factorepo."* (`basic-idea.txt`.) "The Judge" in Books 1–5 always means **Veridica's** Judge.
- **International Judges** exist, with access only to nations' primary repositories. They "do not hold as much power and respect", and their rulings are rarely accepted, because the factorepos of different nations contradict each other.
- **Activated:** **early 2066.** Veridica, the first histonation, was founded with it.
- **Designed by:** the authors of *The Foundation of Certainty*. **Principle One** (HV·01) is its reason for existing. The **Annex** (MT·06, YP·02, VS·09) specifies its architecture, including **deliberate weaknesses**. The Compiler, Elise Vance, secretly built in **Principle Seven**, the means of its own ending.
- **Status after Book 5:** intact and more powerful than ever. It has accepted the **Lost Hour Doctrine** (ruling of Mon Dec 27, 2106), and for the first time a fact can be entered on human testimony alone.
- **Physical form:** not specified in Books 1–5. The Justice Center holds its hearing chamber; the factorepo's primary nodes are in the Factorepo Spire. Clean and clinical. No brass, no dials, no gears.

## What It Is
The Judge is the machine that makes Veridica's truth into law. It is not malicious and it is not, in Books 1–5, a character with a voice. It is honest by its own lights: it computed Marcus's contamination honestly at 58%, and someone else typed the 73. That is the horror of it. It cannot see intention, loyalty or love; it cannot see what isn't recorded; and it cannot tell whether what it has been given was altered upstream at the "true source". Its certainty is only as good as its record, and the record can be written.

## How It Works (Canon)

### Probabilities, and the 60% threshold
- Ordinary citizens meet the Judge as a number. It calculates a **contamination probability** from a person's exposure: time with outlaws, visits to unmonitored spaces, fact-storm exposure.
- **In Veridica, anything over 60% requires corrective intervention** (Story 01, Kade: *"In Veridica, anything over sixty percent requires corrective intervention."*).
- **Canon figures:**
  - Marcus: **73%** (Story 01). The Judge's real figure was **0.58** (Book 4, Ch 16 and 27). Someone typed a seventy-three.
  - Kira: **92%** (Story 02).
  - Marcus's Book 5 tribunal: the Judge's "contamination analysis of the subject's persistent false beliefs", **88%** (Book 5, Ch 18).
- It reasons in formal disjunctions when facts are missing: *"The Judge has determined two possibilities. Either you never entered the fact and falsified your work reports, or you entered it and then deleted it, which constitutes sabotage."* (Story 02.)
- **It is not infallible, and it doesn't claim to be.** It works in likelihoods. Citizens and officials treat the numbers as certainties ("The Judge cannot be wrong. The facts are the facts.", Kade, Story 01). Keep that gap visible.

### What it cannot do
- It cannot weigh intentions, belief or love. *"Your belief isn't measurable, Mr. Chen. Your exposure is."* (Story 01.) *"Love is not measurable, Ms. Osman. Your actions are."* (Story 02.)
- It cannot see what isn't recorded. *"No record means no fact. No fact means The Judge can't rule."* (Wei, Book 5, Ch 1.)
- It cannot detect tampering at the "true source" (Principle Three): a deleted fact, an adjusted index or an erased hour leaves no contradiction for it to find.
- It cannot resolve contradictions *between* nations. Veridica's and Chronos's Judges refuse to communicate (the Temporal War, `nations.md`).
- *"The Judge only knows what it's told. It's a computer, not a god."* (Alexei, Story 03.)

### No appeal, until the Lost Hour Doctrine
- Before Book 5 there is **no appeal** against a determination (`PROJECT-RULES.md` §4).
- **The Lost Hour Doctrine** (Book 5, Ch 25, ruling Mon Dec 27, 2106): *"Where the record is absent, facts may be established by the concordant testimony of multiple independent witnesses, weighted for exposure to known contamination."*
- It is the **first route by which people can stand against, or in for, the record**: the nearest thing to an appeal the system has ever had. Its first use is on the dead themselves: *CHEN, MARCUS. STATUS: LIVING.* (Ch 26), "the first fact in Veridica's history entered on human testimony alone."
- **The trap in it:** testimony can be seeded. "Whoever can write memory can now write law." (Ch 29.) The Judge's own weighting "for exposure to known contamination" is the probability method turned on witnesses, and it can only weigh contamination it *knows* about.

## Voice Rules
1. **In Books 1–5 the Judge has no voice and no POV.** It speaks only through **determinations**, read aloud or printed by officials: Kade, the enforcer in Story 02, the tribunal, the hearing's ruling. Never give it dialogue.
2. **Its language is formal, probabilistic and exact.** Percentages, thresholds, "has determined", "two possibilities", "concordant", "weighted for exposure". No adjectives, no warmth, no menace.
3. **People speak for it.** "The Judge has determined otherwise." "The Judge's determination is final." The humans who quote it supply the certainty it never claims.
4. **Show its limits in every book** (`PROJECT-RULES.md` §8): what it can't see (intention, love, the unrecorded, the tampered).
5. **It is honest.** The Judge never lies in Books 1–5. The lies are in its inputs.
6. **Not weather, not magic, not steampunk.** It is a clean, silent, distributed system. No glowing eyes, no brass, no voice from the ceiling.

### Books 6–9 (provisional; to be rewritten)
- **Book 6, *The Distributed Truth*:** the Judge is still Veridica's authority, but the distributed networks and the Commission's published cross-checks begin to route around it. Principle Three (backup control) is revealed.
- **Book 7, *Battle of Truths*:** the fact war shows it being fed contradictions deliberately. Principle One, its reason for existing, is revealed.
- **Book 8, *Battle of Blood* (~2110): its destruction through Principle Seven.**
  - The SFW invades to **capture** the Judge, not to conquer Veridica.
  - Facing capture, the Judge is destroyed through **Principle Seven**, Vance's handwritten contingency: *"The Judge must never become a weapon. Better destroyed than enslaved. I have built into it the means of its own ending: conditions, authorities and a cascade that cannot be reversed. I have told no one."* The twelve never knew.
  - **Reconciling the old "martyr" idea:** the Judge may **choose** to trigger Seven. The cleanest version keeps it a probability machine to the end: the contingency's conditions are a threshold, and the Judge's last act is a determination that the threshold has been crossed. Whether that is a choice, or only looks like one, is the question the book can leave open, the way Book 4 left open whether Reeves made Marcus love Kira.
  - **Veridica wins the war and loses its certainty.**

### Provisional: The Counselor (Books 8–9)
SERIES-OVERVIEW §4 (Book 8 aftermath): "a partnership between humans and AI begins (possibly 'The Counselor': an adviser, not a judge)." Foundation §9: "The Counselor as Vance's redemption (Book 9): not decided here." Everything below is an idea, not canon.
- **The concept:** an advisory system, not an authority. It calculates possibilities; people decide. Transparent reasoning, no hidden "true source", no binding determinations, no control over any factorepo.
- **Its line:** *"I advise. You decide."* (old file).
- **Fit with the series' answer:** the heroes' answer to the Judge is **many hands**: journals, independent witnesses checking each other, records kept by people. A single new machine risks becoming the Judge again. If The Counselor is used, make it serve the many hands (it checks, it never decides), or make it many small counsellors rather than one.
- **Who builds it:** the old file said Kira and Nikolai. Plausible (the Architect and the technician). Another option is Vance's redemption (Foundation §9). Decide in the Book 8–9 rewrite.
- **Old lines, usable if it exists:** *"The Judge knew everything. I know enough."* *"I calculate possibilities. You choose probabilities. That's not weakness. That's partnership."* *"I can show you facts. You must find meaning."* *"The Judge decided for you. I decide with you."*

## Continuity Traps
- **Each nation has its own Judge.** It is never "the arbiter for all Histologic nations", and it never controls other nations' factorepos.
- **Activated early 2066.** Not "post-World War III", not "about fifty years old" (it is 40 in 2106).
- **It works in probabilities.** Over 60% means custody in Veridica. Other nations may have other thresholds; none are stated.
- **The 73 was typed, not computed.** The Judge's figure was 58.
- **No appeal before the Doctrine.** The Doctrine is a ruling on testimony where the record is absent, not a general right of appeal.
- **Doctrine date:** the ruling is **Mon Dec 27, 2106** (Book 5 summaries, Ch 25). `worldbuilding/core-system.md` §5 says "Jan 2107"; that should be corrected to Dec 2106 (the Commission, appointed Jan 8, 2107, is the January event).
- **No voice, no POV, no consciousness in Books 1–5.**
- **Principle Seven** is Vance's, handwritten, in her copy only. The Architects never knew. Nobody learns of it before Book 8.
