## At a Glance
- **Full name:** Dr. Elise Vance.
- **Born:** **2036.** She is 27 in 2063, **28** in winter 2064 when she begins compiling, 30 in March 2066, **70** in 2106, **72** in 2108 (Book 6), 79 in 2115 (Book 9).
- **Occupation (2060s):** a systems theorist. In Old Songs' memory she is "a brilliant young woman from the systems department" who reads at the capital's Historical Archive every Thursday (Book 5 Ch 12).
- **Her role:** the **Compiler** of *The Foundation of Certainty* (2064–66). She assembled the principles of seventeen contributors into one design, wrote the **Compiler's Introduction**, and in March 2066 added two handwritten pages to her own copy only: the **Compiler's Warning** and **Principle Seven**.
- **Not an Architect.** She **did not sign the Register**. Her copy has **no copy numeral**. The old "DV-12, the thirteenth architect" is retired.
- **Status after Book 5:** unknown to every character. Whether she is alive in Book 6 is an open decision (§10 below). In Book 5 she exists only as an unnamed memory in Old Songs' head.
- **Appearance:** not yet established. Suggested (for Book 6): small and very upright; a precise, slanting hand, recognisable from the handwritten pages; in old age, a slight tremor in the hands that she controls by holding things. This mirrors the manuscript's description of her prose, "precise with a hidden tremor". No ornament; clinical, plain clothes.

## Who She Is
A brilliant, frightened young woman who lived through the Fracture Years and believed a species that couldn't agree on what happened had to be governed through its record. She assembled the most dangerous document in the world because she was afraid, and within two years she knew she had been wrong. She could not unmake it, so she did two secret things: she wrote a warning to whoever would find her copy, and she built into The Judge the means of its own ending, and told no one. Then she went quiet. When she returns in Book 6 she is the only person alive who both built the prison and tried, alone and in secret, to leave it a door.

### Her three texts (canonical wording; quote only the lines a scene needs)
- **The Introduction (winter 2064):** *"This document assembles principles from seventeen contributors across political theory, systems architecture, social psychology, historical methodology and information security. Our goal is a post-democratic order that prevents the chaos of the Fracture Years while preserving the appearance of participation. Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided by a public. It must be decided for one, and the public must be allowed to believe it decided."*
- **The Warning (handwritten, March 2066):** *"To whoever finds this: I assembled these principles because I was afraid. I was wrong. The system contains the seeds of its own corruption: Three enables the war of facts and Five encourages it. Reformers will try to fix it. They will fail, because the design anticipates them: it will make whistleblowers into criminals and reformers into its own restorers. There is no reform path, only replacement. Distribute truth. Distribute verification. Accept uncertainty. Build by many hands."*
- **Principle Seven (handwritten, unlisted):** *"The Judge must never become a weapon. Better destroyed than enslaved. I have built into it the means of its own ending: conditions, authorities and a cascade that cannot be reversed. I have told no one. If you are reading this, you are the one I did not tell."*

## Voice Rules
1. **Precise, with a hidden tremor** (Manuscript §8). Short declarative sentences. No rhetoric. The fear shows only in what she admits, and how briefly: "I was afraid. I was wrong."
2. **The Introduction voice and the Warning voice are the same woman two years apart.** The Introduction is calm and certain ("Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided by a public"). The Warning uses the same syntax to take it back. On the page in Book 6 she should sound like the Warning, never like a penitent.
3. **She loves exact language.** In 2063 she argued with Mikhail about poetry. Let her correct a word in someone else's sentence, gently, the way she once argued at the Archive.
4. **No self-pity, no speeches.** She confesses the way she compiled: in order, completely, without asking to be forgiven.
5. **She keeps secrets alone.** "I have told no one." Her instinct is still to carry things by herself; the heroes' lesson ("many hands") is one she wrote and never practised. Book 6 can make that her arc.
6. **With Mikhail she is 27 again for one line**, and then she isn't.

None. She is never named, and nothing points to her. (Continuity's aim in Book 4 echoes Principle Four, which is Wei Zhang's, not hers.)

### Book 5: *The Lost Hour* (drafted)
- **Ch 12, "The Singer" (Old Songs' POV):** his memory of 2063 includes "a brilliant young woman from the systems department" who reads in the Archive every Thursday and argues with him about poetry. **He doesn't name her.** This is her only presence in the book.

### Books 6–9 (provisional; to be rewritten)
- **Book 6, *The Distributed Truth* (~Nov 2108 – Feb 2109; she is 72):**
  - Old Songs finds the **Compiler's Introduction** and recognises the voice of the young woman from the Archive. He names her, forty-five years late.
  - **Vance is revealed as the Compiler** and confesses. The old plot's framing ("I tried to save humanity and built its cage"; a long written confession) can stay, but the old "final journal (March 2086)" date is wrong: her handwritten pages are **March 2066**.
  - The **Warning** is revealed, and it catalyses Marcus's choice of transparency. Principles Three and Five are revealed in the same book; Page 30 is identified as Principle Six.
  - **The reunion with Old Songs** is the emotional core of both arcs: "two old people, one of whom built the prison and one who survived it." Suggested shape: he doesn't forgive her and doesn't condemn her; he asks her to write it down. She is the first person he teaches his own lesson to.
  - She should **not** mention Principle Seven. It stays hidden until Book 8.
- **Book 7, *Battle of Truths* (2109):** the old plot has failed assassination attempts on Marcus, Mikhail and Vance. Usable. The title page and table of contents are revealed; the contents do **not** list Seven, and she says nothing when people notice there are only six.
- **Book 8, *Battle of Blood* (2110):** when the SFW moves to capture The Judge, **Principle Seven** is found in her copy and The Judge is destroyed by it. That is her act, forty-four years late. Decide whether she is alive to see it, and who "the one I did not tell" turns out to be.
- **Book 9, *Architects of Chaos* (~2115):** the **Annex** and the **Register** are recovered **from her copy**. The old idea that the Counselor (an advisory AI) is her redemption is **not decided** (Manuscript §9).

## Open Questions (from Manuscript §10, plus drafting questions)
**From the Manuscript's own list:**
1. **Where her complete copy is kept, and how it is found in Book 9.**
2. **Whether she is alive in Book 6.** She would be 72 in 2108. The old plan confessed her in 2108. (If she has died, the confession has to be written, and the reunion with Old Songs becomes a reading, not a meeting.)
3. **The fates of the five struck-through contributors.** One is Anselm Kerr, Mikhail's mentor. What she knew about his correction is a possible Book 6 or 7 beat.
4. Linked open names: the marks ·05 and ·12 are still unnamed.

**Drafting questions this file raises:**
5. **Settled (Sept 2026): she wrote none of the Journal essays.** The Manuscript is authoritative: the essays were by "the future contributors", and she is not one. She read them at the Archive as they came out, and from winter 2064 compiled them into a design. Her own words appear only in the Introduction, the Warning and Principle Seven. `old-songs-character.md` has been corrected to match.
6. **Did she know Mikhail was keeping the essays in songs?** If she did, and let him, that is a kind of Warning too.
7. **How did she build Seven into The Judge** when its architecture was Dr. Yuri Petrov's (YP·02, Principle Three and the Annex)? She must have had access during activation in early 2066. Decide what "conditions, authorities and a cascade" means technically.
8. **Why didn't she sign?** Fear, disgust, or a deliberate decision to stay outside the Register so that one copy would be unaccounted for.
9. **Where was she from 2067 to 2108?** Watched, corrected, protected, or simply forgotten?

## Continuity Traps
- **Born 2036.** Not 62 in Book 6, not 81 in Book 5. She is 70 in 2106 and 72 in 2108.
- **Not a signatory, not an Architect, not "DV-12".** Her copy has no numeral.
- **Handwritten:** the Warning and Seven, in her copy only. **Typed:** everything else, on the machine with the chipped *e*. Seven is inserted after p. 32 and **not listed** in the table of contents.
- **Nobody names her before Book 6.** Old Songs does not name her in Book 5 Ch 12.
- **The reveal schedule is binding:** Introduction and Warning in Book 6; Seven in Book 8; Annex and Register (from her copy) in Book 9.
- Her dates are **2064–66**, not the 2080s. The Warning is dated **March 2066**.
- She and Mikhail knew each other at the **capital's Historical Archive**, "young in the same city".
- The Judge's end in Book 8 is **Principle Seven**. No character blows it up.
