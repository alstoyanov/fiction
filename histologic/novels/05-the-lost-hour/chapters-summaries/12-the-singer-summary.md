# Chapter 12: The Singer

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Wed Nov 24, 2106
**Setting:** The resistance safe house in the border zone; memory of the Historical Archive in 2063
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The manuscript thread opens. An old man who is dead to the record turns out to be the only living copy of a text the record erased forty years ago. Write it as a quiet, eerie, beautiful chapter.

## Beats
- **Old Songs at the stove,** humming the Lakeshore Air. Kira has come to the safe house with everything she has: the forty statements, the diary page, and **Singh's photograph of the torn page**.
- He reads it over her shoulder. **He stops humming.** The whole room notices, because the silence is the Wing C signal for "they're watching".
- **His memory of 2063:** he is 25, a junior archivist under his mentor **Anselm Kerr**. There are three issues of a small quarterly, the *Journal of Post-Democratic Governance*, with anonymous essays about governing through the record. A brilliant young woman from the systems department reads in the archive every Thursday and argues with him about poetry. (He doesn't name her. It's **Elise Vance**, a Book 6 seed.) He catalogues the issues, and they frighten him.
- **2066–67:** the journal is classified and every copy vanishes. Kerr asks questions, is corrected, and never comes back as himself. Mikhail understands, and does what he has always done with things he can't bear to lose: he sets them to tunes.
- **The song:** he sings a passage now, cracked and very quiet, to the **Lakeshore Air**, the tune he hummed through every meal slot in Wing C:
  > *"A people that has lost part of its past will accept any past it is given, provided it is given gently, and by someone it trusts."*
- Kira realises the tune she heard three times a day for eight months **had words all along**.
- **The same voice:** Old Songs puts his finger on the torn page. "The same hand wrote this. Forty years apart." Kira checks the factorepo from a resistance terminal: **there is no record of the journal anywhere**, not even a catalogue number. Officially it never existed.
- **The point:** whoever erased the hour may be following a design written before the Founding.
- **Inside Old Songs' head:** why he survived forty years of correction. A song is rote material, and the machine skims it as furniture.

## Ending
He says the line that gives the chapter its weight: *"They think I'm furniture. Furniture remembers everything that happened in the room."* Then, looking at the photograph, he goes still again, because he has noticed the small numeral in the corner, ***XI***. "Where did she get this?" Kira: "Continuity's papers." Old Songs: "Then Continuity was quoting scripture."
