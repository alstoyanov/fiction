# Chapter 30: The Dilemma

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Tue Jan 11 – Wed Jan 12, 2107
**Setting:** The back room of the resistance safe house; the stove; Isaiah by link
**Length:** ~3,500 words

## Purpose
The moral climax. There's no clean win, and a choice to be made. The oldest witness gives the one speech of his book, and the series finds its answer: many hands.

## Beats
- **All seven Continuity subjects** are present, with Isaiah by link, plus Elena, Kovač and Maya. Old Songs sits by the stove and watches them argue. He sees faces, and is silent until the end.
- **The case for exposing Wei (Alexei, with fire):** he murdered or staged a murder, seeded forty people and framed a man. "We tell the world tonight."
- **What exposing Wei would cost (Kira, precise):**
  - It means proving **testimony can be seeded**. That voids the Doctrine and **makes them all legally dead again**.
  - It **frees Reeves**, who is guilty of Continuity but not of this.
  - It hands **Pell** the argument to abolish testimony and go back to the pure record, which Wei's people also control (Principle Three).
  - And their proof is thin: a signature only Kira can read, an author cache that can be called forged, one young woman's memory of a time, a phrase from Story 01, a torn page and **an old man's song**.
- **Dmitri** wants to wait and build the case. **Isaiah:** "Two fragments are a pattern. We don't have the third." **Kovač:** "They'll say I forged it to save my career." **Nikolai:** "I've kept a copy of everything. As always." **Maya** is silent. Her father is in prison for a murder he didn't commit, and justice for him is what would end it all.
- **Old Songs speaks, at last and at length:**
  - 2063. Anselm Kerr, the journal on his desk, the week the archive was sealed, the colleagues who asked questions and came back empty.
  - Forty years of songs, so that *something* would remain.
  - *"I kept it in my head for forty years, and it was worth nothing. A song dies with the singer. They let me keep it because one old man's memory is furniture."*
  - *"Don't be me. Write it down. All of it. Many hands. Many copies. Things that don't need one record to survive, and can't be written by one pen."*
- **The decision:** stay silent **for now**. Keep the proof (Nikolai keeps it). And begin building what can't be seeded: **written journals**, many independent witnesses checking each other, records kept by people rather than by one system. Marcus: *"He designed the reformers he wanted. Then we reform something he didn't design."*
- **Maya's choice:** she agrees to wait, for now, and it costs her. Nikolai stays with her.
- **The dictation:** Old Songs asks for paper. He dictates the Journal passage to Kira word for word, for the first time in forty-three years. She writes it down. *Now it exists in two places.* Then he dictates the Lakeshore Air's ferry verse, "so the tune has somewhere to live too".

## Ending
Late at night, alone at the stove, Old Songs hums the Lakeshore Air, and for the first time in the book the hum is joined by a second voice: Kira, off-key, from the next room, trying out the words she has just written down.

## Notes
- This is Old Songs' "one speech per book" (voice rule 1).
- The **written journals** are the bridge to Book 6's distributed truth.
