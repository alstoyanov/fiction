# Chapter 24: I Remember Doing It

**POV:** Marcus Chen
**Date:** Mon Dec 20, 2106
**Setting:** The Ministry medical wing's interview room; the safe flat
**Length:** ~3,300 words

## Purpose
The most chilling scene in the book: the man who invented seeding sincerely confesses to a murder he didn't commit. Marcus is the only person who understands.

## Beats
- **Marcus is brought in** as the "material witness" to observe the statement: Wei's idea, "so that you can hear it and be free of it." Kira isn't allowed in.
- **Reeves's confession,** calm and detailed and utterly sincere:
  - "I went up at 14:20. The side entrance. I had a staff card from my Ministry days."
  - "She was at her desk. She looked up. I remember her face."
  - "I remember the letter opener. I remember that it was colder than I expected."
  - He describes Singh's office exactly, including the cut flowers Marcus smelled in the corridor (Ch 3), which were in the case file.
- **Everyone is relieved.** Pell is triumphant. Kovač is quietly glad. Wei is grave, and satisfied that justice is done.
- **Marcus watches Reeves's eyes.** He knows those eyes. He looked up into them from a white chair for eight months. Reeves isn't lying. **He believes every word.**
- **After the statement,** as Reeves is led past, he stops beside Marcus. Only Marcus hears him:
  > *"Isn't it interesting, Marcus? I remember it the way you remember your field."*
- **Marcus goes cold.** He understands everything at once, but can prove none of it. Reeves has been **seeded**. The man who put Kira in front of him "to see what grows" has had a murder planted in him to see if it takes. It took.
- **The irony:** the evidence that will clear Marcus is a false memory in another man's head. His freedom depends on a lie that nobody but him can see, and he can't use it without destroying himself.
- **Back at the safe flat:** he tells Kira. She listens. Then she lays two things on the table: Nikolai's photograph of the team room, and the forty statements, open at the bandage.

## Ending
Kira, very quietly: "Say it." Marcus can't. It would make him a man accusing the only official who saved him, with no evidence except a hand and a phrase he can't place. He writes it in his UNCHECKED column instead: *"T.W.?"* And then, below it, as a HOPE: *"Please be wrong."*

## Notes
- **This confession resolves the "murder" plot legally** and deepens Twist 3 emotionally.
- Marcus's reluctance is essential: Wei saved him from Unit 3. The Ch 28–29 proof must overcome that gratitude.
- End of Part Three.
