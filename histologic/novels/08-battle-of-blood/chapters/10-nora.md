# Chapter 10: Nora

*Friday, March 14, 2110. An occupied coastal town, Veridica*

---

Isaiah Okonkwo filed the day in the study of the memory palace before it began, as he always did, because a day filed before it happened was a day you had decided to pay attention to.

The Sovereignty's forward headquarters had moved into a seafront hotel in a coastal town whose name he set down with the date, a tall pale building with a glassed-in sun lounge full of map tables and a terrace over the promenade where the wind came off the water hard enough to make the awnings crack. He had been embedded four weeks now, "the witness," and he had sent three weeks of dispatches that an SFW press officer read before they went out, and he had learned the rhythm of being shown things: he was shown the clean occupation, the paper and pencils, the courtesy, and he wrote it, because it was true, and he filed, in the study, the shape of what he was not shown.

He noted the town: the disciplined occupation, the scrip in the shops now, the quiet on the promenade where the transmitter units would once have stood broadcasting; and the one fact that had held since the beachhead, that nobody here carried an interface except two people, himself, and the colonel named Nora Chen, whom the Sovereignty kept in rooms away from the captured storm units, because she was the one officer in the building a storm could reach.

---

Nora Chen's office was on the second floor, and she sent for him in the morning.

She was thirty-eight, and she was Marcus's cousin, and she had left Veridica in 'ninety-five after her father was corrected, and all of that Isaiah already had. She did not hide the scars behind her ears; she wore them, the mark of where she had come from and what had driven her out. She was quick and direct and, when she chose, warm, and she was certain all the way through, which he had learned to treat as a hazard in a source, because the certain told you what they believed and left you to find out whether it was so.

"Does he still keep lists?" she asked him, before anything, pouring coffee from a flask the press office had issued.

"Columns," said Isaiah.

She almost smiled. "He always had to put things in rows. Even as a boy. I had fifteen years on him and I never once got him to leave a thing un-sorted." She set the coffee down in front of him. "You'll have gathered I want you to see something."

"I had, Colonel."

She put the packets on the desk.

They were printed transmissions, in Veridican, a stack of them, received, she said, through the source's channel over months, going back before the landing. Isaiah read them the way he read a leaked document, for what it said and then for what it was built to do. They described The Judge's core in the Factorepo Spire: the storage floors, the guards, the power. They described what the core held now, since the twenty-sixth of October: one merged source, Veridica's and Chronos's together, so that Chronos's true record lay in a Veridican core. And they came to the line Nora read aloud, and underlined with a finger as she read it, because it was the line the whole stack existed to deliver: that whoever held the core held *the record of two nations.*

"Three hundred and thirty million people," said Nora. "One machine. In a building we can walk into in a month." She sat back. "We are not here to rule Veridicans, Mr. Okonkwo. I want that written. We are here to take the machine away from the people who use it on them. Nobody decides for them again. Not the Judge. Not the Ministry that corrected my father." She said *free* the way people said *home*, and he filed the way she said it, because that was the thing he could stand behind, not the belief but the sound of it in her mouth.

At the foot of every transmission was an electronic closing, the same on each: a chain closed backwards from its far edge, a seam. He had seen that seam before, in a windowless room three floors underground in the Sovereignty in December, on a folder's packet, the account of an operation called RESTORATION that the Sovereignty had been handed eleven days before it ran. He did not say so. He did not say whose seam it was, because he did not know whose it was, only that it was the same, and a man did not name what he could not stand behind. He filed it: *same channel.*

He turned the packets over in his hands, slowly, the way he read any object, feeling the weight and the paper and the way they had been printed. They were good prints, made on a machine with ink, and they had been received, Nora said, over months, which meant the channel had been open for months, which meant the source had been feeding the Sovereignty since before the war, since before the fleet sailed, since before anyone in Veridica could have known there would be a fleet to sail. A source that fed an enemy a target before the enemy had decided to come was not a source that had been turned by the enemy; it was a source that had invited the enemy; and he thought of his own letter, in December, *someone in Veridica wants to be invaded*, and found that he had written it before he had any of this, on nothing but a toll said a day early and a line in a folder, and that he had been right, and that being right about a thing like this gave a man no pleasure at all, only the cold weight of having to carry it somewhere.

Her handler's own note, at the head of the stack, gave the source. *At the highest table.*

She let him photograph the packets. She handed him the camera the press office had issued to the witness, "for the record, after," and watched him do it, pleased, because she wanted a witness to her righteousness, the way a person who has done a hard thing for a good reason wants someone to have seen the reason.

---

Farahani crossed the lobby once while Isaiah was there, courteous and busy, and stopped only to ask whether the dispatches were being cut.

"Lightly, General," said Isaiah.

"Good," said Farahani, and went on, and gave no line of doctrine, because he had given Isaiah his lines already and did not repeat himself.

---

In the afternoon Martinez walked him along the sea wall, in the real wind, out of the building's hearing.

She was cool and exact, and she had been watching him since the beachhead, and she said it without any preamble at all, looking at the gray water and not at him.

"We are being given a war," said Colonel Beatriz Martinez. "Now we are being given the prize."

She did not say by whom. She did not ask him what he would do, and he did not tell her, and they walked the length of the sea wall in the wind and she said nothing else, and he understood that she had said the one thing to him that she was not yet saying to her own command, and that she had chosen to say it to the witness, which was either a great carelessness or a message, and that Martinez did not do carelessness.

He thought about her for a long time afterwards, trying to classify her, which was his habit with people, and failing, which was rare. She had read his spiked column in 2107 and understood it, when almost no one in the Sovereignty had; she had shown him a folder in a room he was not meant to enter, in December, and said *write carefully*; and now, on a sea wall, she had told him, an embedded witness whose dispatches she read before they went out, that her own side was being given a war and a prize by a hand she did not name. A careless officer would not have survived to be a colonel in SFW intelligence. A careful officer did not say such a thing to a witness by accident. So it was a message, and the message was not for him; he was only the paper it was written on; the message was for whoever read him, which was Veridica, which was the people he was going to carry it to; and Martinez had worked out, before he had, that the witness would go, and had given him a line to carry when he went. He did not know what she wanted done with it. He knew only that she had chosen to be on the record, once, saying that she saw the shape of the thing, and that an officer who put herself on the record like that was an officer preparing, carefully, to be able to say afterwards that she had seen.

---

That night, in the study of the palace, he set out the rule of three, the way he set out the structure of a long piece, because he had told Kira two winters ago that one fragment was a curiosity and two was a pattern and he would wait for three, and he had three now.

*Phase: justification*, on the attaché's page, in December, in the folder. The war was expected, prepared, a phase.

The packets, the prize, supplied *from the highest table*, through the same seam, the same channel as the operation the Sovereignty had been handed eleven days early.

Martinez, an SFW colonel, saying it aloud on a sea wall: *we are being given a war; now we are being given the prize.*

He did not have the sender. He had the shape of the thing: a war given, a plan given, a prize being given, each handed over by a Veridican hand that asked for nothing in return but that the Sovereignty come. He wrote the one conclusion: *It was not a leak. It was an invitation.* And then he made the decision that ended everything the Sovereignty had given him, the asylum and the passport and the conditions he had never agreed to aloud: he decided to go back. To carry this across the lines to the one person who kept a list and was trying to make the pieces fit. At the head of the parlour table his aunt watched him decide it, and he did not look up at her, and he filed that he had not.

Late, in the press office's darkroom under the red lamp, he made contact prints of the packets, as the press office had taught him to make prints of his dispatches. Then he went to his billet and unpicked the lining of the coat he had been arrested in three years ago, the coat that already held his prints of copy VII and, under those, the Wing C notes written with a burned match, and he sewed the packets' photographs in among them, with small even stitches, the way he had sewn his life into a coat in 2106, and hung the coat by the door where anyone could see it, because a thing hidden where anyone could see it was a thing nobody thought to look for.

Then he wrote nothing down at all. The prints were the record now. His hands had done the keeping. He sat in the good chair by the window until the sea went black, with the other side's prize sewn into his coat and his asylum spent, and did not feel, yet, the cold that he knew was coming.
