# Chapter 29: Elise

*Saturday, May 24 – Tuesday, May 27, 2110. Elena's farmhouse, the border zone*

---

On Wednesday they had heard the Spire at the dresser, through Nikolai's voice line patched to the farmhouse receiver: the order, and Kira's voice saying *it's asking*, and then the long quiet while something happened that none of them in the kitchen could see, and at the end of it the humming on the line, which was Vance herself, humming her own machine out, the tune shaking with her tremor. After that she had gone to bed, and had hardly left it.

On Thursday night Marcus had come, with a name, and asked the old man to sing it, and Old Songs had sung it: *Nora Chen*, into "The Names", the cousin who held a door against her own men. He sang the song every evening now, with the dead from every side, and hummed at the stove between, and sang with words only when the dead needed naming.

On Saturday morning he did the ordinary work, because the ordinary work was how he got through a thing: the damper on the cracked stove door, open two fingers, and tea. Vance's tremor was so bad now that he held the cup to her lips, because she could not. For one line she was twenty-seven again, correcting a word in something he had hummed without knowing he hummed it: "That's the wrong vowel," she said. "It always was." And then she was not twenty-seven.

He sat beside her in the afternoon and hummed the ferry verses of the Lakeshore Air, not the lullaby, because the lullaby was for children and she was not a child and would not have thanked him for it. He asked her nothing. He did not bring up what she had given him in April, the four lines, which he still held unexamined, cold, the way you keep a thing you will need and must not wear out by handling.

He thought, sitting with her, of the two years they had had, young, in the Archive, before any of this, and of the forty-four years since, in which he had argued with her in his head in one correction room after another, and he found that he could not make the two of her into one. There had been a young woman at the far end of a long table who read the gray journal the day it came and argued with him about rhyme and was nearly always right. And there had been a mind that compiled a design for the government of frightened people, that built the cages he had spent forty years inside. They were the same woman. He had known that since November, since the Introduction, since he had heard her voice in a thing typed in 1964 and known it before the end of the first page. And he had still not made the two of her into one, and he thought, humming the ferry verses while she died, that he was not going to, that some people could not be made into one, that she would always be, to him, the girl with ink on her fingers and the mind that built the cage, both, not reconciled, and that holding the two of her unreconciled was the truest thing he could do for her, because she had been both, and a song that made her only one would have been a lie, and he did not sing lies.

Her last words were precise and brief, the way her first words to him across a reading-room table had been precise and brief.

"I told someone," said Elise Vance.

A little after four her hand went still on the blanket, completely, for the first time since he had met her again across a lake, the tremor gone out of it at last; and he sat with the still hand for a long time before he called Elena, because there was no hurry now, and a man was entitled to sit with a thing before he reported it.

---

In Chronos she had been dead since January of 2109, certified in a sealed ward under a name she had lived under there. In Veridica she had not been anyone for forty years, erased from the record before the record was finished being built. He thought the fact once, that a woman was dying in a border farmhouse whom no record in either nation now held, who was dead on one shore and nobody on the other, and he did not follow the thought, because following it led toward the name she had lived under in Chronos, and the name led toward a riddle, and he was not going to let the riddle open on the afternoon she died.

On Sunday Elena and Dmitri dug the grave in the orchard, under the plum trees, beside Alexei's. Elena had brought Alexei back on the night of the twenty-fourth of April and he was buried here, in the orchard, in the spring; and they dug Vance's grave beside his, and Dmitri worked formally, without a contraction, and counted the spadefuls under his breath, because counting was the thing he did when a thing could not be done any other way. Tanaka came out from the field hospital, which was being struck to go home; she had sent word across the lake to Dr. Vale, Vance's son; and Vale could not come, because a Chronos man's eyes were logged and his mother had been dead in Chronos since 2109 and a man cannot be seen at the grave of a woman his nation's record says he buried a year ago. Tanaka said it as a case note, and could not finish it: *Son notified. Attendance:* and left it there.

On Monday the receiver reported the Sovereignty's general killed on the coast road that morning, in the withdrawal, by a Veridican battery that had been cut off behind the lines since February and never got the ceasefire order. That evening Old Songs added *Omid Farahani* to "The Names", because the song took the dead from every side, and a general killed by a ceasefire his own enemy had signed was a death the song would hold whoever he had been.

---

On Tuesday morning they buried her.

They were all there who could come: Elena, Dmitri, Nikolai, Maya, Kira, Marcus, whose custody order had lapsed with the Judge that would have enforced it, Tanaka, Isaiah, Kovač, and the refugees from the barn, children among them. Nobody read from any page. The plum trees were in leaf over the two graves.

The old man gave the one speech of his book, standing at the head of the grave, plain and dry, with no riddle in it and no rhetoric, in the voice he used for the few things he said.

"I argued with her for forty-seven years," he said. "For forty of them she wasn't in the room. I knew her for two, when we were young, on Thursdays, in the Archive, before any of this, and then she stopped coming, and I argued with her anyway, in my head, for forty years, in one correction room after another, because a thing you were taught to fight is a thing you go on fighting in the dark, long after the teacher has gone."

He looked at the grave, and at the plum leaves, and went on.

"We argued about poetry. About rhyme. About whether a thing scanned. She was nearly always right, and I never once told her so, and I am telling her so now, which is forty-seven years too late and the only time it is safe." A small sound in the kitchen of the throat, not quite a laugh. "She was afraid. She lived through the Fracture Years, when everyone lied and nobody could agree what had happened, and it frightened her the way it frightened all of them, and she built a design for frightened people, a way to make a species that could not agree be told what had happened and believe it. And then she wrote down that she was wrong. Very few people write that down. I have met a great many people who were wrong, in a long life, and I can count on one hand the ones who wrote it down."

"I spent forty years inside what she built. One place after another. I am not going to stand over her grave and tell you I forgive her. It was never a thing for one man to give; there were a great many of us in those rooms, and most of them cannot speak; and it is not mine to give for them." He paused. "But I will tell you the thing I worked out, in the end, about what she did."

And then the key line, the one he had carried to the grave to say:

"She built the cage. And she built the key into the cage. And she hid the key in the one place her own design could not look: in someone else's hand."

He let that stand.

"She wrote 'many hands' in 'sixty-six," he said, "on a page I never saw, and she never practised it once, in forty-four years, because she carried everything alone, because that was the kind of woman she was, the kind who tells no one. She practised it once. Ten days ago. At that table." He looked, for a moment, at Kira, and did not name her, and looked away. "She gave a thing to a hand that was not her own. It was the only time she did it, and it was the last thing she did, and it was the thing she had got right in 'sixty-six and been too afraid to do for forty-four years, and she did it in the end. That is what I have to say over her. She got it right, and then she was too frightened to do it, and then, at the very end, she did it. It is more than most of us manage."

---

Then he sang "The Names", through, all the dead since February, Alexei and Nora among them, and the last new verse, alone, because the joining-in was for later, for the plaza, not for the grave.

*Who went down to the water?*
*Name them, and they stay.*
*Elise Vance went down to the water —*
*name them, and they stay.*

He sang her true name, the name no record in either nation now held, the name that was dead on one shore and had never been on the other, and the song held it, because the song held everyone the record would not, and that was the whole of what the song was for, and he had sung it for forty years for exactly this, for a name a machine had erased, put back into the one record that could not be adjusted, which was a tune in the mouths of people who had heard it.

*The record may forget them,*
*the song will not forget;*
*name them, and they stay.*

The last phrase lifted, like a question, and nobody answered it, and the plum leaves moved over the two graves.

---

In the evening, alone at the stove, he hummed the tune he had set in April, what she had given him, low, without the words, which he still would not let open. He did not try to work it out. He had a thing kept in him the old way, cold and whole, four lines fitted to the Lakeshore Air, and he would hear where it pointed on the day someone needed it, and not before, because a thing you did not stare at was a thing you still had in a year, and he meant to have it. He did not turn a single line of it over. He hummed only the tune, with no words at all, so that even his own ear got nothing but the shape of it; and it lifted at the end, like a question, and he let it hang, over the dark kitchen, over the two graves in the orchard, over a nation with no machine left to tell it what had happened, and he banked the stove, and did not sing anything more that night.
