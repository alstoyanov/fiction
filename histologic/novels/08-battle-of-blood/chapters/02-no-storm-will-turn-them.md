# Chapter 2: No Storm Will Turn Them

*Monday, February 17, 2110. The inland road from the coast, Veridica*

---

*Feb 17. 05:30. Company strength: ninety-four. Position: the cutting below the mill town. Weather: cold rain.*

Dmitri Volkov wrote it in the watch log by the light of a shielded lamp, in his own hand, and counted the company twice before he wrote the strength, because a number you had not counted was a rumour. Ninety-four. He had started the month with a hundred and six. He did not write the other number. It was in the log already, on the other pages, with the names.

He commanded a volunteer company under coastal command: former Border Security men who had come to him because they knew him, lakeshore people who knew the ground, a schoolteacher who had walked forty kilometres to enlist, two brothers who ran a ferry that no longer ran. They were not soldiers. He had been a soldier of a kind, an enforcer, which was a thing he no longer said aloud, and he was teaching them what little of it transferred, which was less than he had hoped. What transferred was counting. You counted your people, and you counted the enemy's habits, and you held what you were told to hold.

He had learned the volunteers' names in the first week, all hundred and six of them, because a man he could name was a man he could order and a man he could grieve, and a commander who did not know his men's names was a commander who lost them as numbers. He knew which of them had wives at the lakeshore and which had sons at the coast and which had come because there was nothing left to stay home for. He knew the schoolteacher wrote every night and the ferrymen argued about the tides and the youngest of them, a boy of eighteen, was afraid in a way that would get him killed if Dmitri did not put him beside a steadier man, so Dmitri had put him beside the schoolteacher, which he would think about later. It was not an army. It was a hundred and six people from one shore of one lake, holding a crossing because a colonel's plan said the crossing had to be held, and Dmitri held it with them, and counted them, and that was the whole of what he knew how to do for them.

His lieutenant was a former teacher from a town up the lakeshore, a quiet man with a pack that held a paper journal he wrote in every night. Dmitri had seen him at it, by lamplight, the careful hand going down the page, and had said nothing, because a man who wrote things down was a man Dmitri respected and respect was not a thing he said.

---

In the morning Veridica's weapons came up, and did nothing, and Dmitri had known they would.

Border Security brought two transmitter units forward behind the river crossing and ran them out into the line of the enemy's advance. He had crewed units like them, years ago, under Sokolov, on the lakeshore; he knew the gray vans and the folding masts and the low hum they made that you felt in your teeth before you heard it. He watched them lay a false history of the district over the true one, a drowned road where the causeway was, and he watched the Sovereignty's infantry come on through it at a walk.

They did not look at the drowned road. They did not look at anything that was not there. They came by the drainage tracks and the orchard lanes in loose order, spread wide, and not one of them turned his head toward the broadcast, because there was nothing behind their ears for it to reach. *No interfaces.* He wrote it in the log in four words. *Effect on enemy: none.*

He wrote the other thing more carefully, because it was the thing he would need later.

The enemy did not take the marsh road, the causeway the ridge batteries covered. They came by the drainage tracks and the old orchard lanes, which kept them wide of the arcs of Batteries 7 and 8, the two that were dug in on the ridge behind, masked, that had not fired a round since the landing and were not meant to be seen from anywhere. The enemy's line of advance bent around both arcs, cleanly, the way a man steps around a hole in the dark that he has been told is there. Dmitri noted it in the flat way he noted everything, and drew no conclusion, because a conclusion was a thing you reached after you had the facts and he had one fact: *Enemy avoided ridge batteries 7 and 8. Batteries had not fired. Positions not visible from the coast or the line of advance.* He filed it where he filed the things that did not yet make sense, which was in the log, in order, so that when they made sense he would be able to find them.

---

At two o'clock in the afternoon, with no warning and no build, a fact storm came up over the cutting at full strength, like a switch thrown, and he knew it for what it was before his own body finished reacting to it.

A natural storm built. It came on across an afternoon, a pressure behind the eyes that gave a man time to get indoors. This one arrived whole, at two exactly, with the clean sudden totality of a thing that had been scheduled, and it followed no fact log he could feel; it simply was, all at once, a second world laid over the first. And then he knew the programme itself, because he had run it, in another life, on the lakeshore: a false history of the coast-road district, out of the Border Security test files, the very files he had helped to make. It was two of the captured units, the two overrun behind Battery 5 on the landing day, turned now and run by men who could not themselves perceive what they broadcast, against troops who could.

The cutting became a road. In one history the flooded rail cutting was a paved road, level, dry, running up out of the water toward the mill town, and the men who had interfaces *knew* it was there, with the certainty that came from inside. Two of them stood up to walk out along it.

Dmitri did what the bond did, without his brothers to do it with. He had felt them all his life as weight and cold and pressure, and he did not feel them now, out here; the bond needed no line of sight but it needed, he had learned, to be fed, and he had not seen Alexei or Nikolai in weeks. So he used what he had, which was procedure and the ground. "Down," he said, flat, in the voice he had once used on suspects, the voice that did not argue. "Everyone down. Hands on the ground. The ground is real. Put your hand on it." And then, because a thing shared was a thing that held when a thing felt alone did not: "Names. Down the line. In order. Keep saying them." And the company lay in the cold water and the mud with their hands flat on the earth and said their names down the line, one after another, over and over, dull and rhythmic and shared, and the storm, which could make a thing warm and a lie sweet, could not make a cold procedure anything but cold, and it held most of them.

It did not hold the lieutenant. He did not hear the names. He got up, in the middle of the storm, and walked out onto the road that was not there, up the dry level road toward the town, into the open, and the enemy's fire took him on the embankment, sudden and unshowy, and he fell on ground he believed was a road. One other man of the company, a ferryman, walked off the edge of the embankment into the flooded cutting, onto a surface his own head told him would bear him, and drowned in a metre of water with his pack on, before anyone could reach him.

He crawled the line himself under the storm, from man to man, in the cold water, because a captain who sent an order into a storm and did not go into it with his men was a captain his men would not follow out of one; and he put his hand on the back of each man's neck where the thin scars were and said, close, in the voice that did not argue, "Names. Down the line. Keep saying them. The ground is real." Some of them had their eyes shut and their lips moving; some of them had stopped; and he got the stopped ones started again, one at a time, with his hand on their necks and his mouth at their ears, the old enforcer's trick of a voice too close and too flat to be anything but real, and it was not the bond, it was a worse and slower thing, a man doing by hand and one at a time what his brothers did without touching, and it held them, most of them, because a thing shared and dull was a thing a storm could not make sweet.

The storm ended at forty-one minutes past two. Dmitri wrote the time, 14:41, and knew without being told what had happened: the enemy had switched the units off, because the enemy wanted to advance, and the storm was in their own way as much as Veridica's, the one weakness of a weapon that worked on everyone with an interface and the enemy had none.

The company held the crossing until dark, as ordered, and then withdrew, as ordered, and the enemy took the mill town. Nine dead. Fourteen wounded. Dmitri wrote all of it, the times and the numbers, flatly, in the log, and then he wrote three lines in a separate place, formally, for the farmhouse, in a hand he knew the old man would read: *He was a teacher. He wrote every night. He went out onto a road that was not there.* He did not write the name on the line; the courier would carry the name; a name went into the song in front of people. He folded the three lines and addressed them west, to Old Songs.

---

Night, in a barn behind the new line, with the rain on the roof and the company asleep in the straw, Dmitri sat with the dead lieutenant's pack across his knees.

The journal was inside it. He could feel the square shape of it through the canvas. He did not open it. A man's journal was a man's own, written in his own hand, for whoever he meant it for, and the lieutenant had not meant it for his captain. Dmitri had spent four years reading other men's private records as an enforcer, turning what people wrote and did into probabilities for a machine, and he had promised himself, in a garden at Ashford, that he was done with it. He would carry the pack to the man's people. He would not read the journal. That was a small thing and he held to it, because small things held to were the only kind of integrity a man kept in a war.

He spoke to the pack, because there was no one else awake and the thing wanted saying and he would say it to no living person, in the formal voice he used for the things that went deepest, without a single contraction.

"We have fought with facts for forty years," said Dmitri Volkov. "We have forgotten what they cost."
