# Chapter 3: Embedded

*Thursday, February 20, 2110. The SFW beachhead, the occupied coast of Veridica*

---

*IO. 20 February. 06:30. A coastal school, a few kilometres inland from the beaches, taken for a headquarters. Filed on arrival.*

Isaiah Okonkwo stood in the doorway of what had been a classroom, with his head a little bowed as he stood in most rooms, and filed the place before the day began, because filing it was the work and the work was the only thing he had carried intact across the ocean.

His asylum review had been *brought forward*, the letter had said, in the Sovereignty, in January, and he had admired the precision of the phrase as he had once admired *conditional*, because it told him exactly what was coming without saying it. It had come back embedded: his asylum made conditional on his going with the Sovereignty's forces as *the witness*, to see and to write what he was shown. He had come ashore on Tuesday with the second echelon, in a landing craft, in the wind, an old man in a coat with its lining sewn, and he had stood on a Veridican beach for the first time in three years and felt nothing he could use, and filed that too.

The coat hung on him as it always had. In the lining were his prints of Kira's photographs of copy VII, pages five to twelve, sewn there since the thirtieth of September; and under the stitching, older, the newsprint margins he had written in Wing C with a burned match. He had carried his life in a coat for four years. He was used to the weight.

He filed the headquarters with his prefix and the date. The classrooms were map rooms now, the small chairs stacked in a corridor, a child's painting of a boat still pinned to one wall above a plotting table, which no one had taken down and which Isaiah looked at twice. The soldiers moving through had no scars behind their ears. Everywhere there was paper: orders on paper, maps on paper, identity tags on cords around every neck, because no interface carried a man's name here and a name had to be carried some other way. Nothing in the building reported to anything. A room full of people and no record of it but what a hand wrote down. He found, standing in it, that after three years in the Sovereignty he still noticed the quiet of it, the way a man who has been ill notices that he can breathe.

---

In the whole headquarters, he worked out over the morning, only two people carried the thin white lines of an interface. One was himself. The other was a woman in an SFW colonel's uniform, whom he saw twice across the map room before anyone named her.

He understood why they were marked when, mid-morning, the captured storm units were run forward against the Veridican line, and a staff officer came and told him, courteously, that he and the colonel were to go back to the rear for the duration. Isaiah asked why, as a journalist asks, for the record. The officer said, without embarrassment, that they were the only two people in the headquarters a storm could reach, and that the general did not spend what he did not have to. Isaiah filed it. The Sovereignty's one weakness against Veridica's great weapon was the two people in the building who had once been Veridican.

---

General Omid Farahani received him for eleven minutes, and Isaiah timed it, because a man's time was a thing he gave or did not, and eleven minutes to a journalist in a war was neither dismissal nor courtship.

He was perhaps fifty-five, plain and precise and unhurried, a professional who had plainly spent a life in rooms where things were decided and had kept the habit of deciding them quietly. He spoke the way he had spoken on the broadcast, careful Veridican with the stress a little wrong, and he did not perform for the witness. He said, near the end: "You are here to see whether we fight cleanly, Mr. Okonkwo. Write it if we do not."

"And if you do, General?"

"Then write that too. I am told your own paper cuts you." A very slight dryness. "We will not. We have nothing to protect here but what we do." He stood, which ended it. "You will be shown what you need."

*What you need.* Isaiah had heard that phrase already that morning, from the colonel named Martinez at the map table, who had looked at him across it with a cool steady attention and said only, "Mr. Okonkwo. You'll be shown what you need," and given no sign at all of the room three floors underground in December, or of *write carefully*, said to him on a sea wall in the wind. She was watching him. He let her. He filed the word, both times it was said, and the way each of them said it, and noted that Martinez said *need* the way a person says a thing they have measured and Farahani said it the way a person says a thing they have decided, and that the difference between the two was worth more to him than anything either of them had told him on purpose.

The unnamed attaché from the editor-in-chief's lunch, a year ago, in the Sovereignty, passed through the map room once that morning with a slim folder under his arm, and Isaiah knew the cover's colour and did not look at it, and filed the fact that the man was here, three floors up and in the open, instead of in a room no one was meant to enter.

---

Late in the afternoon, behind the school, in the wind, the colonel with the scars introduced herself.

He knew the face before she gave the name. It was a face he had studied for three years without ever meaning to, because it was built like another face: the same mouth, the same way of tipping the head and looking at a thing as if checking it against something. She was perhaps thirty-eight, in the SFW uniform, with the thin white lines plain behind her ears because she did not hide them.

"Colonel Nora Chen," she said. "SFW intelligence." And then, without being asked, in the flat way of a thing long rehearsed and never said aloud: "I'm his cousin. I left in 'ninety-five. Tell him I'm sorry about the record."

Isaiah did not ask which record. She did not explain it. He filed the sentence verbatim, with the prefix and the time, and said, "I'll tell him, Colonel, if I can reach him," which was true and gave her nothing.

She was quick, and warm when she chose to be, and entirely certain, which he had learned to be careful of in people. She did not hide the scars; she wore them, he thought, as a kind of credential, the mark of where she had come from. She spoke of her father only sidelong, once: "I had a reason to leave. They corrected it." She believed, plainly and without cynicism, that the Sovereignty was here to free Veridicans from the machine that decided for them, and she wanted the witness to see that it was so. He found her sympathetic, which he noted as a hazard, because the sympathetic were the ones whose certainty you had to watch hardest.

"You were in one of their white rooms," she said to him, not asking, because she had read his file, the way intelligence read everyone's file. "The article. Continuity. I read it in the Sovereignty when it came out. It is why I asked for you as the witness." She looked at him with Marcus's way of looking, tipping the head, checking a thing. "You of all people should want this. You were in the thing. You know what it does. We are going to take the thing away." She said *take the thing away* the way a surgeon says *cut it out*, as a clean and obvious good, and Isaiah, who had been in the thing, who had set a stone outside a ring of seven and stayed to be taken to Unit 3, did not tell her that the thing was not a tumour you could cut out of a body and leave the body whole, that the thing was the body, that a nation grown around a machine for forty-four years was not a nation you freed by seizing the machine; he filed her certainty, and said, "I know what it does, Colonel," which was true, and gave her the agreement she wanted and none of the argument.

He asked her, as a journalist asks the only question worth asking, how the Sovereignty had known where to land. She smiled, with something in it that was almost pride. "We had help." And then, of the source she handled, in the voice of a woman naming an asset she rated highly: "A source at the highest table."

Across the yard, at the corner of the school wall, Colonel Martinez was standing with a map tube under her arm, out of the wind. She did not turn her head. But Isaiah, who had spent twenty years watching people not react to things, saw that she had heard, and had chosen to let him see nothing.

---

He watched the headquarters work through the afternoon, because watching was the job, and he filed what he saw. The staff officers passed paper hand to hand; a runner carried an order across the map room on foot because there was no network to carry it; a clerk read a dispatch aloud to another who wrote it into a ledger, the two of them keeping a record the old way, by mouth and hand, the way the coastal towns were keeping their dead, the way Old Songs kept everyone. It struck Isaiah, filing it, that the Sovereignty ran a war the way his own kind ran a resistance, on paper and memory and the spoken word, with no machine to trust and no machine to betray them; and that the thing the Sovereignty had crossed an ocean to seize was the one thing it had built its whole civilisation on refusing; and he did not yet know what to make of that, and marked it as a thing to make something of.

At dusk they showed him the operations map, in the map room, because the witness was to see that the Sovereignty fought cleanly and the map was clean: axes of advance, phase lines, timings, all of it in the SFW's own script, which he could not read, and none of it, he saw at once, ringing a harbour or a factory or a power station. The arrows ran inland, along the roads, bending around the ground that bit back, and they converged, every one of them, on a single marker near the centre of the capital. He could not read the label beneath it. He did not need to.

---

Night, in the study of the memory palace, in the house on Carver Street that had been pulled down years ago and stood whole only in his head, he filed the day in order and then laid three lines of it side by side on the lace runner, the way he laid out the structure of a long piece.

His own letter, of the sixteenth of December: *Someone in Veridica wants to be invaded.*

Farahani, on the broadcast, to the nation: *We are not here for your cities.*

Nora Chen, in the wind behind a school, of the source she handled: *A source at the highest table.*

He wrote beneath them the one conclusion he could stand behind, with his prefix and the date, the way he ended anything: *The objective is not a city.* And beneath that, smaller, because it was a step past what he could prove and he marked the steps: *It is something in one.*

At the head of the parlour table, through the open door, his aunt sat in the firelight with her hands folded on the cloth in the way he folded his own, and watched him write it, and said nothing, as she had said nothing in all the years he had kept her there. He did not look up at her. He had begun, over the autumn, not to look up at her, and he did not let himself ask why. He capped the pen, and closed the study door in his mind, and went to a narrow bed in a requisitioned schoolhouse in a country that had thrown him out, with the other side's secret three lines closer to his hands.
