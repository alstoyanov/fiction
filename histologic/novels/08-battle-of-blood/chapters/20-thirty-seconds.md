# Chapter 20: Thirty Seconds

*Friday, April 25, 2110. The western corridor and the farmhouse, Veridica*

---

At half past one in the morning Dmitri Volkov was on watch in the grain store beside the lakeshore road, counting, because counting was what his mind did when it could not do anything else.

Flares over the corridor, one about every four minutes. Shells per hour, which he logged on the quarter-hour. Vehicles through since dusk, both ways, which he counted and matched against the manifests, because a vehicle that was not on a manifest was a thing to look at. His company had held the western corridor since the ring closed on Wednesday, the one road open between the besieged capital and the lakeshore, under fire by day, and it was the only road to the farmhouse, and so it would be needed, and so he held it, and did not leave it, because a thing you were told to hold you held.

He had known since ten past six on Thursday morning that Alexei had stayed.

Nikolai had come to him with Isaiah's paper entry, and Isaiah had read it from the paper, standing very straight, because he would not say it from memory, and the words had been exact. Dmitri had asked Elena, by radio, to let him go to the overpass. Elena had said, "No. I go. You hold the corridor." And he had held the corridor, because she was right, because the corridor was the only road and he commanded it and a commander did not leave the only road to go and look for his brother's body, however much the leaving wanted to be done; and the not-doing of it was the hardest order he had ever given himself, and he had given it to himself in the formal voice he used for the hardest things, and obeyed it.

The thing he had learned this week, the thing he would carry, was that at twenty-one minutes to midnight on Wednesday, when his brother said the last words and moved along a parapet into a gun's fire, Dmitri had been in the grain store counting trucks, and he had felt nothing. The bond was not telepathy. He had always known that; he had said it to people who did not understand it; it carried where his brothers were and how they were, as weight and cold and pressure, not as words, and not, it turned out, as death. His brother had died forty kilometres off and Dmitri had felt nothing but the ordinary cold of a night watch, and he had gone on counting trucks, and that was the worst thing the week had taught him, and he said it to no one, and wrote it nowhere, and held it.

---

At fourteen minutes past two the radio spoke, and it was Elena, hoarse.

"I have him."

Her cell had gone onto the overpass on the night of Thursday, after the Sovereignty's gun pulled back, and she had him. Then: "Bring Kolya. Before dark." Dmitri said, "Understood," and handed the watch to his second, formally, and wrote nothing in the log about why, because the log was a watch log and a watch log did not keep a man's brother.

In the morning Nikolai came out from the Spire along the corridor, with Isaiah, and the three of them stood in the grain store, and Isaiah stood very straight and read from his paper, because he would not say it from memory, the full message, both halves of it.

"He said: 'Tell Elena I love her. Tell Dima he was first by thirty seconds, and I never minded.'"

Dmitri's face did nothing. He had learned long ago that his face did nothing when he felt the most, and it did nothing now, and Isaiah folded the paper and put it away and did not look at him, which was a courtesy, and Dmitri was grateful for it in the way he was grateful for things, silently.

---

They sat on the step of the grain store, the two brothers, with the corridor under fire a field away and the flares going up every four minutes.

Nikolai had lost the rooftop at Ashford, the memory the cradle took, the three boys on a parapet arguing about who was born first, and he held it now only as a story, from the primer Dmitri had written for him, testimony of a thing that had happened to him and that he could not reach. He looked at his hands, the Nikolai way, and he said it.

"I don't remember who was born first."

"I was," said Dmitri. "By thirty seconds. He never minded."

They sat together on the step. For once Dmitri counted nothing, not the flares, not the shells, not the minutes; he let them go by uncounted, which he had not done in years, which was its own kind of grief, to stop counting.

"Tell me the roof again," said Nikolai, after a while. "The thirty seconds. I don't have it. Wei gave me the page back, the primer, your hand, and I've read it, but it's a story. It's a thing you wrote. I don't have the roof."

So Dmitri told him the roof, the way he had written it in the primer at Ashford, formally, without a contraction, three boys on a parapet twelve years old and forbidden to be there, arguing about who was born first, and the evening their father came up and told them to question things and made them promise to stay together; and Nikolai listened the way a man listens to a thing that is his and that he cannot reach, with his head down and his hands still; and when Dmitri finished, Nikolai nodded, and did not say that he had it now, because he did not have it, he had only heard it told, which was the thing the cradle had left him, a life rebuilt from other people's telling; and that was the thing he and Alexei had shared, in a way, because Alexei had been the one on the parapet in the middle, and now the middle was gone, and Nikolai had lost the memory of the roof to a machine and Dmitri had lost the brother who had been in the middle of it to a gun, and between the two of them they no longer had the roof entire, and they sat with the half of it each of them had.

After a long time Nikolai said "Lyosha," once, into the cold, and it was not a reprimand, the way Dmitri's "Lyosha" had always been a reprimand to a brother who went too fast; it was only the name, said to the air, because the name wanted saying and there was no one left to say it to.

---

Before they left the grain store Dmitri handed the company to his second in command, formally, in writing, the strength and the dispositions and the hours the corridor could be held, because a company was a thing you did not leave on a word, and then he stood for a moment in the door of the store and looked east, where the overpass was, forty kilometres off, where his brother had died while he counted trucks; and he did not go east, because Elena had gone east and he had held the corridor, which was the order, which was right; and he turned west instead, to the farmhouse, where his brother was, to bury him, which was the only thing east had left for him to do, and it was in the wrong direction, and he went.

In the afternoon they drove to the farmhouse along the corridor, under fire, Dmitri driving, Isaiah in the back with his hands folded.

Alexei lay in the barn, on a trestle, under a sheet. Elena had washed him and laid him out; she had brought his body back herself on the night of Thursday, from the overpass, after the gun pulled back; and Old Songs had sat with him since the morning, humming, because Alexei was the one who had always asked him to sing, who had been his first student in the vault technique in Ashford's library, who had always been glad of a tune. Vance sat at the kitchen table holding her cup in both hands to still it, and said nothing, because there was nothing she had any right to say.

From the kitchen window Dmitri watched Isaiah go out into the yard to Elena and give her the first half of the message, the half that was hers. She did not move. She stood in the yard with her hands at her sides, which Dmitri had never seen, because Elena's hands were always doing a thing, counting or holding or wrapping, and now they did nothing, and she stood and heard that her nephew had loved her and said so at the end, and did not move, and Isaiah stood with her and did not touch her, which was right.

---

In the evening Old Songs sang "The Names" in the kitchen, as he did every evening, with the week's dead from the journals, Veridican and one of the Sovereignty's by name. And then the new verse.

*Who went down to the water?*
*Name them, and they stay.*
*Alexei went down to the water —*
*name them, and they stay.*

On the name, his voice broke.

It broke for the first time that any of them had ever heard, the cracked old voice that had sung for Teo and Hanna and Pavel and Samuel and thirty-one of the first war and a ferryman and a girl of nine and a boy's soldier from a ditch, the voice that had not broken for any of them; it broke on *Alexei*, and he stopped, and nobody in the kitchen moved, and the stove ticked, and after a while he went on, and finished the refrain, because the song had to be finished or it was not the song.

*The record may forget them,*
*the song will not forget;*
*name them, and they stay.*

The tune lifted at the end, like a question, as it always did, and this time nobody could have answered it if they had tried.

Elena did not come into the kitchen. Through the window Dmitri watched her stand in the yard, in the dark, with her hands at her sides, until the song was over; and then she came in, and her hands began to do a thing again, which was to put the kettle on, and Dmitri understood that she was all right in the only way any of them would be all right, which was that her hands had found a thing to do.
