Chapter 26: Consent

Monday, May 19, 2110. The directorate building by the harbour, the Chronos lakeshore city

At six in the morning, on the two hundredth day of maximum recording, Julian Kovač woke and knew the time to the second, and did not scroll to it, and wrote in his handwritten journal, and his eyes recorded every line he wrote, so that the directorate knew he had written something and could not know what, which was the one privacy left to a man seen every second, the privacy of a page that had to be read to be known. He had written to Marcus in it many times. He had sent none of it.

At twenty to eight the letter came, by the lake courier who had carried his aunt's reply in December, and it was in Kira's hand.

She had written it, he saw at once, knowing that whatever he read, the directorate read with him; it gave away nothing that reading it aloud to the directorate could spend. One sheet.

There is a way for Veridica's Judge to end. If it ends, yours ends with it: they restore from one source. We will not do it unless Chronos consents. We are asking.

No page. No mechanism. No day. He read it twice. He thought of his own message, which had gone to Nikolai through his aunt in March: I would like Chronos to be asked this time. He had not expected to be answered. A man at maximum did not expect to be asked anything; he was a thing that was watched, not consulted; and here was a letter in a Veridican hand, carried across a lake, asking him.

He knew the thing the letter did not say, from his own log. On the night of the twenty-fifth of October his key's rights had been revoked in the true source, from the Veridican side, in eleven seconds; he had read that in his log a hundred times; the source had never been Chronos's; the source was one, and the one was in Veridica, and whatever ended it ended Chronos's past with it. He had written that down in November, in his journal, and nobody had read it, because it was on a page and a page had to be read.

At ten Aubry came to his room herself, for the first time, not to the family room but to the recorded room where he lived, and she had printed the letter, as she printed everything, and she sat in the soft chair with it in her hand and thought, as she always thought, in distributions.

She had changed her paper, he saw, and not her manner. In December she had printed the Veridican broadcast of his aunt's letter, dated the twentieth of January 2108, and put it in her drawer beside her order of the twenty-first of August, and she did not apologise for the eleven weeks in which she had not believed him; she stated the error the way she would state an error in a probability.

She sat in the soft chair across from him, in the recorded room, with the printed letter in her hand and her gray eyes moving over him the way they moved over a column of figures, and he thought, as he had thought at their first meeting on the ninth of November, that she was a woman who saw the world as a set of overlapping curves and was always quietly working out where they crossed. She had destroyed him, in a way, in the autumn; she had believed the record against him on the one point where the record lied, and recommended the maximum, and signed it; and she had been wrong, and the proof had been a letter in his aunt's folder the whole time, and she had set the likely thing aside because the method said one document did not outweigh a nation's record. And here she was, in his room, having read a Veridican letter through his own eyes at twenty to eight that morning, come to ask him to help decide whether Chronos's Judge should die; and she did not pretend the autumn had not happened, and she did not grovel over it, and he found that he preferred the not-grovelling, because a woman who groveled would have been a woman trying to be forgiven, and Aubry was not trying to be forgiven, she was trying to get a hard thing right this time, which was the only apology worth anything.

"I set aside the likely thing," she said, "because it was one document against a nation's record, and the method is that one document does not outweigh a nation's record. The method was correct. The method was written by someone else, for reasons I did not know, and it was correct and it destroyed you, and both of those are true, Mr. Kovač, and I have had to learn to hold them." She folded her hands. "I will not make the mistake twice. That is the whole of what I have to offer you by way of apology, and it is not much."

Then she stated the problem, plainly.

"Chronos has no one who can say yes for it. We are an algorithmic democracy. The Judge reads the collective will from patterns in the facts, and there are no representatives, and there is no one whose signature is the nation's. If I ask the Judge whether it should end, it will answer from the record." She looked at him. "And they wrote the record. In October. The Judge would answer in the voice of the people who wrote it. It cannot be asked."

"Then it has to be people," said Julian. "With every word logged. You and me. Two people the thing was done to. One by a man, and one by the record. Deciding in the one room in Chronos where nothing can be hidden, which is this one, because I am at maximum and everything here is already public."

He mentioned the minority pattern, the 0.07, the eleven thousand and the nine-and-a-half thousand, the 9S patients and the six paper keepers, the only Chronos memory there was; and Aubry accepted the point, that there was a Chronos that remembered the old history, that could in principle be asked; and then she set it aside, because there was no time to count them before Wednesday, and because a referendum of the only people who remembered was its own kind of writing the answer. There was no clean way. There were two people who had been wronged, and a page they could not see, and a day coming.

The argument was brief, because they agreed.

A Judge that restored from a source in another nation's core was a Judge whoever held that core could write. The Sovereignty would hold the Spire by Wednesday. If the thing did not end, Chronos's past had a new author, in a foreign capital, chosen by whoever had invited the war. Better ended. Better the machine gone than the machine enslaved, which was, though neither of them knew it, the front of a page across a lake, written forty-four years ago.

"They wrote us once," said Aubry. "Nobody writes us again."

Julian agreed, and added nothing to it, because there was nothing to add; Aubry had said the whole of it in six words, and a thing said whole did not want a second saying.

He thought, though, before he agreed, of what it was they were consenting to. Not a shutdown of a machine; the ending of a thing a whole nation had been built around; the loss of the record that had settled every dispute in Chronos for forty-three years, so that after it Chronos would have to decide its own disputes, by hand, by people, for the first time, without a Judge to read the collective will; and he, a historian, who had come to Chronos at nineteen because he wanted to live in a country that forgot nothing, was consenting to make his country into one that would have to remember for itself, with no machine to remember for it. He had spent fifteen years keeping lists in an annotation layer, trusting the log to hold them. The log would be gone. Everything he had written in it, two thousand three hundred lists, would be gone, or would survive only as whatever he could carry out by hand. And he found, weighing it, that he consented anyway, because a record you could not trust was worse than no record, and a record whoever held the Spire could write was a record you could not trust, and better no Judge than a Judge with a new author in a foreign capital; and he agreed with Aubry, and added nothing, and did not say the part about his own lists, because his own lists were a small thing against a nation and he had learned, at maximum, the size of things.

They drafted the consent together, and she printed it and signed it.

Chronos has been asked. Chronos consents that its Judge may end with Veridica's. — C. Aubry, Administrator, Temporal Defence. J. Kovač, citizen.

And then the part that mattered as much as the consent, which was how it would travel. If Chronos's consent arrived in Veridica only on paper, by courier, anyone could say Veridica had forged it, the way Chronos had once said Veridica forged everything. So it would be published before it was sent. Julian would disclose the whole morning, under the right of self-disclosure that survived his sentence: the letter, the conversation with Aubry, the drafting, the signing, uncut, timestamped to the nanosecond, to the world. After that no one could edit it, and no one could say it had been written for them, because the whole world would have watched it be written.

At one o'clock Aubry left. At the door, the only personal thing she said, dry, not turning: "Your aunt keeps good paper, Mr. Kovač." Which was as near as Constance Aubry came to tenderness, and Julian took it as what it was, which was a great deal.

At two o'clock he chose the hour, because it had been Marcus's hour, the hour of the Day of Testimony, the hour he had published MERIDIAN in, the hour that was, in his own private reckoning that the whole world could now read, Marcus's; and he spoke the formal words of self-disclosure, and the indicator at the door changed, and the whole morning went out into the world, permanent, timestamped, beyond recall.

Then he took Aubry's printed copy, the one for the courier, and below the two signatures, in his own sloping hand, the capitals leaning forward together, eager, he wrote one line for Marcus.

This time you asked.

The line was logged to the directorate, like everything he did; but it was not part of the disclosure; it was a thing written on a paper copy for one man to read, kept the old way, by a hand, for a reader. He folded the sheet, and gave it to the officer at the door for the courier, and it went across the lake to reach Kira on Tuesday, with the consent above it and the one line below, so that when she stood at the console on Wednesday she would have beside her both the thing that made it lawful and the thing that made it, to one man, forgiven.
