# Chapter 22: Dad

**POV:** Maya Reeves
**Date:** Mon May 5, 2110 (13:40 to about 15:30)
**Setting:** The **National Broadcast Directorate** by the Meridian Bridge: the entrance hall, relay hall 2, the calibration floor with its cradles, and her father's glass office above the relays; the bridge outside
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
A daughter asks her father how to stop the thing he's proudest of, and he tells her, because he's curious. The man who did the worst thing in her life is the only one who knows how to undo this one. She takes the key and refuses him the absolution.

## Beats
- **13:40, the bridge.** Nikolai waits in the van at the east end of the Meridian Bridge with an earpiece. He has raised a war-systems **work order**: *clock distribution fault, relay hall 2*. Maya is the Commission technician named on it. She times everything: *In at 14:06.*
- **The hall.** Public Order officers are at the doors, calm and pleasant. Everyone in the Directorate is calm. She notices how the staff type: slowly and certainly, without backspacing.
- **The calibration floor.** Among the relay racks are **six cradles**, hoods on jointed arms, and in each a person reclining with a calm face: "reference minds" from the list, against which the carrier is tuned. She knows the shape of a cradle from Isaiah's article and from her father's code. She feels sick, as if a stair were missing.
- **14:19, Dad.** He comes down the stairs from his office, courteous and unhurried, in a gray suit, with clean hands. *"Maya. You came in properly. Through a work order."* A soft, delighted laugh. *"I'm proud of you."* He shows her the scale as a colleague would show a visitor round: a wall panel of the capital's districts, each with its curve. *"Interesting, isn't it. Nobody has ever done a city."*
- **The preamble.** She asks whether he knew what was at the front of the order. *"I heard it said in meetings, years ago. I always assumed it was the Ministry's. It was so obviously right that nobody needed to sign it."* He never knew who wrote it and never asked. He doesn't name the manuscript.
- **Her question.** *"How do I stop it?"* He considers her with real interest. He has no patriotism and no justification, and his candour is the horror.
  - ***"I'd like to see what happens if you turn it off."***
  - **The weakness, in his words:** they gave him a week, so the carrier has **no clock of its own**. It rides the **national clock**, the time signal every interface keeps. Shift the capital's clock under it, even by a fraction, and the carrier arrives out of step, and the interfaces discard it as noise.
  - *"Nobody should die of it. Probably. I don't know. That's what's interesting."* Why tell her? *"I've never seen a population wake up. Nobody has."*

  She times it: *14:41.* Nikolai, in her ear, says nothing at all.
- **The cradles again, on the way out.** She asks what happens to the six. *"They'll wake with everyone else, if you do it."* She doesn't thank him.
- **Anger and grief together.** On the stairs she cries and is furious in the same sentence, and she won't let him see either. She thinks of her journal on Mar 29 (Ch 14): *He didn't do it*, crossed out, and *He is doing it now.*

## Ending
**15:02, at the door.** He holds it for her, courteously. ***"You still write that I didn't do it."*** Maya: ***"You didn't. You did this."*** She walks out onto the Meridian Bridge and doesn't look back. She times that too.
