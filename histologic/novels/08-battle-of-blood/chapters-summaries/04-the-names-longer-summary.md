# Chapter 4: The Names, Longer

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Mon Feb 24, 2110 (afternoon into night)
**Setting:** Elena's farmhouse in the border zone: the kitchen with its cracked stove door; the barn, which is full of refugees from the coast; the yard at dusk
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The war reaches the farmhouse as names. Old Songs does the one job he has always done, and the song stops being Veridica's. The chapter is quiet, and it should hurt. Vance nearly says something and doesn't.

## Beats
- **Afternoon.** Elena and Alexei are on the coast, Dmitri at the front, Kira and Nikolai in the capital. Two of Elena's people (unnamed) run the farmhouse. The **barn holds refugees** from the coastal towns: about forty people, families and old people, sleeping in the hay under blankets from every house for twenty kilometres. Old Songs, 71, peels potatoes for all of them. His POV is sharp, tired and dry.
- **Where the names come from.** Not from the bulletin: The Judge counts only what reports to it, and the enemy dead are *not recorded* (Ch 1). The names come on paper, as copies of journals carried west by the pairing couriers, and in the refugees' own books. Coastal people have written down Veridican soldiers, neighbours, a harbourmaster, and **SFW soldiers they buried**, copying the names off the paper tags the Sovereignty's soldiers wear because no interface carries them. There are also **Dmitri's three formal lines** about his lieutenant (Ch 2), in a hand Old Songs knows. He reads them twice.
- **The rule he keeps** (as in Book 7 Ch 7): he will not sing a number. Where there is no name, he hums the place where it should go.
- **Vance** sits at the kitchen table. Her tremor now **shakes the cup**, and tea slops onto the wood; she no longer tries to hide it from him. The radio on the dresser carries Farahani's sentence again, as it has every night since the 13th: *We are not here for your cities.* Vance listens to it with her head a little on one side, the way she listened to a line of verse in 2063. She says, very precisely, to no one: *"No. They wouldn't be."* Then, after a moment: *"The page—"* She stops. She puts both hands round the cup. He doesn't ask. He watched her take a page out and put it back on Jan 10 (Book 7 Epilogue), and he didn't ask then either. He hums one phrase of the Lakeshore Air, low, which asks nothing, and goes back to the potatoes.
- **Dusk, "The Names."** He sings in the yard by the barn door, with words, because at the farmhouse he is free. The refugees come out in coats and stand in the cold. Quote the opening exactly:
  > *Who went down to the water?*
  > *Name them, and they stay.*
  Then one verse per name: the Book 4 dead, Book 6's thirty-one, the Gullhaven ferryman, and now the war. A Veridican gunner from Battery 5. A harbourmaster. A girl of nine from a coast-road town. An SFW soldier whose name a fisherman's widow copied off a tag. Dmitri's lieutenant. The prose may say he sings each name without printing them.
- **It grows every evening.** Tonight it runs past half an hour. People go in to the children and come back. Some stay for all of it. A few learn the refrain in the first week and sing it with him, *name them, and they stay*. Nobody objects to the SFW names; they were buried by the same hands.
- **The boy.** Near the end a boy from the coast, about ten, who came with his grandmother, pushes to the front. He watched a soldier die in a ditch outside his town on the landing day, "who wasn't ours". The soldier said his own name to him, as if handing it over, and the boy has kept it all the way west, because nobody wrote it down. He asks Old Songs to put it in.
- **Old Songs does.** He listens to the boy say it and repeats it carefully, the way he once learned words in a language he'd never been taught. Then he sings it into the third line, *(—) went down to the water — / name them, and they stay*, and the boy mouths it with him. The page doesn't print the name.
- **The close,** exact:
  > *The record may forget them,*
  > *the song will not forget;*
  > *name them, and they stay.*
  It lifts at the end like a question.

## Ending
Night. The yard is empty and the barn is quiet. Old Songs stands a long time in the cold. The last name in the song tonight came from no record and no paper, only a boy's memory of a dying man's voice. It is the way the old songs were kept before the Fracture Years, and the way, he thinks, the Sovereignty still keeps its own. **The song is as long as the dead.** Tomorrow it will be longer.

## Notes
- **Lyrics exact** (`old-songs-character.md` §3). Unnamed dead are hummed, never counted (Book 7 Ch 7 practice). **No new names are printed;** the boy's soldier's name is sung, not shown.
- **"Veridican and SFW alike"** (plot v2 Ch 4): the SFW names come from paper tags copied into journals, and from the boy's memory. The enemy dead are "not recorded" (Ch 1).
- **(Twist 3 clue, very light)** Vance's *"The page—"*, broken off. **Don't** have her say "There is a page"; that is Ch 8's line. Her *"No. They wouldn't be."* should read as an old designer hearing the obvious. Don't gloss it.
- **Riddle caution:** no riddle, and nothing about apples, shelf marks, catalogue numbers, Kerr, or anyone asking or saying a thing twice (README decision 16). The boy says the name once; Old Songs repeats it once.
- **Voice rules:** one spoken request (to the boy); no speech, no riddle; words because he is free; every tune lifts like a question.
- **Vance** (74): the tremor is worse than in the Book 7 Epilogue.
- **The refugees** are about forty, all unnamed, and they stay in the barn through Ch 8.
