# Chapter 6: Field Hospital

**POV:** Dr. Yuki Tanaka
**Date:** Mon Mar 3, 2110 (dawn to night)
**Setting:** The Chronos field hospital going up on the capital's eastern edge, in a requisitioned depot and a field of tents; the triage tent; the ward tents; the linen store. Ashford's roofline is visible across the fields to the north-east.
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Chronos comes to Veridica's aid because it believes a history Veridica wrote for it. Tanaka comes home legally alive and practises medicine on the edge of the city where she was a prisoner. In her case notes one small, identical phrase keeps recurring, and she files it without knowing what it is.

## Beats
- **Case-note opening.** *Mar 3, 2110. 06:40. Chronos Field Hospital, eastern edge of the capital. Beds: 180, of which erected: 96. Staff: Chronos, 212; Veridican, 4.* She is 50. The Chronos hospital badge is clipped to her coat. In Veridica she has been *STATUS: LIVING* since Dec 2106, so she is here lawfully.
- **Why Chronos came.** Since the Awakening (Book 7), Chronos believes Veridica's history, and its Judge found Veridica "without responsibility" (0.93). So Chronos is formally at peace with Veridica, even friendly. When the SFW landed, it sent a hospital. Tanaka knows exactly whose history made that friendship, and says nothing.
- **Morrison** comes with the hospital on **Chronos papers**. In Veridica he is still officially dead, by choice (Book 5). He keeps to the equipment tents and wears a Chronos engineer's tabard. His hands are steady. Nobody in the capital has looked twice at him yet.
- **Ashford.** From the depot yard she can see its roofline across the frozen fields. She looks once, touches the thin scar on her right temple, and doesn't look again. *(It is to be reopened in Ch 12; don't mention that here.)*
- **The Chronos staff** are life-logged at every moment, and they know it and like it: orderlies, nurses and drivers who volunteered because their logs tell them Veridica is a friend who never harmed them. They work hard and cheerfully. The first convoy of wounded arrives at 09:00: soldiers from the front on the capital road, and volunteers.
- **The cost.** At midday a Chronos ambulance on the road east is caught by SFW fire. Two orderlies are killed, both unnamed. They died, Tanaka thinks, for a past they never had. She writes it in case-note form and can't finish the line: *Cause of death: shrapnel. Reason:* She leaves it there.
- **The phrase.** Through the afternoon in triage she takes histories, as she always does: name, district, what happened. Five patients, then seven, all volunteers from the **capital's own districts**, answer a question she didn't ask with the same sentence, in the same words and the same flat, contented tone: ***"We must be certain now."*** A boy with a shattered hand says it. So does a woman of sixty with shrapnel in her thigh. Tanaka notes the phrase verbatim in each file, with the patient's home district beside it. It is only **three districts**, the same three, and she writes them down without knowing why.
- **Her discipline.** She doesn't diagnose, and she doesn't call it anything. Wartime produces slogans. People repeat what they hear. She puts it in *the place where she keeps things*, unlabelled, beside 7-K and Moss's sincerity. She doesn't mention it to Morrison; he is under a generator all afternoon.
- **Evening, the ward tents.** She sets down a cup of tea very carefully when a shell lands a kilometre off. The Chronos nurses don't flinch. Their logs have never recorded a shelling, and they don't yet know how to be afraid of one.

## Ending
**22:30, the linen store.** A young Chronos nurse, unnamed, is folding sheets beside her. She asks it simply and warmly, and every word goes into her log: *"Why is Veridica so grateful to us? We've only done what anyone would do for a friend who never hurt us."* Tanaka knows the honest answer: that Chronos's memory of its friend was written in Veridica last October, by people she loves. She also knows that anything she says here will be in this girl's life-log for ever. She has no honest answer she can give. She says, "Get some sleep," and folds the next sheet.

## Notes
- **(Twist 2 clue)** *"We must be certain now."* (plot v2 Ch 6.) Keep it light: an identical phrase in several patients, from a few districts, filed and not interpreted. **Don't** note an "absence of doubt" clinically; that is Ch 17's diagnosis. Morrison doesn't hear it and doesn't recognise anything yet (Ch 17).
- **Dating (settled, README decision 11):** the phrase appears **before** the Stabilisation Order is signed (Mon Mar 17) and before district-by-district broadcasting begins (Mon Mar 24). **Pilot "stabilisation" broadcasts began in three capital districts on Mon Feb 24, under emergency information powers**, less than two weeks into the war; the Order legalised them after the fact. Every patient here comes from those three districts. Nobody in this chapter knows any of that, and nothing on the page names a broadcast. Tanaka's list of home districts becomes evidence in Ch 17.
- **The hospital** (README decision 5): arrives **Mon Mar 3**, at the capital's **eastern edge**. Tanaka and Morrison come with it. "Friendly because Chronos believes the history Book 7 wrote." Ashford being in sight is new; it is on the capital's eastern edge (`nations.md`).
- **Deaths:** two unnamed Chronos orderlies. Keep numbers modest.
- **Chronos life-logging** is why she can't answer: it is a canon rule (`nations.md`). Tanaka's and Morrison's Veridican interfaces don't record.
- **Tanaka's voice:** case notes, lines left unfinished, formal address, tea, and the cup set down carefully. No jokes.
