# Chapter 26: Consent

**POV:** Julian Kovač (his only POV chapter in this book)
**Date:** Mon May 19, 2110 (about 06:00 to 14:30)
**Setting:** Chronos lakeshore city: Julian's recorded room in the detention wing of the directorate building by the harbour (clean, warm, a window on the harbour, fog far out on the lake); nothing else
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Book 7 decided for Chronos, and this chapter asks it. The two people Veridica's operation wronged are the man whose trust was the door and the official whose nation was written. They answer in a room where every word is public before it is spoken. They consent, and they make the consent impossible to forge.

## Beats
- **06:00, maximum, day two hundred** (he was sentenced Nov 1). He still has no sensation of it, only knowledge. He writes in his handwritten journal, and his eyes record every line. He has written to Marcus in it many times and sent none of it.
- **07:40, the letter.** It comes by the lake courier who carried his aunt's reply in December, and it is in **Kira's** hand. The writer knows that whatever Julian reads, the directorate reads with him, and wrote accordingly. It is one sheet:
  > *There is a way for Veridica's Judge to end. If it ends, yours ends with it: they restore from one source. We will not do it unless Chronos consents. We are asking.*
  There is no page in it, no mechanism and no day. Julian reads it twice. He thinks of his own message, which reached Nikolai through his aunt on Mar 18: *"I would like Chronos to be asked this time."* He had not expected to be answered.
- **What he knows, from his own log.** On the night of Oct 25–26 his key's rights were revoked **in the true source**, from the Veridican side, in eleven seconds. The source was never Chronos's. He wrote that down in November and nobody read it.
- **10:00, Aubry.** For the first time she comes to his room herself, not to the family room. She read the letter through his eyes at 07:41. She has **printed it**, as she prints everything. She sits in the soft chair, courteous and exact, and she thinks in distributions.
  - **She has changed her paper, not her manner.** In December she printed the Veridican broadcast of his aunt's letter, *20 January 2108*, and put it in her drawer beside her Aug 21 order. She doesn't apologise. She says what she got wrong the way she'd report an error in a probability: "I set aside the likely thing because it was one document against a nation's record. That was the method. The method was written by someone else."
  - **The problem, stated plainly.** Chronos has **no one who can say yes for it**. It is an algorithmic democracy: the Judge reads the will from patterns in the facts, and there are no representatives. ***"If I ask the Judge whether it should end, it will answer from the record. They wrote the record."*** Julian mentions the 0.07, the only Chronos memory there is, and she accepts the point. There is no time to count them before Wednesday.
  - **Julian:** "Then it has to be people, with every word logged. You and me." Two people wronged, one by Marcus and one by the record, deciding in the one room in Chronos where nothing can be hidden.
- **The argument, briefly.** A Judge that restores from a source in another nation's core is a Judge whoever holds that core can write. If the SFW takes the Spire, Chronos's past has a new author. Aubry: ***"They wrote us once. Nobody writes us again."*** Julian agrees. He adds nothing to it.
- **The consent.** They draft it together, and she prints it and signs it:
  > *Chronos has been asked. Chronos consents that its Judge may end with Veridica's. — C. Aubry, Administrator, Temporal Defence. J. Kovač, citizen.*
- **Why self-disclosure.** If Chronos's consent only arrived in Veridica on paper, anyone could say Veridica forged it, as Chronos once said of everything. So it is **published before it is sent**. Julian will disclose the whole morning under the right that survives his sentence (Book 7 Ch 19, 21): the letter, the conversation and the signing, uncut, timestamped to the nanosecond. Nobody can edit it after that, and nobody can say it was written for them.
- **13:00.** Aubry leaves. At the door, the only personal thing she says: "Your aunt keeps good paper, Mr. Kovač."

## Ending
**14:00.** Julian chooses the hour because it was Marcus's. He speaks the formal words of disclosure, and the indicator at the door changes. Then he takes Aubry's printed copy for the courier and, below the signatures, in his own sloping hand, writes one line for Marcus: ***"This time you asked."*** The line is logged to the directorate like everything else, but it isn't part of the disclosure. He folds the sheet and gives it to the officer at the door for the courier.

## Notes
- **Aubry's line is exact** (plot). So is "This time you asked." (README decision 26).
- **Kira's letter deliberately gives away nothing** that could stop the handover: no page, no trigger, no date. After 14:00 the whole world can read that Chronos has consented to *something*. That is why the SFW sends its team into the Spire early on Wednesday (Ch 27). Maitland still doesn't know that his signature is the trigger.
- **The consent wording and the courier are new.** The paper copy, with Julian's line, reaches Kira on **Tue May 20** by the same courier, through Kovač. Kira has it beside her at the console on Wednesday (Ch 28).
- **Julian's setting** follows Book 7 Ch 21. He is still at maximum until the Epilogue.
- Love stays unspoken, and there is no thought of Marcus beyond the journal and the line. Adrian is not present.
- There is no manuscript text here, and Julian never sees the page.
