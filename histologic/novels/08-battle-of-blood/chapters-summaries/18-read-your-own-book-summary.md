# Chapter 18: Read Your Own Book

**POV:** Kira Osman
**Date:** Fri Apr 18, 2110 (07:00 to about 23:30)
**Setting:** Kira and Marcus's flat, early; The Judge's war coordination floor in the Factorepo Spire; at night, a cellar under a bakery in the north district, one of four cellars running that night, linked by courier
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The antidote is the thing they already built. A broadcast can take away a feeling, but it can't change what a hand wrote in January. Kira turns the journals into a mirror that people hold up to themselves, and she learns what it costs to gather people in a city whose machine can count them.

## Beats
- **07:00, the flat.** Tanaka's handwritten copy of Principle Four came by pairing courier on Tuesday. Kira has read it every morning since, in incident format: *Source: copy X, p. 19, via Tanaka. Status: verbatim. Mark: none on the page.* She doesn't read it aloud.
- **Her own book first.** Before she asks anyone else to do it, she opens her paper journal at January. *Jan 10, the headland. I am afraid of what is coming and I don't know what to do with it.* She checks what she feels now, after weeks under the broadcast, and the fear is thinner. She can't tell whether that's courage or the carrier. **She decides not to trust the feeling, only the page.** That is the whole method, and she writes it at the top of a clean sheet.
- **The Spire, war coordination.** The Judge is running the capital's shelters, water points and shell warnings superbly. On the district panel the calmest districts line up in the order of the broadcast schedule (Ch 17). Since Tuesday she and Nikolai have hunted the carrier inside the network. It has no separate channel, no node of its own and no source they can isolate. Nikolai: *"It isn't in the network. It moves when the network moves."* Taking the capital's broadcast down would take the shell warnings with it. Under stress her old compulsion comes back, and she hears herself reciting 7.3 under her breath, *rotation interval ninety seconds*, and stops.
- **The sheet.** *Read your own book.* Three lines:
  1. **Read your January aloud,** in your own hand.
  2. **Say what you feel now.**
  3. **If they differ, write the difference down,** dated and signed.

  There is no index and no list of who came. The paired copies of people's journals, held in other districts under the pairing method (Book 6), come in by courier for anyone whose own book burned in the shelling.
- **The risk, seen and accepted.** The Judge knows every citizen's location. It can see thirty people in a cellar at 19:30, even though it can't see what they read. Kira says it to the couriers plainly: *"It can count us. It can't read us."* She goes ahead. *(See Ch 21.)*
- **19:30, the bakery cellar.** There are about thirty people, lamps on crates, and shelling in the distance, with dust shaking down from the joists. The volunteers who come in are smiling. Nobody is frightened, and that is the frightening thing.
  - **A woman** reads what she wrote on Jan 12 about her son, posted to the coast: *I want him home. I don't care who wins.* She says she now feels only pride. Then she weeps and doesn't know why.
  - **An old man** reads, feels nothing different, and signs nothing. Kira logs him too.
  - **A girl of about sixteen** reads her mother's entry aloud because her mother can't, and her mother takes the book back.
- **Marcus comes late** and sits at the back. He reads his own book silently, the way he did after the clerk in Ch 15, to check that he still disagrees. He looks up once at Kira: a colleague confirming a result.
- **The count.** Couriers bring tallies from the other three cellars. It is **four cellars and a hundred and nine readers**, and sixty-two of them wrote a difference down. It's small, and it is written.

## Ending
**About 23:00.** A man in a volunteer's armband, calm all evening, reads his entry for **Fri Jan 3** aloud: ***"I don't know if we should fight."*** He reads it twice and looks up at the room. ***"Who wrote this?"*** Kira: ***"You did."***

## Notes
- **Why paper:** Phase Two erases doubt; it doesn't forge a hand. Registered Concordance journals were rewritten in Book 7; paper pairing books were not.
- **Setups:** "It moves when the network moves" points at the clock dependency; only Reeves names it (Ch 22). The Judge's count of who gathers feeds Pell's list (stated in Ch 21).
- **Manuscript:** Principle Four is known to the circle from Tanaka's copy. **Kira does not read it to the cellar:** "They don't need the page. They need their own." No mark is on the page and none is mentioned.
- **Dates:** Jan 3, 2110 was a Friday; Jan 10 is the headland (Book 7 Epilogue).
- **New:** the three-line sheet, the bakery cellar, the four cellars, and the tallies (109 readers, 62 differences). The walk-ons are unnamed.
- **Voice:** incident format; cold, level anger; the 7.3 recital under stress.
