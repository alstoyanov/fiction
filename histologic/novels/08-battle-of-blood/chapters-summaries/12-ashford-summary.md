# Chapter 12: Ashford

**POV:** Helena Kovač
**Date:** Fri Mar 21, 2110 (05:50 to about 18:30)
**Setting:** Kovač's flat (dawn); her office behind the Commission's testimony intake, and the intake hall; the street outside with one box
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The state asks the woman who ran Ashford for eleven years to sign it open again, and to hand over the names of the people who write things down. The file carries the title of a principle nobody in the circle has read. She refuses on paper, which is the only kind of refusal she trusts, and it costs her the office. She takes her folders and leaves everything else.

## Beats
- **05:50, the flat.** She hasn't slept. The file came to the intake by Public Order courier on **Tue Mar 18**, the morning after the order was signed, with a deadline of **Fri Mar 21, 17:00**. She has read her part of it three times. The guns to the east are audible at night now.
- **The file, as an object.** A thick card cover with a typed label:
  - *STABILISATION OF SYMPATHISERS ORDER (WAR POWERS), 17 MARCH 2110*
  - and beneath it, the file's title: ***CORRECTION OVER PUNISHMENT***.
  
  Her copy is **an extract only**: *Schedule 3, Duties of the Registrar of Testimony*. The preamble and the technical file aren't in it. The extract's recital cites the Order as **"regularising the stabilisation measures in effect in designated districts under emergency information powers since 24 February 2110"**. She reads the date twice. A law has been written for something that was already running, and she doesn't know what the measures are. She notes it in her folder without comment.
- **The title.** She has seen those three words once before, in Marcus's pencil, on his notes of copy VII's contents page (Book 7 Ch 11): *Correction Over Punishment …… IV …… 18*. Nobody in the circle has the text. **Someone in the government has.** She writes that down first, because it is true, and then writes nothing after it. *(Twist 2 clue.)*
- **Schedule 3, in order,** as she would brief a committee:
  1. The Registrar shall transfer to Public Order **the intake's register of depositors**, meaning every citizen who has deposited a written journal or testimony since 2107, "for assessment of sympathy".
  2. The Registrar, as custodian of Ashford's records since 2107, shall **countersign the recommissioning of Ashford Correctional Facility** "for the treatment of enemy sympathy".
  3. The categories to be assessed: **journal keepers**, and persons who **accepted SFW paper** on the occupied coast (Ch 7).
- **09:00, the intake opens as always.** Depositors queue in the hall with their books even now, under shelling, and she watches them from her doorway. Some of them are in Ashford's old files. She knows their names without looking.
- **09:30, the call.** She telephones Public Order and asks for the full order, with its preamble. It is **restricted under war powers** and not available to the Registrar. The voice is pleasant and certain. The recording is on, so she answers in her administrative voice (voice rule 3): "Thank you. I'll note that."
- **10:15, what she used to sign.** Her history in flat inventory: eleven years as Director; *remains processed according to standard protocols*, a hundred times; ten false deaths certified to save lives; four years keeping a folder instead of acting. *Not again* (Book 6 Ch 7). She pushes her glasses up her nose.
- **11:00, the refusal.** She types a minute on intake paper, and signs it:
  > *1. The register of depositors is testimony, given freely under the Doctrine. It is not evidence against the people who gave it. I will not transfer it.*
  > *2. I directed Ashford for eleven years. I will not countersign its reopening.*
  > *3. I decline both duties in Schedule 3.* H. Kovač, Registrar of Testimony.
  She sends the original by the returning courier, and files the carbon in her own folder. She knows that paper refusal delays a transfer by an afternoon and prevents nothing. It is on paper, and that is the point.
- **15:40, the removal.** It is quick and courteous. A letter comes from the Ministry of Historical Integrity over **Chen Zhao's** signature, thanking her warmly for "a lifetime of careful records" and relieving her as Registrar with immediate effect. He never gives a hard order, only a kind one. An acting Registrar, unnamed, arrives with a Public Order officer and a handover inventory.
- **The keys.** She hands over the ring the inventory lists: the office, the hall, the strongroom. The **key to the basement archive** isn't on the inventory. It is an old key from 2107 that lives on her own ring with her flat keys, and nobody asks for it. She notices, and says nothing. *(Set-up for Ch 21.)*
- **The shelf.** Her own folders are on the shelf behind her desk: Ashford, the Commission, the certificates, and at the end ***ANSWERED*** (Book 7 Epilogue). The depositors' boxes belong to the depositors. She can't take them and has no right to.

## Ending
**18:30.** She walks out through the intake hall past the queue with **one box**, her folders, held against her coat. She leaves every other box behind. On the step she doesn't look back at the building. She looks at the queue, which is still there.
