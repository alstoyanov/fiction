# Chapter 23: Silence

**POV:** Nikolai Volkov
**Date:** Fri May 9, 2110 (02:30 to about 14:00)
**Setting:** The **maintenance vault under the east abutment of the Meridian Bridge**, where the capital's clock distribution feed enters the Directorate; afterwards, the capital's streets at dawn and the morning after
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
They give the city back its fear. It is the right thing, and it is terrible, and it can't be taken back. The old minute that was used on two nations is used this once by the people it was used on.

## Beats
- **May 5–8, compressed.** Nikolai and Maya build the offset in a basement: a small module that doesn't cut the capital's clock but makes it **wander**, a fraction of a millisecond, never the same way twice, so the Directorate can't re-lock. It costs The Judge's war coordination about **four minutes** of degraded shell warnings. Nikolai: *"I'll take four minutes."* He keeps a copy of everything, as always. His one flash of dark humour: *"If they shoot me, at least it will be for a clock."*
- **Why 03:12.** Maya asks. It was the Chronos Gap, the Rewriting, and the default on Wei's too-clean tools (Book 7). *"Because it's theirs. I'd like it back."*
- **02:40, the vault.** Wet concrete, cable trays, and the river loud above. Shelling in the east. Maya has tapped the Directorate's carrier-lock monitor and reads it aloud. He looks at his hands while he works. He thinks, once, of Alexei, who would have wanted to do this tonight, and puts the thought away.
- **03:12:00.** He applies the offset. Maya: *"Lock lost. Oh-three-twelve and four seconds."* The Directorate tries to re-lock at **03:31** and again at **04:50**, and the wander defeats it both times. The Judge's coordination drops at 03:12 and is back at **03:16**.
- **The city wakes over hours.** It isn't a switch.
  - **About 04:00:** a shell lands near a street shelter, and for the first time in weeks people scream.
  - **05:30:** a queue at a water point breaks up into argument.
  - **06:00:** the volunteers stop smiling.
  - **Through the morning:** a man sits down in the road and asks where his son is. A woman stands in a doorway reading her own journal aloud to nobody. People weep at last for the siege dead they had felt only as pride. Doubt comes back, and so does grief. It is theirs.
- **06:30, the radio.** Kira, from a north-district cellar: *"They're reading."* Nikolai thinks of Marcus in the dark under the intake, waking the same way, and of the six in the cradles on the calibration floor (Ch 22).
- **The government.** The war cabinet meets at 07:00. By noon Maitland **suspends stabilisation broadcasting "pending review"**, over Pell's objection, and **mass correction stops**. The fault is logged as a relay fault in the capital's time distribution. Nobody has yet said otherwise.
- **Wei, reported.** A clerk at Emergency Information tells Maya that Wei's light was on all night. He watched the carrier fall on his panel at 03:12, made no call, and went home at 07:00. Nikolai hears it secondhand and doesn't interpret it.
- **Reeves, reported:** at 03:20 he asked for the logs.
- **Noon, the bridge.** Nikolai and Maya walk out into a city that is frightened for the right reasons. It is a city of people who know they are under siege, who know their sons are at the coast, and who aren't sure of anything.

## Ending
On the Meridian Bridge, among people crying and arguing and alive, Nikolai says to Maya: ***"It's worse. It's better."***
