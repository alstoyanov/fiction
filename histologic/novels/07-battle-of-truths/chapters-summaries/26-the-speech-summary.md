# Chapter 26: The Speech

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Fri Dec 6, 2109 (afternoon into dusk)
**Setting:** Elena's farmhouse in the border zone: the kitchen, by the stove with the cracked door
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This is Old Songs' one speech of the book. He tells them what the songs were for, and what the design does to people who check. It is neither forgiveness nor exile. Then he sings the whole of "The Two Maps" for the first time, to the man who was using its giver in the month it was written. It ends on a question that everyone in the room can answer.

## Beats
- **Morning.** Since the morning of Oct 5 (Ch 13) he has hummed nothing at Marcus: no rising yes, no falling no. On Monday he stood at the back of the crowd on the Justice Center steps, officially dead, and heard a confession with nothing attached (Ch 25). He has been deciding ever since. Show ordinary work: the damper on the cracked stove door, open two fingers; potatoes; Vance at the table, holding a cup to still her hand. She knows what he is deciding, and she doesn't ask.
- **Afternoon.** Marcus comes by day, with Dmitri driving, and has to be back before dark. Present: Vance, Elena, Kira (at the far end of the table from Marcus), Dmitri, Alexei, and Nikolai, pale and quiet *(see handoff)*. Marcus has come to return the farmhouse book, which he borrowed for the confession's dates. Old Songs stops him with a hand flat on the table.
- **The speech.** Plain, dry, and without rhetoric, spoken standing by the stove. Expand it to about 600–800 words around this canonical core (plot v3, exact): ***"I kept my memory in songs for forty years so that no one would ever have to trust one man. You trusted one record, because it said what you were afraid of. That is not a failure of the system, Marcus. That is how the system works. It works on the best of us first."*** Around it:
  - *"You understood the falling phrase in August. Everyone at this table did. You went anyway. I wrote it in the book. Not to shame you. So it would be written."*
  - *"The man you were lying to sent me my wife's and my daughter's storm, with its run number. He sent it because you told him an old man sang. I broke a cup. It was a good cup."* He says Anya's and Katya's names once.
  - *"You were the one of us who checked. That's why it chose you. It doesn't need to fool the careless."*
  - *"I don't forgive you. It isn't mine to give. It's his, and you mustn't ask him for it."* A pause. *"And I'm not sending you away. Somebody has to keep singing at you."*
  - He uses no riddle, and he doesn't say "A song dies with the singer."
- **The room.** Nobody speaks. Elena looks at her hands. Alexei starts to say something, and Dmitri touches his sleeve. Vance, who is precise, corrects nothing.
- **The song.** He sings **"The Two Maps"** to Marcus, whole, for the first time, to the tune he began in October (Ch 13). Quote all three verses exactly:
  > *Two maps of the one river,*
  > *one for either shore;*
  > *one puts the ford by the willows,*
  > *one by the mill-house door.*
  >
  > *They fought for the ford forty winters,*
  > *and neither would give an inch;*
  > *the river ran on between them*
  > *and never once asked which.*
  >
  > *But who drew the maps for the fighting,*
  > *and sold them the ink and the quill —*
  > *and why is the hand in the corner*
  > *the same on both maps still?*
- **While he sings.** The second verse is Anya's lakeshore. At the third he watches their faces. Dmitri, who read Principle Two aloud at this table (Ch 11), shuts his eyes. Kira looks at Marcus for the first time that day. Vance's cup is quite still.

## Ending
The last line lifts like a question, as all his tunes do. He lets it hang. Nobody answers, because every one of them knows the answer, typed on pp. 9–12 of copy VII: *"Let there be several Histologic nations, differing in method and agreeing in substance, and let there be enemies outside them all."* Marcus leaves before dark. At the door Old Songs hums, very low, for the first time since October. It is one phrase, neither rising nor falling. Marcus stops, and then goes.
