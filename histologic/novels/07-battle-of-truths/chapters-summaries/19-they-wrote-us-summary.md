# Chapter 19: They Wrote Us

**POV:** Administrator Constance Aubry (her only POV chapter)
**Date:** Fri Nov 1, 2109
**Setting:** The Temporal Defence Administration, Chronos lakeshore city (her office, early morning); the detention wing of the **directorate building by the harbour** (an interview room: clean, warm, soft chairs, a window on the harbour, a camera in each corner)
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This is the one Chronos official who knows what happened and can't prove it, because the record agrees with the attackers. She is honest and certain, not cruel. She believes the record on the one point where it is lying, and she has good reasons to. Julian tells the truth, and it sounds exactly like what a guilty key-holder would say.

## Beats
- **06:30, her office.** Temporal Defence is in crisis, and her staff are perfectly calm. Her deputy (unnamed) reports that the record is concordant on every contested event and asks what, exactly, the crisis is.
- **Her paper.** Aubry prints every directive she signs; it is Temporal Defence practice, and she has always thought it a vanity. In her drawer is the printed order of **Wed Aug 21** that resumed corrective broadcasting "in response to Veridica's second attack on the memory of Chronos". The record's copy of that order now reads otherwise. On Monday Oct 28 she submitted the paper to The Judge, which weighed one unlogged document against 150 million concordant records and set it aside. She thinks in distributions: the chance that every contested record in a nation moved one way in one night by accident is nil. So is the chance of proving it.
- **The Archive's record of the night,** read in order:
  - The reconciliation rooms were opened at 22:00 on Fri Oct 25 under **co-chair access, granted by Kovač, J., on Fri Oct 18**.
  - A run from the true source began at 02:40 and finished at about 04:06. It was accepted as legitimate, because by definition it was.
  - The Archive key's rights in the true source were revoked during the run. The record reads that as the Archive correcting a compromised key.
- **Her own part.** She accepted the Joint Inquiry within two days in August (Ch 9), and her signature is on the approval. She thought she was using the commissioner again, as with MERIDIAN. She files that against herself and goes on.
- **10:00, the interview.** Julian has been in detention since the morning of Oct 26. He is gray, exact and courteous, in yesterday's cardigan. She is courteous too. She never tells him what to do; she tells him what is likely. She takes him through **his key, his logs, and his hours with the Veridican commissioner**. He answers everything. He did not know. He gave the access because he trusted Marcus.
- **The key.** He says, plainly, that he got it only in 2108, from his predecessor: ***"the keys in January, the office in February."*** (He took office on Wed Feb 1, 2108.) He calls him "the old man" and does not explain. She lays out the record:
  - the Archive's **public roll**: *Key-holder since 2104*;
  - the key's signing history, one countersignature every night since 2104, under his name;
  - his own log's system layer, which carries each of those countersignatures;
  - her own **printed MERIDIAN annex** from Nov 2108, a personnel sheet: *senior reconciliation key, held since 2104*. It is paper, and it agrees with the record.
  The night of Oct 25–26 touched contested events. The roll isn't one, and her paper shows it read 2104 a year before Veridica came.
- **The January morning.** He asks to see his own log for the morning in January 2108 when he remembers the old man at his door with the keys. It shows him **alone at his desk**, working, at that hour. He watches it twice. "That isn't what happened." Aubry, not unkindly: "In Chronos, Mr. Kovač, that is what *happened* means."
- **The garden.** She remembers Thu Aug 15 at the Customs House: *"I've only had it since—"*, and her own point of protocol, which cut across him. She never asked how the sentence ended. Now she has heard it, eleven weeks late, and she doesn't believe it. The probability that the record is wrong in the two places that would clear him is small, and she says so.
- **Either way.** He is Veridica's instrument or its fool. The sentence is the same. He asks, quietly, whether the war is over. She answers: ***"They didn't defeat us. They wrote us."***
- **The law, stated plainly for him.** At maximum, recording is streamed at full resolution to the directorate, with nothing sealed. **The right of self-disclosure survives the sentence;** it always has. Nobody at maximum has ever used it, because there is nothing left to disclose that the directorate hasn't seen. She says this to be fair, not as a hint.

## Ending
17:30, her office. She writes the recommendation herself, the maximum for an Archive key-holder: **recording forced to maximum, and removal from office.** She reads it once, prints it (as always), and signs it. The Judge ratifies it before evening. She is not cruel, only certain.
