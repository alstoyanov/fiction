# Chapter 21: Maximum

**POV:** Julian Kovač
**Date:** Mon Nov 11 – Fri Nov 15, 2109 (ending at 14:00 on Friday, the Day of Testimony)
**Setting:** The detention wing of the **directorate building by the harbour**, Chronos lakeshore city: his room (clean, warm, a window on the harbour and the fog far out on the lake); the family room, where visitors are brought (soft chairs, a camera in each corner)
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This is the inside of the man it was done to. He is seen every second, and he reads himself being used, at nanosecond resolution, until he understands it whole. His answer isn't revenge. It is the one thing Chronos can't take from an outlaw: he publishes all of it, uncut, including what he never said.

## Beats
- **Mon Nov 11, maximum, day eleven.** There is no sensation. He simply *knows* that every heartbeat, glance and word goes live to the directorate, with nothing sealed. The officer at the door has read what he dreamt. He was removed from office on Nov 1, and he is no longer Senior Historian. The key is gone too. Its rights in the true source were revoked on the night itself.
- **His handwritten journal** was returned to him with the sentence, because in Chronos paper is nothing. Now his eyes record every line he writes in it, so the one private thing he had isn't private. He keeps writing anyway. (This sets up the Epilogue: *"I am seen every second and nobody sees me."*)
- **What he remembers.** He is a historian, and he remembers the old Chronos history. His log now tells him otherwise, but on Nov 1 his log also showed him alone at his desk on the January morning he remembers the old man bringing him the keys (Ch 19: the keys in January, the office in February). He no longer defers to it. He may be the only man in the building who doesn't.
- **Tue Nov 12, the reading.** A citizen keeps read access to his own log, even at maximum. He starts at **Mon Sep 9**, the opening, when he couldn't look at Marcus all day, and **Mon Sep 16** on the Archive steps ("I hoped it would be you"). He reads forward slowly, the way he would read a source.
  - His log holds what his eyes caught without his noticing: where Marcus looked when Julian wasn't watching.
  - **Fri Oct 18:** the co-chair access, given unasked, and Marcus's face in the half-second after. Julian watches it four times.
  - The evenings in his flat; the journal page he turned past too quickly; the garden at Saltmarsh Crossing, talked about as a hope; **Wed Oct 23** at his door, "Marcus, I—", and Marcus's hand on his arm.
  - **His own heart rate** every time Marcus came through a door. The log knows what he never said.
- **Wed Nov 13, Adrian.** His younger brother comes across the lake again, by the route he never describes, and is brought to the family room. The brothers sit together in a room where nothing is private, and everything they say goes to the directorate as they say it.
  - Adrian is easy and theatrical at first, then blunt. He saw it at the September dinner, and that was the "odd look" when he asked after "the commissioner" (Ch 10). He didn't tell Julian, because Julian was happy. He went to Kira instead (Ch 14).
  - Adrian: he has done this himself, to a woman he loved. He knows what it looks like from the other chair. It was real on his side too, "and it doesn't help".
  - He offers to hate Marcus for him. Julian says no. They talk about their father, who stopped saying Julian's name. Adrian promises to come back. He does, and he is beside Julian in the Epilogue.
- **Thu Nov 14, the understanding.** He finishes on **Fri Oct 25**: the Inquiry's closing session, and Marcus staying on "to finish the draft report". Then he goes back to the night of Oct 25–26 in the Archive's public bulletin, and to what Chronos woke up believing. He sees the shape of it all at once: his trust was the door, and his key was the lock. He says it aloud, at maximum, where it becomes the confession the directorate wanted: ***"I helped them erase my own nation."***
- **The decision.** The right of self-disclosure survives outlaw status. Aubry told him so, to be fair (Ch 19). Disclosure is complete or it is nothing. He won't cut a second, because cutting is what was done to his log, and to his country. He will publish **every hour he spent with Marcus Chen**, from the first day of the Lakes Talks (Mon Nov 12, 2108) to the closing session on Fri Oct 25, 2109, with his heart rate and everything else he never said. The hour is **14:00 on Fri Nov 15**, the Day of Testimony. That is Marcus's hour: publication, and the plaza readings.

## Ending
Fri Nov 15, 13:59, in his room, facing the window. At **14:00** he speaks the formal words of disclosure. It is instant and permanent, timestamped to the nanosecond, and it goes to the world. The directorate's indicator at the door changes, and nobody comes in. He sits down and opens the journal to a clean page.
