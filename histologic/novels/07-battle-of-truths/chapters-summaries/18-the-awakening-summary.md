# Chapter 18: The Awakening

**POV:** Dr. Yuki Tanaka
**Date:** Sat Oct 26 – Mon Oct 28, 2109
**Setting:** The teaching hospital in the Chronos lakeshore city: the storm-trauma ward, Tanaka's fourth-floor office (shared with Morrison), the ninth-floor corridor, and **Ward 9S**; Tanaka's flat on Saturday night
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This chapter shows the rewriting from inside, as a clinical presentation. A nation of 150 million agrees, calmly and sincerely, with a past it didn't have on Friday. There are no storms and no seeding, only people consulting their records and trusting them over their own memories. Tanaka is a Veridican in Chronos, and her own interface records nothing. She remembers everything, and she knows exactly who did this.

## Beats
- **Sat 07:30, the handover on the storm ward.** The night registrar, a careful young man (unnamed), reads back his own casualty report from Friday afternoon. It now says what Veridica has always said about the lakeshore. He pauses, checks his log, and says, "I must have been tired. I wrote it right, though." He means it. Tanaka sets her cup down very carefully.
- **The morning.** Colleagues talk about the lakeshore over the ward desk the way people talk about the weather: it was always Veridica's, and the Temporal War was Chronos's own aggression, "which everyone knows". Nobody is distressed. Tanaka hears a phrase she learned in a Veridican school primer forty years ago, word for word, in a Chronos nurse's mouth.
- **The broadcast, mid-morning:** *Chronos Archive key-holder detained. Kovač, J.* The Judge's daily reading of the collective will reports the record **concordant** on the lakeshore for the first time anyone can remember. Give no probability; the attribution comes on Nov 4 (Ch 20).
- **How she knows.** She wasn't told the plan. She doesn't need to be. The Joint Inquiry was sitting in the Archive, a Veridican commissioner held co-chair access, Julian has been arrested, and the new past reads like a Veridican textbook. She does the sum once and doesn't finish it.
- **Morrison** comes in at noon and reads the morning bulletin over her shoulder, as he did the stabilisation circular. He is Veridican-born, and he remembers too. He goes gray and says "Sorry" to nobody. He says nothing else about it all weekend.
- **Dr. Vale on the ninth floor,** tall, stooped, beautifully mannered, says it is "a sad business at the Archive". He agrees with the new record as calmly as everyone else. That frightens her more than anything else on Saturday. She doesn't ask him about it.
- **Case note (italics), Saturday evening, at her kitchen table:** *Onset between 02:40 and 04:10 (per the Archive's own bulletin). No sensory event reported. No distress. Patients consult their records and report agreement. Memory, where it disagrees, is discarded as error. Presentation resembles recovery.* She stops there.
- **Sun Oct 27, Ward 9S,** which is sealed, shielded and unlogged, and runs on paper. The day-room screen carries the new history. **The long-term patients argue with it.** Their lives have never been logged in here, so they have nothing to consult but memory. An old man (listed as **"Patient B.N."**) tells the screen it is wrong: when he was a boy the ferry to the other shore meant "going abroad", and the lakeshore towns were Chronos towns. A woman laughs at the screen and turns it off. The bed by the east window is made and empty.
- **Tanaka begins taking testimony** in the **twelfth notebook** she started in 9S in March (Book 6 Epilogue). Each entry is dated, timed and placed, in the patient's words, and signed or initialled by the patient, Kestrel-style. She makes the distinction her voice requires: *I am not recording what happened. I am recording what they remember. That isn't quite the same thing.* It is still more than anyone outside this room has.
- **Mon Oct 28, afternoon.** A woman from a Chronos-shore town comes to the hospital asking for "the Veridican doctor". She was one of the **six Chronos keepers** at the Night of Copies (the one who told the officers, "Wait. I'm writing." See Notes). She has her paper book in her coat. Her log says one history, and the book in her own hand, countersigned by her neighbours, says another. She believes the book. She asks, quite steadily, whether she is ill. Tanaka: "No." The keeper has spoken by line to the other five. **All six books disagree with their logs, and all six keepers have kept their books.**

## Ending
Late on Monday, alone at her fourth-floor desk, Tanaka writes the case note she has been avoiding since Saturday: ***Population, approx. 150 million. Presentation: acquired certainty. Onset: overnight. Aetiology: my friends.***

## Notes
- **README decision 14:** nothing is seeded. Chronos citizens trust their logs over unaided memory. The only people who still remember the old history are the **9S patients** (never logged) and the **six Chronos keepers** (paper). Tanaka and Morrison remember as Veridicans whose interfaces don't record; they are witnesses, not Chronos memory.
- **Tanaka was not told about RESTORATION in advance** (new; flagged). If Ch 4 or another earlier chapter has her told, change "She wasn't told the plan" to "She knew there was a plan; she didn't know it could do this."
- **The keeper** is the Chronos-shore keeper from Book 6 Ch 26 ("Wait. I'm writing."). She stays unnamed, and so do her town and the other five keepers. Ch 27 uses all six.
- **"Patient B.N."** is initials only, as the ward requires. It is a new detail. The Epilogue's "patient who remembers the old lakeshore" can be him.
- **What the 9S patients remember is Chronos's old version**, which was itself partly broadcast. Tanaka's distinction covers this. Don't resolve which version was true.
- **Storms:** none on the page. They stop on Nov 4 (Ch 20).
- **Vale's belief** is shown once and not examined. Nothing about his mother.
- **Voice:** italic case notes, unfinished lines, exact distinctions, formal address. The Ending's case note is the plot's, word for word.
