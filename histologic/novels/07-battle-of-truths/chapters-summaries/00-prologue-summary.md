# Prologue: The Rewriting

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Tue Aug 13 – Wed Aug 14, 2109 (evening to about 07:30)
**Setting:** Kestrel Point: the rebuilt reading room in the old chandlery on Harbour Street; Ruth's house; the neighbour's kitchen at number 14; the ferry office at dawn
**Length:** ~3,300 words

## Purpose
Open on the attack at human scale, in the town that already knows what a book is for. A lie arrives in a neighbour's handwriting, under names that never signed, and the only thing on Harbour Street that holds is paper. Close on the first accusation that will kill someone.

## Beats
- **Tuesday evening, the ferry season.** It's a hot, still night. The summer timetable hangs in the ferry-office window: **07:40, 12:10, 17:40, 21:40.** At the end of Harbour Street the old pier is only water, and everyone agrees about that now. Ruth locks the **reading room**, rebuilt over the spring inside the chandlery's shell: new pine shelves, and paint over char that still smells faintly of smoke when the day is hot.
- **Two books.** The **Kestrel Book** has **43 contributors**, is on paper, and is unregistered. It goes home in Ruth's coat, as it always has, because the keeper takes it home at night. It has a thinner companion on the shelf, **the pairs**. Under the pairing method, that volume holds hand copies, made in February, of two other towns' books. One of them is the street book of **Net Loft Row in Gullhaven**, the next town up the shore. The pairs volume stays locked in the reading room. (This matters in Ch 3.)
- **The Concordance terminal.** In April Commission outreach installed a public Concordance terminal in the corner of the ferry office, "for anyone who wants to register". About a third of Harbour Street did so after the fire.
- **03:14. Hammering on the door.** The neighbour at number 14 (**unnamed**) is a widow who registered her personal journal in April "to be safe". She had scanned her paper pages from 2108, and she has written at her home terminal since. She couldn't sleep for the heat. At **03:12** her terminal redrew the page she was reading, as though someone somewhere had pressed *restore*. She scrolled back, and entries she knew had changed.
- **The kitchen at number 14.** Ruth reads the screen. The changes are small and plausible, the sort that turn a street against itself:
  - a quarrel over a fence that never happened;
  - a loan "never repaid";
  - and on **Christmas Eve, 2108**: *"Saw E. Varley at the chandlery door, 22:05, alone."*
  Each is **countersigned by neighbours who never signed**, in forged countersignatures that look like any others on a screen. The widow is shaking. "It's my name on it. I'd never." From the drawer she brings the paper book she kept until April. Its Christmas Eve entry has no Varley in it.
- **Ruth knows.** At 22:05 that night Emmet Varley was on the shore road with her, carrying the lantern while they walked an elderly contributor home. The Kestrel Book says so, countersigned. Varley is still on probation with his red margins (Book 6 Ch 19), so a registered line accusing him is the easiest lie in town to believe.
- **By dawn the whole street is comparing screens** in doorways, in nightclothes, in the heat. Every registered journal disagrees with the paper it once was, and the registered journals disagree with one another. Ruth opens the Kestrel Book on a doorstep under the lamp. It agrees with itself, margins and all, and with nobody's screen.
- **Wren**, fifteen, reads the widow's screen over Ruth's shoulder: "Somebody wrote it in her voice." Ruth notices that Wren says *wrote*, not *changed*.

## Ending
**06:20, the ferry office.** Ruth opens up for the 07:40 and, because she has to know how far it goes, reads the next town's pages on the public terminal. Net Loft Row registered its street book in May. An entry dated **Sun Jun 10, 2108** records a street meeting, and in it a named man "admitted" that in **November 2107** he gave Enforcement the names of the Row's households who sheltered people from the strip. It carries **three countersignatures, two of them from the dead**: two neighbours who died in last year's storms. Ruth knows the name. Through the window, the **Gullhaven launch** comes round the harbour wall to meet the 07:40, and its skipper lifts a hand to her, as he does every morning.

## Notes
- **(Twist 1 clue)** The page "redraws" at **03:12**, and the Rewriting runs **03:12–03:29**, the Chronos Gap's minutes. Ruth doesn't know that. Ch 1 makes the link.
- **(Twist 1 texture)** The feeling that "someone pressed restore" anticipates Ch 1's **restore path from the master**. Ruth has no word for it.
- **Deviation (flagged):** plot v3 has the hammering "at 03:12". Since the edits begin at 03:12, the widow notices at 03:12 and the hammering comes at 03:14.
- **The ferryman is unnamed on the page** (README decision 4). The entry names him and Ruth knows him, but the prose never gives the name. Keep that true in Ch 3 and Ch 7.
- **New details:** the pairs volume; the Net Loft Row street book (paper from May 2108, paired in Feb 2109, registered in May 2109); the Jun 10, 2108 entry; the public terminal in the ferry office; Varley on the shore road on Christmas Eve (compatible with Book 6 Ch 18, where Ruth walked a contributor home).
- **Storms:** there are none. This is not a storm. Nothing is perceptual; the lie is on a screen, which is worse.
- **Kestrel Book:** 43 contributors (Book 6 Epilogue). Ruth is its keeper (Book 6 Ch 26). The chandlery burned on Christmas Eve 2108 (Book 6 Ch 18).
