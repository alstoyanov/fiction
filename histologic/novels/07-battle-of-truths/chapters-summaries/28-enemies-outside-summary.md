# Chapter 28: Enemies Outside

**POV:** Isaiah Okonkwo
**Date:** Mon Dec 16, 2109
**Setting:** The Sovereignty of Free Will: a windowless reading room in the intelligence directorate, which he was never meant to enter; his rooms, at night; the memory palace (the study, then the parlour)
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
**Twist 3.** The SFW knew about RESTORATION before it happened, because someone in Veridica told them, and told them in a form built for a war. Every clue Isaiah has filed since August falls into one row. His own column is in the case. Principle Two stops being a page and becomes a schedule.

## Beats
- **Morning.** Colonel **Beatriz Martinez**, SFW intelligence, sends for him with no reason given. She is courteous and exact, and like everyone in the Sovereignty she has no interface. Her family came to the Sovereignty as refugees in the Fracture Years; she has the name and not the language. She is the officer who read his spiked Doctrine column in 2107 and understood it (Book 5 Ch 27). She says so once, as a credential.
- **The room.** It is three floors below the office where, in March, he saw a folder he wasn't meant to see. This is the same folder: ***HISTOLOGIC COORDINATION: STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS***. It is thicker now. Martinez opens it and sits back. She wants to watch him read.
- **The packet.** It is marked **received Mon Oct 14, 2109**: eleven days before the operation. Inside is a **full account of RESTORATION**:
  - the codename;
  - the target, the Chronos Temporal Archive and its reconciliation rooms;
  - the cover, the Joint Inquiry;
  - the access, through the co-chair and a named Senior Historian;
  - the date, Fri Oct 25, overnight.
  It is accurate in every line he can check.
- **The seal.** The packets came through channels inside Veridica, and each is sealed with the same electronic **seam**: a chain closed backwards. He has seen it described in Kira's notes, the signature she first read on a lost hour. He doesn't say whose. He only knows it is the same.
- **The attaché's page.** Behind the packet is the military attaché's analysis, the same courteous man from the editor's lunch (Ch 5) and the "full set, for assessment" (Book 6 Ch 12). Its heading reads ***Phase: justification***, the line he glimpsed in August.
- **The row, filed in the study as he reads:**
  - **Aug 23:** the attaché quoted the Gullhaven toll a day before Veridica published it (Ch 5).
  - **Sep 30:** he was asked to authenticate pages he hadn't published (Ch 12). The request sits here too, clipped to a packet with the same seam.
  - **Oct 14:** RESTORATION, in full.
  - **Nov 18:** his own column, "The Restorer", printed within the hour and uncut (Ch 22).
  It isn't a leak. It's a supply line.
- **His sentence.** In the Council's war brief, under *Grounds*, his condemnation of Marcus is quoted in full, with his name. He hears belief in how people say words, and the brief says *Veridica* the way people say *the target*.
- **Principle Two, in his hands.** His prints of Kira's photographs of copy VII, pp. 5–12, have been sewn into his coat lining since Sep 30. He has no originals and no p. 1. He doesn't take them out, and he doesn't show Martinez. In the palace he reads it again: *"let there be enemies outside them all… Controlled conflict answers every question before it is asked."* The page was written for a war of facts, and someone is now arranging the other kind.
- **The exchange.** **Martinez:** *"We are being given a war. I would like to know by whom."* He sets the question out like an editorial, and then says it: ***"Someone in Veridica wants to be invaded."*** She doesn't disagree. She closes the folder. *"You were never in this room, Mr. Okonkwo."* A pause. *"Write carefully."* She doesn't know whose phrase that is. He does.

## Ending
Night, his rooms. He writes one letter, by the Synthesis courier, which is the only channel he still trusts. It carries his prefix and the date, and one line: ***Someone in Veridica wants to be invaded.*** He addresses it to Marcus. Then he sits in the parlour of the palace for a long time. At the head of the table his aunt is watching him, and he doesn't look up.
