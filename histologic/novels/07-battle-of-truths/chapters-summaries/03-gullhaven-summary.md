# Chapter 3: Gullhaven

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Mon Aug 19, 2109 (morning to about 04:00 on Tue Aug 20)
**Setting:** Kestrel Point: the ferry office and the locked reading room; the shore road north in fog; Gullhaven: Net Loft Row and the ferryman's house above the slipway
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This is the first death of the second war, caused by a countersigned lie on a screen and a proof that arrived on foot. The method works, and it is slower than a mob. Ruth has to live with the four hours.

## Beats
- **Five days since the Rewriting.** The Commission has said publicly that entries were altered, and it can't say which ones. People believe the altered entries that match their fears. The Gullhaven launch still meets the 07:40. Its skipper, the **ferryman (unnamed)**, is quiet this morning. Since Wednesday, fewer people from the Row ride with him.
- **The real wound.** In November 2107 two households on Net Loft Row really were taken by Enforcement, for sheltering people from the strip. One of the men came back corrected. The lie in the Jun 10, 2108 entry is fastened to a grief that is true, and that's why it holds.
- **17:40. The launch doesn't come.** At 18:10 a Gullhaven woman who walked down the shore road tells Ruth that the Row has "asked him to stay in", and that they're meeting at seven "to have it out".
- **The pairs volume.** Ruth knows what's in the locked reading room: the Row's street book, hand-copied into the Kestrel pairs in **February 2109**, in Kestrel hands, and checked line by line against the original by two Row contributors. It is the one copy nobody on the Row could have written. She gets the key from the day's keeper. Then she does what the book has taught her and **checks before she carries**. She finds the **Jun 10, 2108** meeting entry and reads it against the countersigners' hands. There is no admission in it. The two neighbours who have since died signed it in their own writing, and one of them left a margin note about the rain. It takes time. She knows it's taking time.
- **The shore road,** eleven kilometres. There is no car. **Wren** insists on coming. The fog comes off the lake at dusk (real weather) and the road narrows to the white line. The pairs volume is in Ruth's coat beside the Kestrel Book.
- **Gullhaven, 23:40.** Net Loft Row is silent, with doors open and lamps on and people standing in the street not looking at one another. He was beaten on the slipway at about **19:40** by neighbours who believed a sealed, countersigned page. When he held up the Row's paper original, the book he had kept for them since May 2108, they said he'd have written it himself. Someone threw it off the slipway into the harbour. He is alive, just, on a bed in his house, with a doctor from up the coast.
- **Ruth reads the February copy aloud** under the lamp at his door, in front of witnesses, the book's way. The street hears its own dead neighbours' hands say nothing of the kind. Nobody speaks. The widow of the man who came back corrected sits down on the step.
- **Wren writes the entry** in the Kestrel Book. It is her first as a contributor: date, time, place, what was read, who heard it, and a line of her own: *We came four hours late.* Ruth countersigns. Two people from the Row ask to countersign as well, and Ruth lets them, and writes that they asked.
- **He dies in the night,** at about **03:50**, without waking.

## Ending
First light on the Row. Ruth tears a slip from the back of her order book and writes the dead man's name on it in square capitals, because there is an old man who sings the names of the dead, and this is where they go. She gives it to the book's courier to carry. It is the first death of the second war.

## Notes
- **README decision 4:** the ferryman is **unnamed on the page**. He is beaten on the evening of Mon Aug 19 and dies in the night. Ruth brings the Kestrel copy **four hours late** (19:40 to 23:40) and writes his name down for Old Songs. The prose never shows the name, even on the slip.
- **The slip's route (for Ch 7):** the courier hands it to **Alexei** on his lakeshore paper run (Book 6 Ch 18). He brings it to the farmhouse on Tue Aug 27.
- **Why the paired copy matters:** the accused man's own paper proves nothing to a mob, but a copy in another town, in other hands, checked when nobody was accused, does. That is the pairing method's point. Keep it implicit.
- **The dead count** starts here: **one**. Ch 5 and Ch 6 carry it on (see the handoff: five by Fri Aug 23, published Sat Aug 24; seven by Mon Aug 26).
- **No storm.** Chronos resumes corrective broadcasting on Wed Aug 21. The fog is real weather.
- **New:** the Nov 2107 arrests on the Row (real events); the paired copy made in Feb 2109; the original thrown into the harbour; Wren's first entry; the 03:50 time of death. All walk-ons stay unnamed.
