# Chapter 22: The Restorer

**POV:** Isaiah Okonkwo
**Date:** Mon Nov 18, 2109 (with the weekend's reading, Fri Nov 15 evening – Sun Nov 17)
**Setting:** The Sovereignty of Free Will: Isaiah's rooms; his paper's offices; the parlour of his memory palace; the Council broadcast in the evening
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The witness has to condemn his friend, and he does it honestly. The column is true. What frightens him is how much somebody wanted it. Plant the third Twist 3 clue and end with Isaiah understanding, without knowing who, that his testimony has been drafted into someone else's case.

## Beats
- **Fri Nov 15, evening in the Sovereignty.** Julian's disclosure arrives, and the SFW press runs it before Isaiah has read a page. It is **318 hours** of one man's life in a friend's company, timestamped to the nanosecond. Isaiah reads it as evidence, over three nights, and files it in the palace's study by date: the Lakes Talks; the garden in August; the Archive door in September; the access on Oct 18; the evenings; Julian's heart rate every time a door opens.
- **What the log shows** is what Isaiah was taught to see in Wing C: **R-2 turned outward**, performed by the one man who knows exactly what it did to him. Isaiah withheld the R-2 pairing from his own exposé in 2106, the one time he edited the truth. He doesn't edit now.
- **What he doesn't know,** and is careful not to claim: the operation's name, who built the rest of it, and what Chronos believed on Friday morning compared with Saturday. He knows only what the log shows and what Chronos has published: a night run in the Archive, a nation that now agrees with Veridica, and a key-holder at maximum. (He never claims more than he knows.)
- **The temptation.** He still has Principles One and Two, sent by Kira on Sep 30 and unpublished: his prints of her photographs, sewn into his coat lining (Ch 28). *"Let there be enemies outside them all"* would turn his column into a thesis. They aren't his to publish, and a column about one man's betrayal shouldn't be carried by a page about design. He leaves them where they are.
- **Mon Nov 18, 06:00–09:50, the palace parlour.** He writes the headline where he always does: ***THE RESTORER***. It is the Warning's own word, published last December ("reformers into its own restorers"), and he uses it deliberately. The column, in his editorial voice, every unnecessary word struck:
  - the man who refused KINDRED in a garden where Julian's log would keep it has now done it;
  - the core sentence: *"In one night, Veridica wrote its history into a hundred and fifty million people, and called it a restoration."*
  - Marcus's own line from the Reply (Feb 2109), quoted against him: *"Trust no restorer, including us."*
  - it ends, as his pieces do, on a question he admits he can't answer: *"He told us not to trust him. Why is it that we only believe a man when he stops meaning it?"*
  At the head of the parlour table Aunt Sarah watches him write, as she always has, and says nothing.
- **10:04, filed.** He expects what always happens in the SFW: a day's hold, a call from the managing editor, and three paragraphs cut for "tone" (as with the Doctrine column, and every column since).
- **10:51, printed.** It runs **uncut, within the hour**, on the front of the afternoon edition, with his byline larger than usual. There was no call. **In three years this has never happened.** The managing editor passes his desk and says, "Important work," the way people say *useful*. Isaiah hears belief in how people say words.
- **Afternoon.** It is reprinted across the Sovereignty and translated for Synthesis by evening. People he doesn't know stop him in the corridor to thank him. His asylum is still *conditional*, and for the first time he wonders what the condition is.

## Ending
19:30, in his rooms. The SFW Council's evening session is on the radio: a debate on the Council's **"posture towards Veridica"**. A member (unnamed) reads a sentence aloud to the chamber as proof: *"In one night, Veridica wrote its history into a hundred and fifty million people, and called it a restoration."* The member stops there. The question that followed it in the column is not read. The chamber applauds. Isaiah sits very still with his hands folded, and understands that he has become a witness for somebody's case, and doesn't know whose.

## Notes
- **(Twist 3 clue)** Printed uncut within the hour, which has never happened, and quoted in the Council by evening. Keep the attaché out of the room; the clue is the speed.
- **Isaiah does not know the codename RESTORATION.** It isn't in Julian's log, since Julian never heard it. His headline's echo of it is his own coinage, from the Warning. The echo pays off in Ch 28, when Martinez's folder carries the codename. Don't let him or the narration remark on it.
- **New details:** the 318-hour count, the quoted sentence, the column's closing question, and the times 10:04 and 10:51. Ch 25 and 28 may quote the sentence. The Council debate is "posture", not yet war (Ch 28–29 escalate it).
- **Manuscript:** he holds One and Two unpublished (Ch 12). No title, no marks, no Aunt Sarah link (SO·04 stays unspoken).
- **Voice:** editorial order, counts and files, formal courtesy, folded hands, no borrowed maxims. His pieces end on a question.
- **The Warning and page 30** were published in Dec 2108, so he may think of them. Don't quote Principle Six's unrevealed "disclose first" line.
- The SFW has no interfaces. The Council broadcast is radio or screen, not a feed.
