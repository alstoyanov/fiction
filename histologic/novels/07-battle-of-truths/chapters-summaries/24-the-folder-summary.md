# Chapter 24: The Folder

**POV:** Helena Kovač
**Date:** Mon Nov 25, 2109 (03:40 to about 21:00)
**Setting:** Kovač's flat in the capital (the table, the terminal, the hall cupboard with its boxes); her office behind the Commission's testimony intake; Marcus and Kira's flat in the evening (Kira is away at the farmhouse)
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
**Twist 2.** The proof was never in the record. It was a letter in a box, from the one witness nobody asked. Kovač keeps folders instead of acting; it is her flaw, and this once it is her gift. She has to admit that she made Marcus's mistake first.

## Beats
- **03:40, her flat.** She hasn't slept properly since Fri Nov 15. Every night she reads Julian's disclosure in order, as a Registrar reads testimony, using the transcript layer and stopping for what text can't carry. Tonight she reaches **Thu Aug 15, the Customs House garden.** Julian, gray, on the bench under the pear tree: ***"I don't understand how they used the key. I've only had it since—"*** Then a woman's voice, cool and courteous, on a point of protocol. She plays the half-sentence three times.
- **The memory.** *Since.* There was a letter. It came before the war, when the lake post still ran, in handwriting she hadn't seen since he was nineteen, and she never answered it. She pushes her glasses up her nose.
- **The hall cupboard.** She keeps her folders in boxes, because paper can't be adjusted. The first box is Ashford. The second is the Commission. **The third is family:** a few letters and a photograph from 2093. There she finds it, in an envelope with a Chronos lake-post frank. It is one sheet in Julian's sloping hand, dated at the top **20 January 2108**:
  > *"They've made me Senior Historian. I start in February. They've given me the old man's keys. I'm frightened of them, Aunt Helena."*
  It is signed *Julian*.
- **Why she never answered,** in her flat briefing voice, against herself. A nephew she hadn't seen in fifteen years wrote to say he was frightened. She had been dismissed from Ashford eleven months earlier. Her brother no longer said his name. She didn't know what to say, so she said nothing and filed it. **I kept a folder. That's what I did.**
- **Her own failure, made explicit and brief.** Last November she told Marcus, *"He has never travelled since he was given the key."* She knew *that* he'd been given it. She didn't give the date, because she'd put the letter out of her mind. When she looked at his career, she read the Archive's public roll, like everyone, and the roll said what she assumed: *Key-holder since 2104.* She hadn't really followed his career in those years. **She checked the record, not her own folder.** She writes that down first, before anything else, because it is true.
- **Morning, her office behind the intake.** It opens at 09:00, as always. She works by procedure: she lays it out on paper, in order, the way she briefs a committee.
  - **The letter:** 20 January 2108. *"They've given me"*: the keys were already his in January 2108, and he took office on Wed 1 February 2108.
  - **The Chronos Gap:** Tue Dec 13, 2107. **So the key wasn't Julian's then.**
  - **The public roll**, read again on the Commission's terminal: *MERA, A. Senior Historian emeritus from 2104. KOVAČ, J. Senior Historian. Key-holder since 2104.* It shows Mera appointed in **2088**. **Both of the 2104 entries are false.**
  - **The old man:** **Dr. Augustin Mera**, Senior Historian from 2088. That is twenty years. By the letter, he retired in **January 2108**.
  - **The Chronos public death record:** *died Sat Feb 18, 2108*, seventeen days after Julian took office. *Cremated the following day.*
- **The pattern.** She has signed the form a hundred times: *Remains processed according to standard protocols. No family viewing.* She certified ten false deaths herself, and in 2106 she sat under inquiry while Singh's body went to the fire within a day. She knows what a death that ends in paper and fire looks like. She writes *died* in quotation marks, and nothing after it. She doesn't know who Mera was. She doesn't guess.
- **Custody.** She puts the letter in a card folder, marks it with the date and her initials, and lets nobody copy the original. She takes it herself.

## Ending
**About 20:30, Marcus's flat.** He opens the door, thin, glasses pushed up, his lists folded on the table behind him. She stands by the door with her hands in her coat pockets and briefs him flatly, in order. Then she hands him the letter. He reads it twice. He says, almost to himself, *"Nikolai said, 'I know the key. I don't know the hand.'"* Then, holding a paper letter from the one witness nobody asked: ***"I checked the record."*** Kovač: ***"Yes. You did."***
