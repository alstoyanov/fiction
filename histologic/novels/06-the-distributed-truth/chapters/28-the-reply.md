# Chapter 28: The Reply

*Monday, January 28 – Monday, February 4, 2109. The resistance farmhouse, the border zone; the Justice Center, Veridica Capital*

---

*Incident log. January twenty-eighth, 2109. Oh-seven-fifty hours. Farmhouse kitchen, border zone. Items on table: six. Condition of items: copies. Originals: elsewhere, or none.*

Kira laid them out left to right, in page order, the way she laid out everything that had failed.

The Introduction, pages 2 to 4, gone soft at the corners from two months in her satchel. Principle Three, 13 to 17, *YP·02* at the foot. Five, 23 to 28, *JM·08*. Singh's photograph of page 30, the torn edge dark against the print. The strip, a finger wide, three numerals and three page numbers and no words. The Warning, in a young woman's slanting hand, *40* at the top right and *March 2066* at the foot.

Six items. She counted them again. Six.

The woman who had written two of them sat at the other end of the table with a cup held in both hands, watching her do it. Kira did not look up. She had found that she could be in a room with Elise Vance only if she treated her as a source, a thing with provenance, to be read in the order it had come in. It was not fair. Vance, who seemed to know exactly what she was doing, let her.

She had left gaps on the oilcloth between the items. Two page-widths between the Introduction and Three. One between Three and Five. The strip had told them where the missing pages began and ended and nothing else, and she had made the spaces the size of what she did not know.

*Layer one,* said her mouth, very quietly. *Key exchange. Asymmetric, lattice-based—*

She stopped it. She put both hands flat on the table and pressed until she could feel the grain through the cloth. At the stove Old Songs did not turn round. He eased the damper and closed the cracked door on it, and the fire changed its note.

It had not happened for a week. It had happened because she had seen, for a moment, a building with most of its walls missing, and every voice in the country telling her the kind thing was to let someone put them back.

She took a clean sheet and drew a line down the middle of it.

"It answers them," she said.

Elena, at the door with one boot on, stopped. Alexei looked up from the bread.

"Point for point. The same order. Their Introduction, ours. An answer to Three, an answer to Five. Where we don't have their page, we leave ours blank and say so." She touched the spaces on the oilcloth. "Every answer in a different hand. Nobody writes two. No name on the front."

"Who writes it?" said Elena.

"Whoever will."

Elena looked at her the way she looked at a road before she sent a truck down it. "That's not an answer," she said. "That's a method." And went out to count the barn.

At the end of the table Vance set her cup down. Her right hand began at once to tremble, and she laid it on the table edge until it stopped.

"It will want a title," she said. "People will give it one if you don't."

"Then they can. In their own hand."

Vance inclined her head. It was not agreement. It was the look of someone checking the first line of a proof.

---

The Introduction took three days.

They wrote it the way the Kestrel Book wrote an entry. One person wrote a sentence; a second countersigned it in the margin, or wrote an objection beside it; a better sentence went underneath, and the first was not crossed out. By Wednesday night the draft ran to four pages for three sentences.

Alexei proposed the first on Monday afternoon, standing, with his mouth full: *This reply is written by as many hands as will write in it.* Elena countersigned it without a word. Dmitri, in from the checkpoint road at dusk, read it twice and wrote under her initials, in his square hand: *Accurate.*

The second was Kira's. She wrote it at two in the morning, alone with the stove, turning the Introduction's own words over like a component she was reverse-engineering, looking for the one part that, reversed, would make the rest run the other way. *Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided by a public. It must be decided for one.* She changed a preposition and a verb. *Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided for a public. It has to be checked by one.* In the morning there were initials beside it in pencil, *M.P.*, and a short rising stroke.

The third took all of Tuesday. Every version sounded like a Ministry leaflet or a man shouting in a square. The one that survived was the plainest: *and the public must be allowed to disagree.* Four people countersigned it. Nobody much liked it.

On Wednesday evening Kira gave the draft to Vance.

She had not meant to. Then she had understood that leaving her out was only another way of making her the centre.

Vance read it under the lamp, margins first, the object before the words. At the third sentence she took up a pencil and held it until her hand was still.

"May I?"

"In the margin. In your own hand."

Vance drew a light line under one word and wrote beside it, in an old woman's version of the hand on the Warning, smaller, the slant the same: *able.*

"'Allowed' is a word for whoever holds the keys," she said gently, as if correcting a student she liked. "If the public is allowed to disagree, somebody allows it. Somebody can stop. You want the other word."

Kira looked at it for a long time. It was the Introduction's verb. *The public must be allowed to believe it decided.* She had kept it without seeing it, because she had been reversing the original, and she had not noticed that it was the part that held the rest together. She wrote *able* above *allowed* and countersigned Vance's line, and wrote the time beside it, because the book kept its arguments.

Isaiah's page came on Thursday, by a Synthesis courier with a passport and no interest in what he carried. He had sent the draft back on paper, which was his way of saying he had read it properly: one sheet, the whole thing copied out in his upright hand and gone through with a ruled line.

He had struck out eleven words and added none. She counted them twice. *We sincerely believe*, three. *At this hour of our history*, six. *Truly*, one. *Freely*, one. At the foot: *The rest holds. Your hour is still mine.*

She read what was left.

*This reply is written by as many hands as will write in it. Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided for a public. It has to be checked by one, and the public must be able to disagree.*

Three sentences, nine hands in the margins. She could not have said who had written it, which was correct.

---

The lines came in over the week, in the order the roads allowed.

Kira wrote hers on Monday night, before she could be tempted to write two. To Three: the backups that were never independent, the true source, the record restored from above whenever anyone below it tried to change it. She knew that principle better than anyone alive except the man who had used it on her. She did not write about that. She wrote the procedure.

*Keep copies no one can recall: on paper, in many houses.*

She found she had nothing to add, which was how a procedure ought to feel.

Sokolov's came on Tuesday. Dmitri brought it back from the lakeshore checkpoint in the inside pocket of his coat and laid it in front of Kira without comment: a sheet of Border Security notepaper, the crest cut off along a very straight line, folded once.

*A border is where two peoples compare notes, not where they are measured.*

"She said nothing?"

"She said, 'Traffic normal.'" Dmitri hung his coat on its nail. "Then she handed it to me. In that order."

Marcus's came from the capital the same evening, by one of Elena's couriers, on a page torn from the back of his lists. He had not been out to the farmhouse since the fourteenth. At 0.58, one night at an outlaw's table would put him over the line, and on the first night he had written to her, in pencil, *I will not give them the two points.* She kept that note. She kept this one beside it.

To Six. The restorer's authority. The Commission that was the principle in person, and the two of them on its benches.

*Trust no restorer, including us.*

The last two words were pressed harder than the rest. She could feel them with her thumb through the back of the sheet.

Julian's came on Wednesday night, after the fog. Alexei brought it over the Drowned Causeway at two in the morning, wet to the knees, grinning the way he grinned when a crossing had gone clean. Julian had met him on the marsh road and given him an envelope and said, Alexei reported, "Nine minutes late. I didn't mind." Inside was a card, and on the card, in ink:

*For Chronos: Record less. Remember more.*

Kira held it under the lamp. It was not a practised hand. The letters were careful, a little too far apart, the hand of a man doing something he had not done in years. And the capitals sloped: the *R*, the *M*, the *C*, all leaning forward together, eager, as if each letter wanted to reach the next word first.

For one beat she saw another hand, from another year, with the same forward lean in its capitals, in a family that had split along a lake.

She put the card down. *Kovač, J. Capitals slope.* She filed it where she kept the things about Adrian she did not need to look at, and closed the drawer on it.

Ruth's came on Thursday with Wren's, in one envelope from Kestrel Point, countersigned on the back by the courier and by a woman at the ferry office Kira had never met. Wren's was on school exercise paper with the margin ruled in red, in capitals, and then again underneath in ordinary writing, as if capitals might not count: *Teach children to check, not to believe. Wren Calloway, 14. My mother says it is too short. I say it is the right length.* Beside it, in Ruth's hand: *It is the right length. R.C.*

Ruth's own was an answer to the Warning. Kira read it twice and understood that it was also a description of every page of the Kestrel Book, and of its red margins, and of Emmet Varley's name still standing in it.

*Where we disagree, write both down, each in our own hand.*

The last was Alexei's. He wrote it on Thursday night in the chair nearest the stove, fast, and pushed it across before he could change his mind. "To itself."

*If this ever becomes one record, burn it and start again.*

Dmitri, reading upside down, said, "Lyosha."

"It's a warning, Dima. Not a plan." Alexei grinned. "I've wanted to burn something since November. This way I do it in writing and nobody has to put out the barn."

Dmitri reached across and countersigned it. *Accurate.*

---

By Friday morning the New Foundation stood in a plain account book like the one Kira had kept since the border zone, dark boards, ruled pages. She numbered the lines in the margin, because someone had to and it was the kind of work she was for.

*1. The Introduction. 2. To One. 3. To Two. 4. To Three. 5. To Four. 6. To Five. 7. To Six. 8. For Chronos. 9. For the children. 10. To the Warning. 11. To itself.*

Two, three and five were blank. Each had a page to itself, and at the top of each, in a different hand, the same sentence: *We will answer these when we know what they said.* Elena wrote it on the first, Nikolai on the second, Dmitri on the third, in capitals, as if it were an order.

Nikolai had wanted the blanks left out: three empty pages would be read as fear. Kira had wanted them in. To answer only the pages they had seen was to reply to a smaller document than the one that existed. It was Vance, asked for once, who settled it. "Leave them. It is the only honest thing in either document that admits what it doesn't know." Nobody asked her what she knew about those pages, because nobody wanted her to have to refuse.

The sheets from outside were fixed in with paper tape along one edge: the notepaper with its crest cut off, the page from Marcus's lists, Julian's card, Wren's exercise paper, Ruth's, Isaiah's struck draft facing the finished Introduction. In every copy made after this one, every line would be in the copier's hand, and that was right too. This book was not the original. It was only the first.

After the eleventh line came forty blank pages, ruled and empty. At the top of the first Maya, out from the capital with the courier schedules, wrote the heading in her quick technician's hand, because she had been the one to say it:

*Anyone may sign. There is no last page.*

Kira had meant it as an answer. On the fourth of January, in a ward in Chronos, Dmitri had asked Vance whose copy X was, and she had said that names would make them a court, and a court was one record. Kira had thought about it for nearly four weeks. A list that could be closed was a court. It could be read out, checked off and used. A list that could not be closed was something else. Anyone could sign it; nobody could finish it; nobody could say who was not on it yet.

The first names came that morning. Elena. Alexei, very large. Dmitri. Nikolai, small, with the date. Maya.

Vance signed at noon. She turned past the first page, past the names already there, to the second, which was empty, and held the pen until her hand was still, and wrote her name in the middle of the page. Not at the top. In the middle, with white space above and below, where anyone who came after would have to sign around her.

*Elise Vance.* The slant of the Warning.

She did not say why. She put both hands round her cup and looked out at the yard, and Kira, who had watched her do it, wrote nothing about it anywhere.

---

Old Songs wrote no answer.

Alexei had asked him on Tuesday, and he had hummed one falling phrase, the Wing C *no*, and gone back to the stove.

On Friday afternoon, while the others loaded the first bundle of copies into Elena's truck, Kira came back for her satchel and found him at the table with the book open at the back. He had turned past the eleven lines and the forty blank pages to the inside of the back board, the stiff gray card where nothing was ruled, and he was writing in pencil, slowly, the way he had once written a name on a sheet of paper in November. She stood in the doorway and did not come in.

Four lines. She knew them. She had sung the third voice under them on the twenty-first, in this kitchen, off-key, and had not slept that night for the sound of it.

*One hand writes and one hand checks,*
*one hand holds the light;*
*one voice fails and one voice mends,*
*and many make it right.*

Under them he drew a line of short strokes, each a little higher than the last, and a final one that lifted at the end: the same strokes she had drawn under the ferry verse in her own first book two Januaries ago, so that whoever read it would know that it went up.

He did not sign it. He closed the book and laid his hand flat on the cover, and went back to the stove humming, and the tune lifted at the end like a question and did not come down.

---

*February first, 2109. Publication: none. Copies: uncounted.*

It was published nowhere. That was the point, and it was also simply what happened.

The first copies went out that afternoon in Elena's truck and Alexei's coat, to the towns that had read the confession on the twenty-first: Kestrel Point first, then the other Veridican towns, then over the causeway to the six on the other shore. In each town a keeper copied it by hand into two books and sent one on, and the next town did the same. By Saturday night it had gone along the pairing lines faster than any courier could have carried it, because it was not being carried. It was being copied. On Monday Nikolai reported that two Chronos-shore towns had copied it into their logs, which meant it stood in the permanent record of a nation that erased nothing, as something its citizens had chosen to see.

Kira began a tally on Friday night. By Saturday she had forty-one copies she could name. By Sunday she was counting towns instead. On Monday morning, in the car to the capital, she took the tally out and found she had not added a line to it since Saturday.

She had not decided to stop. She had simply stopped, and she sat with the column of numbers on her knee and found that it did not frighten her.

---

The Judge's chamber was as it had been on the fourteenth.

The benches rising towards the back, full. The lectern. Minister Chen Zhao in the presiding chair, a glass of tea steaming at his elbow. Behind the lectern, the contamination panel, a band of white light across the curved wall, with its one fine red line at 0.60 and the three names from the fourteenth still standing in a column.

*PELL, I. 0.11. WEI, T. 0.03. CHEN, M. 0.58.*

Nine cameras in the chamber, and one over each of the four doors. Wei at the technical bench, hands folded, pleasant. Marcus on the front public bench, because a speaker under review did not sit with the Commission, his glasses pushed up on his forehead. He was not looking at the panel.

At ten o'clock the determination came up, and the clerk read it into the record in the careful voice of a woman who knows every word will be quoted.

The Judge had weighed the question put to it on the fourteenth of January: whether keeping an unregistered journal should be classed as relevant to contamination. It had set the testimony of registered and unregistered books against each other across the months of the storms, and found that they agreed in a proportion it could not distinguish from the agreement of registered books alone.

*Contamination relevance of keeping an unregistered journal: 0.41. Below threshold.*

There was a sound in the chamber. Not a cheer. Something lower, a room full of people letting out a breath they had not agreed to hold.

The Judge could not, on the evidence, class a large and concordant part of the nation's testimony as contaminated. The National Concordance of Testimony would proceed on a voluntary basis. Journals might be entered at their keepers' request. None was required to be.

*Voluntary.* Kira wrote the word down. She knew what it meant. On Monday there would be queues outside the Concordance offices in every district, and many people would stand in them, because they were tired and frightened and a sealed master copy sounded like safety. The restore path from the master would still exist, for every page that went in. The index would still be a list of everyone who remembered, only shorter.

And nobody who did not want to be on it had to be.

She had wanted it gone. She made herself take it for exactly what it was: more than they had had on the fourteenth, and less than enough.

Chen Zhao thanked The Judge, the clerk and the chamber, warmly, in two short sentences, and closed the session.

---

As the chamber stood, Wei took a small book from the inside pocket of his jacket.

Kira saw it because she was looking at him, as she had been on and off for two years. A pocket book with a dark cover, worn at the corners. She had never seen him carry it. She had never seen him write in anything but a tablet.

He opened it on his knee and wrote a single line with his right hand, without hurry, without looking round. Then he closed it, put it away, buttoned his jacket over it, and turned to say something pleasant to the clerk.

*Wei, T. February fourth, 2109. Ten-fourteen. Technical bench. One line, right hand, in a small book. Dark cover. Not seen before. Content: unknown.*

She filed it. She did not know where it went.

---

The panel had not cleared.

The determination had gone, but the column of names was still there, and under it a line in the small type the panel used for notices: *Speaker indices of 14 January recomputed under revised classification.*

She stopped, half out of her seat.

*PELL, I.* The number flickered and settled. *0.12.*

*WEI, T. 0.03.* It did not move.

*CHEN, M.*

The number went out. For a moment there was nothing beside his name. Then the digits came back one at a time, the way the panel had built every number on the fourteenth while she refused to look at it.

*0.44.*

She looked at it now, for as long as she would have looked at any number on any screen, and then a little longer, because this one she was allowed.

He had come up the aisle while she watched, and stood at the end of the commissioners' bench with his coat over his arm, his head on one side, the way he looked at an entry he was checking. Around them the chamber emptied.

Kira came along the bench and stood beside him.

"I'd have liked it lower," she said.

Marcus did not take his eyes off the panel.

"I'd have liked it checkable," he said.
