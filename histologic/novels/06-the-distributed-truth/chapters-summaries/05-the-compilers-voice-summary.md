# Chapter 5: The Compiler's Voice

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Fri Nov 9, 2108
**Setting:** The resistance farmhouse in the border zone: the kitchen with the cracked stove door, and the back room with its stove; memory of the Historical Archive in 2063
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The manuscript comes back, this time in a voice Old Songs loved. It is the book's quietest chapter and one of its most important. He recognises a person not by what she says but by how her argument turns. Plant Twist 3 twice, and make both plants small.

## Beats
- **Old Songs at the stove,** humming the Lakeshore Air, every phrase lifting like a question. He is seventy now, and still officially dead by choice.
- **Alexei** arrives in the afternoon with the two songbooks, green and blue. Tanaka handed them over that morning on the Veridican side of the lakeshore checkpoint (she crosses legally, under her own name). Her note: *From a patient, for Mikhail. She asked that they go home.* Alexei is teasing and warm: "You've got an admirer, Misha. In Chronos. I'd be worried."
- **The books** are genuine old lakeshore collections. The green one is a school songbook. Old Songs knows half its tunes from his grandmother and hums two of them. The blue one's **back endpaper has been lifted and re-glued**. He feels a flatness under it that isn't board.
- **The Introduction** is folded in thirds under the endpaper: **three typed pages numbered 2, 3 and 4.** An archivist reads the object before the words:
  - **no copy numeral** in the lower right corner, where page 30 had *XI*;
  - **no mark** at the end;
  - a small bite out of the top of **every lowercase *e***, the same nick he stared at on the photograph of page 30.
- **He reads it twice.** Quote only the canonical lines the scene needs: *"This document assembles principles from seventeen contributors…"*, then *"…Truth, we have learned, cannot be decided by a public. It must be decided for one, and the public must be allowed to believe it decided."* Seventeen contributors. It is a design, and somebody **assembled** it.
- **He stops humming,** in the middle of a phrase, where the tune begins to lift. Alexei goes still: in Wing C that silence meant *they're watching*.
- **It is not the words.** It is how the argument turns. Each paragraph grants the objection first and then turns it, the way a young woman at the Archive won every Thursday argument by **conceding the first point**. "Yes, rhyme is a prayer. And a prayer is a compression algorithm."
- **The memory, 2063,** matching Book 5 Ch 12 exactly: the reading room smelling of dust, floor polish and Kerr's eleven o'clock apple peeled in one curl; the green-shaded lamp; *Periodicals, Governance, 114*; a theorist from the university's systems department with ink on her fingers, who read the gray journal "the way you read something you mean to build on", and argued with him about poetry every Thursday for two years, in whispers, while Kerr pretended not to hear. He had never once wondered what she wanted with it.
- **He says one word aloud: "Elise."** Alexei asks who she is. Old Songs: "Someone I argued with on Thursdays." Nothing more.
- **Evening.** Alexei calls Tanaka on the link and asks where the books came from. Her answer, precise and immovable: **"A patient."** She is sorry, but that is all she can say. Confidentiality.
- **The smell.** Handling the pages, Old Songs notices they don't smell of old paper. They smell faintly clean, sharp and cold, like a hospital. Alexei sniffs: "All hospitals smell like that." Old Songs isn't sure that's true, and has no way of knowing. Only Tanaka would know that smell, and she isn't there.
- **Elena** comes in, counts the pages left to right, and says the pages go to Kira. Old Songs asks for one night with them. She gives it to him.

## Ending
Late, alone at the kitchen table, with the stove glowing through its crack, Old Songs takes a pencil and a sheet of paper. He has kept everything he loved in tunes for forty years, because a thing written down can be found. He has never written a loved name on paper. He writes it now, slowly, in an old archivist's hand: ***Elise Vance.*** Then he sits and looks at it for a long time.

## Notes
- **Canon wording:** `00-FOUNDATION-MANUSCRIPT.md` §4.2. Pages 2–4, no mark, **no numeral** (the Compiler's own copy), typed with the chipped *e*. Don't invent further quoted manuscript text. Paraphrase the rhetoric ("grants the objection first").
- **Twist 3 clues:** "a patient" (Tanaka's confidentiality), and the **disinfectant** smell. Nobody at the farmhouse can identify it. Keep both light.
- **What Old Songs knows:** that Elise compiled this document, and that it had seventeen contributors. **He does not know she is alive, or where she is.** He doesn't connect "the patient" to her, and doesn't let himself.
- **The title *The Foundation of Certainty* is not known to anyone in Book 6.** The title page is a Book 7 reveal (Manuscript §4.1, §5), and "Compiler" is the characters' own word for her.
- **Voice rules:** he hums; he says "Elise" and one short sentence; no speech (that is Ch 22); no riddle (that is Ch 27). His dry wit may appear once.
