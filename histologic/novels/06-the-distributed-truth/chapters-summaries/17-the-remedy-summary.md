# Chapter 17: The Remedy

**POV:** Marcus Chen
**Date:** Tue Dec 18, 2108
**Setting:** The Commission's offices in the capital (the commissioners' room, with the First Minister's broadcast on the wall screen); the session room; Marcus's flat, late
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Part Three opens with the embrace. Within a day the government has confessed, praised the people who exposed it, and offered them exactly what they asked for. Marcus's unease has a small reason: the speech was too well made. Plant Twist 2 through polish and pleasure. Recall the December key-showing as proof of Julian's good faith; it is a Twist 4 plant that must read as nothing.

## Beats
- **Opening fact entry, 09:10:** *Dec 18, 2108. Commission offices. First morning without a storm since May 2. Chronos determination (Dec 17, 23:40): 0.88. Border Security stand-down (Dec 17, 14:05). Status of the war:* He leaves it blank.
- **The morning.** The publication is everywhere: on the Justice Center steps, in the public archive, and in journals copied overnight. Julian's MERIDIAN orders sit in his public log. Colleagues who wouldn't meet Marcus's eye in November shake his hand. He keeps waiting for an arrest, and none comes.
- **12:00. Maitland confesses first**, on every screen. He is calm and sounds sorry. KINDRED was "an error of judgement, now ended", and he takes it on himself: "I authorised it. I was wrong." He names no one else and pays tribute to **"our commissioners' courage"**. Then he announces the **National Concordance of Testimony**. Every citizen's journal may be registered, indexed and weighed by The Judge as testimony. The Commission will run it, and a Concordance Act goes before The Judge in January. He closes with the Warning, published less than a day earlier: *"Build by many hands."* The room applauds. Kira doesn't.
- **What Marcus notices.** The speech has numbered provisions, a crest, a budget figure and a date. It borrows the Commission's own vocabulary. It was written, costed and rehearsed in **about a day**, twenty-two hours from publication at 14:00 to noon today, by people who were fighting a war yesterday. Ministry drafting doesn't go that fast. His POV loses a word (voice rule 3): the word for something that has been ready a long time. There's a smooth place where it should be.
- **The capital cheers.** In the street people hold their journals up to the broadcast screens, as if to be counted.
- **16:00, the session room.** **Chen Zhao** presides, delighted. To Marcus: *"You have restored more than a record, cousin. You've restored a country's faith in its own voice."* That is his one "restore". Since Ch 13 Marcus has known that the Commission is page 30 in person, and he says nothing. Then, pleasantly: *"A confession without a remedy is only noise."*
- **Wei presents the architecture**, and it is beautiful. Each registered page is scanned, sealed and cross-referenced with every page that mentions the same day. Disagreements are flagged "Kestrel-style", with a nod to the lakeshore, and keepers are protected. *"No journal will ever be lost to a fire or a flood again. If a book burns, we restore it."* He is openly pleased, the way a man is when something he admires finally happens, and he thanks Marcus for "making it possible". One beat of excess: his look at Marcus on *restore* lasts a fraction too long.
- **The key, recalled.** Maitland said Veridica "no longer seeks any other nation's keys". Marcus remembers **Thu Dec 13**, the last day of round 3 and the Gap's first anniversary. In the garden at Saltmarsh Crossing, on **the bench under the bare pear tree**, in fog, where Julian's log recorded every second, Julian opened the senior reconciliation key unasked: *"So you can see there is nothing behind it."* He made a **harmless test signature**, countersigning a one-line test entry (*Saltmarsh Crossing, garden, 13 Dec 2108*), and handed Marcus the signed slip. Marcus didn't touch the key. Because of the date, Julian mentioned that his log for that night a year ago shows him **asleep** through the seventeen minutes: "Like everyone. I've never slept so well." Marcus laughed. On **Fri Dec 14** he gave the slip to Nikolai, who keeps copies. He still thinks it was the most honest thing anyone did at the talks.
- **19:00.** The Commission's **endorsement of the Concordance** is circulated for signature. Chen Zhao has signed first, and others under him.

## Ending
The flat, 22:30. The endorsement lies beside his lists, with the pen on it. He wants to sign: it would make the journals safe and the war's end law. Under UNCHECKED he writes: *Maitland's speech. Drafted: when?* Kira, reading over his shoulder, puts her hand flat on the page. "Don't sign anything tonight."

## Notes
- **(Twist 2 clues)** Three of them:

  - Wei is pleased (after Ch 15's "Good."). Show pleasure, never a gloat. No interior.
  - "If a book burns, we restore it" pays off after Ch 18 and in Ch 24.
- **(Twist 4 plant)** The key-showing: **Thu Dec 13**, on the bench, in fog. It is a test signature on a one-line entry, and the slip went to Nikolai on **Fri Dec 14**. The *asleep* remark is a joke about the date. Marcus laughs, and it must read as nothing. Don't show the key or the signature in close-up. Ch 30 reads its "habits". Ch 29 assumes the bench.
- **Chen Zhao** is not suspected personally. His aside is a hindsight echo only. **Don't quote** the unrevealed Principle Six line about disclosing first (Manuscript §4.8).
- **Maitland** quotes only the published Warning. No one says the manuscript's title (README decision 15).
