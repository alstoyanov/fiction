# Chapter 25: 0.58

**POV:** Marcus Chen
**Date:** Mon Jan 14, 2109
**Setting:** The Justice Center, Veridica Capital: The Judge's chamber on the upper floor (above the Commission's great hall), for a public session on the Concordance Act
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Part Four opens with a public defeat. Marcus argues against the most popular reform in Veridica's history while The Judge weighs him live, on a panel the whole chamber can see. "Don't give it a center" starts to cost.

## Beats
- **The opening entry.** Marcus pauses on the steps, as he did two years ago to the day (Book 5 Ch 31, Jan 14, 2107): *January fourteenth, 2109. Oh-nine-fifty hours. Justice Center, main steps. Involved parties: Chen, M. Purpose: public session, Concordance Act. Status of subject:* He leaves the status blank. Five days have passed since Ch 24's "Then we don't give it a center."
- **The chamber.** It is above the great hall, with full public benches, the commissioners' bench (Kira), a lectern, and behind it **the contamination panel** (README decision 10): each speaker's live index beside their name. Minister Chen Zhao presides from the chair: warm, with a glass of tea steaming at his elbow. Dmitri sits on the highest public bench, the seat "with a view of every door", as he did in Book 5.
- **The question** before The Judge: should unregistered journals be classed as "unverified records", which would make keeping one relevant to contamination? Three speakers.
- **Pell** (panel: **0.11**) speaks for the League of the Record: burn them all and restore the pure record. He reuses his Book 5 line, *"A nation of memories is a nation of liars."* His fear is real.
- **Wei** (panel: **0.03**) speaks for the government. He is lucid and kind, and the architecture is beautiful: register every journal "for its own protection", index it and weigh it as testimony. His line has two meanings: *"Nothing anyone has written down should ever be lost again. We are only offering to keep it for them."* The benches applaud. Marcus knows since Ch 24 that these drafts predate the publication they answer. He does **not** put Maya's evidence before The Judge: the metadata could be called forged, it would expose Maya, and the argument has to stand on its own.
- **Marcus** (panel: **0.20** when he starts). He argues for journals kept free, on paper, unindexed, and checked by the people who wrote them. He quotes only what is already public. Principle Three: a record held in one place *"may be restored from the true source."* The Warning: the design *"will make … reformers into its own restorers."* *"An index of every journal is a list of everyone who remembers. And whatever can be restored can be edited."*
- **He enters his own exposure on the record, one fact at a time,** because hiding it to stay safe is exactly the Concordance's logic. The panel moves after each statement:
  - "I keep a journal. It is not registered." **0.27**
  - "Some of its contributors I have never met." **0.34**
  - "Some of them are officially dead." **0.41**
  - "Some live on the other shore." **0.49**
  - "It has been copied by hand into two other houses. I will not tell this chamber which." **0.53**
  - "I will not register it. I ask you not to make anyone else." **0.58**
- **He loses the room.** Someone laughs, and a woman on the benches says *"Then what are you afraid of?"* Marcus reaches for a word in his closing sentence and it is gone, a smooth place (his POV holes, voice rule 3). He finishes anyway, and does not soften anything. He does not run.
- **Chen Zhao** thanks all three speakers "for restoring our confidence in open argument" (his one use of the word). The Judge will determine the matter on **Mon Feb 4**, after receiving testimony.
- Kira does not look at the panel. She looks at Marcus. At the back, Dmitri is counting the doors.

## Ending
The panel stops at **0.58**: two points below custody, and the number Marcus was once at before someone adjusted it (Book 4). He looks across the chamber. Wei is looking at the panel too, with polite interest.
