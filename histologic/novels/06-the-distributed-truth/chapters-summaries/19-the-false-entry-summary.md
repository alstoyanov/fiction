# Chapter 19: The False Entry

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Thu Dec 27, 2108
**Setting:** Kestrel Point: the burned chandlery on Harbour Street; the ferry office, closed for the winter except for freight; the church hall, where the book now meets
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
A journal that can be burned can also be poisoned. The Kestrel Book finds a liar inside itself and has to decide what a book of many hands does with one false hand. The answer it reaches is its own. Then the liar says who paid him, and Ruth sees that both sides want her book, and neither wants it free.

## Beats
- **Morning, Harbour Street.** The chandlery is a black shell that smells of wet char. Ruth stands where the reading-room table was. The book is in her coat, where it has been since Monday, and she hasn't let it out of her hands since. Wren, beside her, has a cough and a bandaged wrist from the window frame, and is furious, which Ruth takes for a good sign.
- **Ruth's decision.** If the book can burn, the town has to know exactly what is in it. So the seventeen contributors will read it aloud, **every entry, with every signer present**, and check that each countersignature is a hand its owner recognises. She sets it for the afternoon, in the church hall.
- **The reading.** It takes three hours. Most entries hold: the doubled pier (the Prologue), the Chronos town charter that half the town "remembers" (Ch 4), the night the war stopped (Ch 16). Disagreements are already written beside them in the other person's hand, as the book has always done.
- **The false entries.** Emmet Varley's hold only on paper. There are six, dated from **Sat Dec 8** onwards. The first reads: *a Chronos boat tied up at the old pier at 02:00, three men ashore.* It was witnessed at a pier Veridica says has been gone since 2089. Each one is countersigned by two neighbours.
- **How Ruth knows.** In August the ferry office ran the evacuation boats for the lower end of Harbour Street when the storms were at their worst. Ruth wrote the manifests herself. **The two countersigners left on those boats in August and haven't come back.** They couldn't have signed anything in December. Nobody else ever wrote a disagreement beside those entries, because nobody else was there at 02:00. That was the point.
- **Varley,** pleasant and embarrassed, admits it within a minute: he signed their names himself, in two different hands, practising at his kitchen table. He is a contributor of long standing and a decent neighbour. The hall doesn't know what to do with him.
- **The argument.** It is a book of many hands, so it argues in many voices.
  - Exclusion: tear out his pages and strike his name. One old man: "Burn them. That's what the League would do," and he realises what he has said.
  - Probation: he stays, his entries stay, and every future entry of his needs a third signature.
  - **Wren** settles it without meaning to: "If you tear them out, nobody will know he did it. Mark them." The book decides by show of hands and records the vote in the book. Varley's six entries stay **marked in the margin in red**, in Ruth's hand, with *FALSE: countersignatures forged, confessed Dec 27, 2108*. Varley countersigns the red himself.
- **Then Varley confesses the rest,** because being forgiven undoes him. A pleasant man came to his door in the **first week of December**, **Thu Dec 6**, and paid him to write a few "vivid" entries, "to show that unregistered books can't be trusted". At first the man said he was from Ministry *public outreach*, surveying the town books. He came back on **Thu Dec 20**, two days after the First Minister's speech, and paid him again. It was the same man with a new card: *National Concordance of Testimony, Outreach*. Varley still has both cards. The man told him the book would be registered soon anyway, and it would be "safer for everyone" once it was.
- Ruth pins both cards inside the back cover and writes the confession into the book, with the date, and three signatures beside it.

## Ending
Walking home past the chandlery with the book against her ribs, Ruth sees it plainly. The League wanted her book **burned**. The Concordance wanted it **registered**, and had paid a man to make it look like it needed to be. Neither of them wanted it *free*. She writes that down too, at the kitchen table, and asks Wren to countersign.

## Notes
- **(Twist 2 clue)** Varley's outreach officer. The **same man, before and after the announcement**, with a new card: the first payment (Dec 6) comes twelve days before the Concordance was announced. Ruth notices the new card and reads it as the Ministry reorganising. She does **not** notice the date problem. Kira does, from Ruth's written account, in Ch 24.
- **Varley's earlier entries are genuine** (e.g. his vivid Nov 4 storm entry in Ch 4). The man picked him *because* he was vivid. The false ones start on Dec 8.
- **The outreach man is unnamed.** The cards carry office titles, not a name. Don't give him one.
- **The evacuated neighbours** (August 2108, Harbour Street) are unnamed. They are a new detail: the ferry office ran the evacuation boats and Ruth kept the manifests.
- **Contributor count stays at seventeen.** Varley remains, on probation.
- **Red-margin marking** comes out of the book's existing disagreement rule (Ch 4). It is new here, and Ch 26–28 may reuse it.
- **Storm language:** there are no storms now. The fire and the fog are physical. Keep them that way.
- **Wren** is fourteen and practical. The line she writes in the Reply (Ch 28) grows out of moments like this one.
