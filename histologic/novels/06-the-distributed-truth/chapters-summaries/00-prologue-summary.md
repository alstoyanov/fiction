# Prologue: The Doubled Pier

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Tue Oct 30, 2108, night
**Setting:** Kestrel Point, a lakeshore town in the claimed strip, on the Veridican shore: Harbour Street, the end of the old pier, a neighbour's kitchen, the lakeshore road
**Length:** ~3,200 words

## Purpose
Open the book on the war's cost at human scale, before any politics. A mother, a daughter, a pier that both exists and doesn't, and a handwritten book that is the only thing in town that agrees with itself. Close on the fact that nobody in the capital will say aloud: some of the storms are ours.

## Beats
- **Kestrel Point at the end of the ferry season.** The last sailing is tomorrow. Ruth closes the ferry office at six, as she has for nineteen years, and pins the summer timetable back up in the window out of habit. Fog comes in off the lake at dusk (real weather). She has the **Kestrel Book** in the inside pocket of her coat, because the keeper takes it home at night.
- **21:40: the storm alert,** the third since Sunday. Wren, fourteen, is not home. She was at a friend's house further down Harbour Street and should have left at half past nine.
- **Onset, described only as sensation.** A pressure behind the eyes. Then a certainty with no seam in it: the **old pier** at the end of Harbour Street has always been there, with its lamp posts and its tarred boards (the Chronos history). And at exactly the same time Ruth *remembers* it being demolished in **2089**, when she was twenty-seven and stood in the crowd watching the cranes (the Veridican history). Both are memories. Both are hers. Neither gives way.
- **Wren in the fog,** walking with her hands in her pockets towards the end of the street, where the stone quay stops and there is a drop into black water. She is walking out onto boards her mind can see. Ruth runs. The street flickers between two harbours. Ruth holds on to one dull, procedural fact to keep her feet moving: the ferry office's closing time, the till total, anything boring.
- **The catch.** Ruth gets a fist in the back of Wren's coat at the very edge of the drop. Wren's front foot is already over the water. They sit down hard on the wet stone. Wren says, perfectly calmly, "I was nearly at the lamp." There is no lamp.
- **The kitchen.** The nearest lit house belongs to the retired ferry engineer and his wife, who live at the end of Harbour Street (both unnamed). Tea. Wren shaking now it is over. The storm fades over about twenty minutes. Afterwards Ruth still knows both piers. She expects to know both for the rest of her life.
- **The Kestrel Book.** Ruth opens it on the oilcloth. It was begun in **April 2108** from a hand copy of Kira Osman's method, carried to Kestrel Point by a courier nobody knew. It has **seventeen contributors**. Show the rules in action: Ruth writes the entry in her own hand, with date, time, place, what happened and who saw it, and the engineer and his wife countersign in theirs. The wife disagrees about the time Wren reached the edge (she says 21:52, Ruth says 21:50), so she writes her own time in the margin, beside Ruth's. Neither is crossed out.
- **Wren reads the entry upside down** and asks why they wrote the argument in. Ruth: "Because we both saw it." (Make this the reader's first lesson in how the book works.)
- In a town where every broadcast contradicts the last, the book is the only thing that agrees with itself, *because* it keeps its disagreements.

## Ending
Walking home with Wren along the **lakeshore road**, Ruth passes a **gray Border Security van** parked at the end of the road with its engine running and its lights off. There is a **mast folded flat on its roof**. A man in the cab is drinking something from a flask and doesn't look at them. Ruth knows what a Border Security van looks like. She has sold its crews ferry tickets. She understands, without wanting to, that the storm came from *our* side.

## Notes
- **The pier:** Veridica's record says it was demolished in 2089; Chronos's says it stands. Physically there is only the drop and the water. Ruth was born in 2062, so she was 27 in 2089.
- **Storm rules:** onset only as sensation. The flicker is perception, and the danger is Wren acting on what she perceives. Fog is weather. Nothing physical changes.
- **The 21:40 alert time** is the summer ferry's last departure. Ch 4 pays this off: the storms keep to the ferry timetable. Don't make that link in the Prologue.
- **The van** is Sokolov's programme (transmitter units, Book 4). Ruth doesn't know Sokolov's name. The van leaves in Ch 16 with its mast down.
- **The book is in Ruth's coat.** She keeps this habit all through the book (Ch 18: the book is in her coat when the chandlery burns).
- Seventeen contributors, as README decision 2 requires. Wren is not a contributor, and she has not written in the book yet.
- The neighbours are deliberately unnamed.
