# Chapter 21: Mrs. Kerr

**POV:** Dr. Yuki Tanaka
**Date:** Thu Jan 3, 2109 (04:00 to evening)
**Setting:** The teaching hospital in the Chronos lakeshore city: **Ward 9S** on the ninth floor (the linen room, the row of beds, M.K.'s bed by the east window over the lake), with the director's office next door
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
**Twist 3.** Tanaka built one wall for herself, confidentiality, and here she has to take it down with her own hands. The patient she has treated for two and a half years is the Compiler, and the director who protected her is the Compiler's son. Tanaka survives the chapter by writing it as a case note in real time, and the case note is its spine.

## Beats
- **04:00.** Tanaka takes the three men into 9S, because leaving them in a logged corridor would be worse, and hides them in the **linen room**. She tells them the room's rule: *nothing here is recorded*. Old Songs hums anyway, very low, and she can hear that he doesn't trust the room yet. She knows the Wing C signals: rising means *yes*, falling means *no*, and silence means *they're watching*.
- **She calls the director,** as protocol requires. Dr. Vale comes up at 04:40, tall and stooped. He looks at the three men with beautiful manners and no surprise at all, tells them to stay where they are, and says nothing else.
- **Morning.** The old man won't speak, so Alexei explains: he thinks the patient who sent the songbooks is someone he knew in 2063. Tanaka refuses. "I don't discuss patients. Not with you. Not with anyone."
- **The necessity.** At 09:30 M.K. hears the humming through two doors, sits up, and asks who is singing the Lakeshore Air on her ward. She asks to see him. Tanaka can't let an unregistered stranger near a frail patient on a guess, and she can't refuse her patient either. So she makes the name the test. In the corridor: *"I am going to tell you the name she is registered under. If it means nothing to you, you'll go home on the next fog."* For the first time in the book she says it aloud: **"Margit Kerr."**
- **Old Songs stops** mid-phrase, where the tune lifts. The corridor goes quiet the way Wing C did. His face says the name means something to him. Tanaka doesn't know what, and he doesn't say. (The reader knows: Kerr was his teacher at the Archive, Ch 5. Tanaka's POV must not state it.)
- **The bed by the east window.** The woman there turns her head. She is small and very upright, with short white hair, and holds her cup to still her right hand. **"Hello, Misha. You look terrible."** He answers with one **rising phrase**.
- **The real-time case note,** in italics throughout:
  - *03 January 2109. 09:41. Ward 9S. Patient M.K. Visitors: three, unregistered. Patient identifies one visitor as "Misha". Visitor responds with a rising phrase (Wing C: yes).*
  - *09:44. Patient M.K. states her name is Elise Vance.*
  - *09:46. Patient states she wrote the Introduction carried in the blue songbook. Carried by me.*
  
  Tanaka thinks of what she carried across the lake three times in her bag, and sets her pen down very carefully.
- **Vale in the doorway.** Vance, without looking round: *"Soren. Close the door, you're letting Chronos in."* To Tanaka: "My son. You've met." *Director present. Director states he is the patient's son. Hypothesis, August 2106: the director has a daughter. Revised: the director has a mother.*
- **Why he protected her.** In the corridor, courteously, Vale finally tells her. In August 2106 his mother read Isaiah's article and asked him to take in the émigré physician and keep her safe: *"She said you were proof it could be survived."* He blocked salience therapy and then stabilisation because she asked. He gave Tanaka the case himself because his mother wanted her. He doesn't apologise.
- **The day.** Tanaka keeps the ward running around them, because a sealed ward that stops running gets noticed. There is no fog that night. The lake is clear all the way to the Veridican shore, so they have to wait.

## Ending
Evening. Tanaka comes to take M.K.'s pulse and finds Old Songs in the visitor's chair, not humming. Vance leans towards him and says quietly, not for Tanaka: **"I sent you three things. Only three."** Tanaka writes: *Patient states she sent three documents.* Then she counts, in her head, what reached Veridica and was published on Dec 17: the Introduction, Three, Five, the strip and the Warning. There were five.

## Notes
- **Twist 3 revealed.** "Margit Kerr" is spoken **only here** (README decision 5). In the case notes she stays **"Patient M.K."**, even after she gives her real name.
- **(Twist 2 clue)** The turn is "three things" against a count of five. Ch 22 adds "Copy X isn't mine."
- **Old Songs doesn't speak.** He hums, stops, and answers with a rising phrase. His speech is Ch 22.
- **Vance:** precise and dry. In this chapter she doesn't mention the withheld page, any contributor or copy X, and nobody says the manuscript's title.
- **Vale** (Book 5 Ch 6) pays off Tanaka's old guess that he "had read Isaiah's article and had a daughter". **Don't compare Vale to Kerr** (Book 5 Ch 12's Kerr is also "tall, stooped, courteous"). Nothing may hint at who Vale's father is.
- **The ward (Ch 3):** beds, initials only, paper charts, the disinfectant, and the east-window bed. The linen room is new.
- **Thu Jan 3 is a Thursday.** Ch 22 uses that; don't point at it here.
