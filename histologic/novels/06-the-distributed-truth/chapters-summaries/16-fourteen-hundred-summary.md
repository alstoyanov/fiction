# Chapter 16: Fourteen Hundred

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Mon Dec 17, 2108
**Setting:** Kestrel Point: the ferry office, Ruth's kitchen, the lakeshore road at the end of Harbour Street; night
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Publication day, seen from the claimed strip where the storms fell. The pages land on seventeen people who have lived inside Principle Five without knowing it had a name. The war stops, and Ruth has to decide how to write that down.

## Beats
- **Morning, winter hours at the ferry office.** The fog is real weather. The book's storm schedule says 15:00 today, on the winter timetable, as it has for weeks. (Since late November the storms have kept the ferry office's winter freight hours instead of the summer sailings of Ch 4, and Ruth's back pages recorded the change.) Ruth just brings Wren's coat in early.
- **13:20, the courier.** A young woman Ruth has never met, on foot, frost on her scarf. She hands over a sealed envelope, heavy with paper, marked *Not before 14:00, Monday 17 December. For the Kestrel Book.* "There are four more towns on the shore." Ruth signs, and the courier countersigns the receipt, the book's way.
- **The kitchen fills.** Word has come down Harbour Street. The school has been shut since October, so Wren is home. By 13:55 there are fourteen people and one borrowed chair. Ruth fetches the book from the chandlery reading room.
- **14:00.** She opens the envelope in front of witnesses. On the kitchen screen, live from the capital, a thin man in glasses reads his orders aloud on the Justice Center steps: Commissioner Chen. Ruth met the other commissioner, Kira Osman, in the church hall in November. The broadcast isn't cut. Ruth half-notices that no one cuts it.
- **The pages go round:** the KINDRED orders, heard before they are read; the Introduction (*"…decided for one…"*); Principle Three, at which someone laughs once, horribly; the strip; the handwritten page; Five.
- **Wren reads Five aloud**, standing, in her school voice. Quote it in full:
  > *"External threats unify more reliably than internal messaging. Fact warfare between Histologic nations is not failure but function, so long as it remains a war of facts and not of blood. Border regions of broadcast overlap should be preserved, not resolved: they are where a population's tolerance for contradiction may be measured, and raised."*
  Silence. Ruth thinks of the pier, Wren's feet on boards that weren't there, and the schedule in the book's margins. *Measured.* The rope-seller: "Who's the population?" Wren: "Us."
- **14:15, the lakeshore road.** Ruth goes out without her coat to where the gray Border Security van has stood before every scheduled storm since May. It is there with its engine running. As she watches, the mast on its roof comes **down**, folding flat. Someone in the cab speaks into a radio. The van turns and drives away inland, and Ruth stands in the fog until she can't hear it.
- **15:00.** Nothing: no pressure behind the eyes, no second pier. The kitchen waits, and the storm doesn't come.
- **Evening news:** *Border Security, lakeshore command: all transmitter units stood down, 14:05*, one line, attributed to Captain Y. Sokolov. In Chronos, a historian of their Temporal Archive has published his own orders from his own log. In the Sovereignty, a column headed *Decided For Us?* The neighbours argue in the kitchen, and the book takes the disagreement down in the margins, in the other person's hand.
- **23:40.** The Chronos broadcast that carries across the water, the one Kestrel isn't meant to watch, reports that its Judge has read the collective will in the logs of millions and determined, **at 0.88**, that corrective broadcasting "no longer serves the collective interest". Then the Chronos channel goes quiet.

## Ending
It is the first night without a storm since May. With Wren asleep across two chairs, Ruth writes: *Today the war stopped. Witnesses: seventeen.* She crosses out *seventeen* and writes *everyone*. Then she crosses that out too, because it isn't how the book works, and writes *seventeen* again.

## Notes
- **Fixed times (README):** **14:00** publication, **14:05** stand-down order, **0.88** at **23:40**.
- **(Twist 2 clue, optional and light)** The state broadcast carries the steps reading uncut. Ruth notices it once and moves on. It pays off in Ch 17 and Ch 24.
- **Sokolov is not at the van.** No interior and no scene. The stand-down is known only through the van and the statement.
- **Kestrel Book:** **seventeen** contributors on this date (README 2). It is kept by day in the chandlery, which burns on Christmas Eve (Ch 18).
- **Storms are perceptual.** The 15:00 storm is marked by the absence of sensation. No weather words, and the fog is real.
- **The packet** follows Ch 15, with no names.
- **Walk-ons stay unnamed.** Emmet Varley may be one of the fourteen, pleasant and quick to countersign, with no plant (Ch 19 owns his falsification).
