# Chapter 4: The Kestrel Book

**POV:** Ruth Calloway
**Date:** Wed Nov 7, 2108
**Setting:** Kestrel Point: the shuttered ferry office, the **old chandlery** reading room on Harbour Street where the book is kept by day, the church hall; home at dusk
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
Show how a journal network works, one town at a time, and why it matters. Ruth notices the pattern the capital won't say out loud. The League's leaflets appear. Kira sees her own method in a stranger's handwriting. The chapter ends with a broadcast that steals a town's founding and a book that won't let it go.

## Beats
- **Morning at the ferry office,** closed for the winter. Ruth does end-of-season paperwork. The **summer timetable** is still pinned in the window: **07:40, 12:10, 17:40, 21:40.**
- **The pattern.** Ruth has been writing down storm onsets in the back pages of the book, with time, street and duration. Laid out today, they match **the ferry timetable to the minute**, including the last sailing time, 21:40 (the Prologue alert). The last ferry went on **Oct 31**, and the storms have kept the timetable anyway. They arrive at full strength all at once, "like a switch", and have nothing to do with anything new in either factorepo. She doesn't know whose timetable it is. She remembers the van.
- **The chandlery reading room,** where the book is kept by day on a table under the window. **How the book works,** shown in use:
  - every entry is dated, timed and placed, and **countersigned** by a second witness in their own hand;
  - disagreement goes in the **margin, in the other person's hand**, and nothing is crossed out;
  - it has **seventeen contributors**, and a copy of the method sits at the front, in the courier's handwriting from April.
- **Emmet Varley** comes in: pleasant, eager, and always with something to add. Today it is a long entry about the storm on the 4th, with more detail than anyone else remembered. Ruth countersigns the part she saw.
- **League of the Record leaflets** have been pushed under doors all along Harbour Street overnight: ***THE JOURNALS ARE CONTAMINATION.*** They call for the pure record and give a capital address. The leaflets are well printed on good paper. Somebody has paid for them.
- **The listening session** is held by the Commission in the **church hall**, with folding chairs and a tea urn. **Kira Osman** is there in a plain coat. She listens more than she speaks. Ruth reads three entries aloud, the Prologue entry among them, with the neighbour's time in the margin. Afterwards Kira asks to see the book and turns to the method page. She reads her own rules in a stranger's hand, and for a moment her face does something Ruth can't read. **She is quietly impressed:** "You kept the disagreements." Ruth: "That's the only part that works."
- **Ruth shows her the timetable pages.** Kira copies them into her own book, line by line, asks two exact questions about onset, and says only, "Keep writing it down." She doesn't say what she thinks it means. (She knows Border Security's programme from Book 4. She says nothing, which is part of this book's cost of silence.)

## Ending
**17:40.** On the walk home a **Chronos broadcast** comes in over the harbour, full strength at once. It is corrective broadcasting of the kind the war is fought with, and it is aimed at the town's past rather than its streets. It says that Kestrel Point was **chartered by the Chronos Lakeshore Province in 2071**. It is gone in minutes. By evening **half the neighbours "remember" a Chronos town charter**, and one of them tells Ruth over the fence that it's on a plaque by the church. There is no plaque. Ruth goes back inside and opens the book to the April pages, the first thing the town wrote when it began: the church hall's foundation stone, the old harbour wall, the pier demolished in 2089. **The book remembers otherwise, in twelve hands.**

## Notes
- **The ferry-timetable storms (flagged):** `fact-storms.md` says manufactured storms start "on the hour". These keep a *timetable* instead, the ferry's. They share every other marker: full strength at once, return to the same streets, nothing new in either factorepo. Ruth can't tell which side sends which storm. Later writers may use the fact that both sides keep the same timetable as a quiet echo of Twist 4 ("both sides working together"), but **must not state it**.
- **Varley (Twist 2 support, very light):** keen, prolific, pleasant. He exposes himself in Ch 19. Nothing here should look false.
- **The leaflets' good paper** is an early echo of Ch 18's Ministry budget line. Keep it to one sentence.
- **"Twelve hands"** means twelve of the seventeen contributors wrote the April founding entries.
- **Numbers:** seventeen contributors. Don't count the journals.
- **Kira's silence about the timetable** is deliberate. She doesn't name Sokolov to Ruth.
- The broadcast is perceptual: nothing physical changes, and the "plaque" exists only in memory.
