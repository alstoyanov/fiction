# Chapter 6: The Library

**POV:** Alexei Volkov
**Date:** Dec 2105 (starting a few days before Ch 4 and running through it)
**Setting:** Ashford library (serving the general population and privileged Wing C inmates), Old Songs' trolley route
**Length:** ~3,000 words

## Purpose
Alexei's moral urgency, set against Dmitri's caution. Show why Alexei acted, and introduce Old Songs as the source of the brothers' survival.

## Beats
- Alexei shelves, stamps and delivers. He is gentle and mechanical, the model reformed outlaw. The general-population inmates like him. Inside he is The Phantom, still running escape routes in his head.
- The books: story 03's image, "when he touched a book he felt other hands". Here it becomes literal. Alexei notices which inmates hold on to which books. The library is a map of minds that haven't given up yet.
- **Old Songs (Mikhail Petrov):** A years-long inmate everyone treats as harmless furniture. Alexei explains to the reader what the brothers owe him. At the border facility and again here, the old man hummed pre-Histologic songs, and Alexei noticed that **the songs survived correction**. The old man told him in one whispered sentence: *"They look for what you love. So I hide what I love in what I've memorised."* The brothers combined that with the bond. It is the reason they are still themselves.
- He watched Marcus Chen go from frightened to hollow in eight weeks, and his guilt is sharp: *We could have told him. We watched.*
- The brothers' argument happens without words: the rule set by Dmitri, Nikolai's fear, Alexei's refusal. He thinks of the people he helped cross the border, and of the woman crying over tea (Story 03). Helping people is who he is. Correction didn't take that, so why is he letting caution take it?
- **The decision:** He can't reach Marcus, who is too far gone and too closely watched. But the new woman is still fighting. He picks the dullest book in the library, a pre-Histologic water-systems manual. He underlines one word on page 47 and routes it to 7-K through Old Songs' trolley. The old man looks at the book, then at Alexei, and hums a little louder.
- He knows the cameras. He knows the risk.

## Ending
That evening, from the library window, he watches the book go into Kira's slot. Through the bond he feels Dmitri's fury land like a blow. He answers with the only defence he has: *Mama said save each other. She didn't say only each other.*

## Continuity Notes
- Old Songs speaks rarely and elliptically, which is consistent with his character file.
- Establish the library's reference-shelf blind spot, used in Ch 10.
- Alexei is warm and wry, and uses contractions. The contrast with Dmitri is deliberate.
