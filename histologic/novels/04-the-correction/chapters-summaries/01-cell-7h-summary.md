# Chapter 1: Cell 7-H

**POV:** Marcus Chen
**Date:** Oct 8–9, 2105 (first day and night)
**Setting:** Wing C, Cell 7-H
**Length:** ~3,200 words

## Purpose
Show Wing C: its physical rules, its silence, and the horror of watching. End on the realization that correction is erasure.

## Beats
- **The cell:** Two walls are transparent (facing the corridor and 7-G). The other two are white. There's a bed, a steel sink, a camera dome, and a book-and-meal slot. It is fully soundproofed. Marcus can see perhaps a dozen cells and cannot hear a single voice.
- **The layout** as he maps it, historian-style: the triangle of identical men across the atrium. A small, gray-streaked Japanese woman (Tanaka) who watches everyone with a clinician's stillness. Several blank-faced inmates. Next door in 7-G, Samuel Ferris, a young man who waves at him through the glass and mouths words Marcus can't read.
- The triplets are the strangest thing he sees. They are identical, compliant and expressionless, and they are the only inmates who wear work overalls instead of cell gray. They leave the wing on escorted work details. Marcus assumes they are the model patients, the "cured".
- **The meal trolley:** An old man with a white ponytail (Old Songs) pushes it. When he opens the slot, Marcus hears **humming** for two seconds, a tune he doesn't know, older than anything in the factorepo. It is the only human sound in Wing C.
- Marcus writes down what he knows in his head, as dated entries, the way he used to at work. It is a reflex and a comfort. (This seeds his later hiding technique.)
- Hayes visits and tells him sessions begin tomorrow. Marcus asks what happened to the man in 7-G. She says, "Samuel's doing very well."
- **The first night:** Orderlies come for Samuel. He looks back at Marcus, frightened, and presses his palm to the glass. Marcus, not knowing why, presses his own palm to his side of the glass.
- Hours later Samuel comes back. His posture is the same and his face is peaceful. He looks at the glass where Marcus's hand was, and there is no recognition. He sits down and stares at the white wall until dawn.
- Across the atrium, one of the triplets (Dmitri) watches Marcus watching. Marcus doesn't notice.

## Ending
Marcus lies awake doing the maths: sessions, weeks, months. He was told correction would teach him to believe. But Samuel hadn't been taught anything. Something had been *removed*. **This isn't therapy. This is erasure.** And he still believes in Veridica, which makes it worse.

## Continuity Notes
- Wing C rules to keep consistent: soundproof cells; privileged inmates move under escort; cameras in every cell; no clocks, so time is marked only by meals and lights.
- Samuel pays off in Ch 23–24. Old Songs' humming pays off in Ch 11 and Ch 14.
