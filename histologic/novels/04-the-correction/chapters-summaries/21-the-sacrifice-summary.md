# Chapter 21: The Sacrifice

**POV:** Isaiah Okonkwo
**Date:** Late July 2106
**Setting:** Maintenance room B-4 (last night meeting), Cell 7-E
**Length:** ~3,000 words

## Purpose
Isaiah chooses to stay. The escape gets its moral weight, and the book gets its voice after the storm.

## Beats
- Isaiah lays out his reasoning like an editorial:
  - If six escape and the evidence leaves with them, Veridica calls it outlaw forgery and hunts them.
  - If the evidence is published **from inside**, by a subject, through the Director's own channel, then it's a *witness account*, and it can't be traced to the six "dead".
  - Someone has to make sure the evidence gets out if the escape fails.
  - "I'm a journalist. That's the job: stay where the story is."
- **Protests:**
  - Alexei: "We don't leave people. That's the whole point of me."
  - Tanaka: "They will put you in maximum security. You know what the cradles are like there."
  - **Marcus**, struggling for words but clear: "We don't leave people behind." (He is thinking of Samuel.)
  - Isaiah to Marcus, gently: "You're not leaving me. I'm staying by choice. There's a difference, and you of all people know it. They *took* your choice. I'm *making* mine. And you need to heal. You can't do that here."
- Kovač will stay too, as Director, and cover the story: storm, chaos, six cognitive failures, one surviving subject. Isaiah and Kovač are the two who remain.
- The alternative Isaiah quietly rejects: he could escape and publish from the border zone. He explains why that's worse. The world believes a prisoner more than a fugitive.
- Kira squeezes Marcus's hand. **It's decided:** six escape (plus Old Songs and Morrison), one remains. Everyone has a part.
- **Late that night, in 7-E:** Isaiah's memory palace. He walks through the house for what may be the last time before maximum security, and moves everything he knows into the cellar. He composes the first sentence of his article. He's afraid, and he lets himself be.

## Ending
The book-slot opens. It is Old Songs, humming a tune Isaiah doesn't know. The old man leans in and whispers, his longest speech in the book: *"The one who stays is the one who remembers. That was always my job. Now it's yours."*
