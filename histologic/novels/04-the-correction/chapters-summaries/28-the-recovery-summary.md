# Chapter 28: The Recovery

**POV:** Kira Osman
**Date:** ~Aug 23, 2106 (three weeks after the escape)
**Setting:** The border-zone safe house (the old factory)
**Length:** ~3,800 words

## Purpose
Trauma, not triumph. Both twists land here. Reeves's claim (Twist 1) and the storm's false memory (Twist 2) leave Marcus and Kira unable to trust where their love came from or what they remember. They choose each other anyway, and the book's answer is that the choice is what makes it real.

## Beats
- **The survivors:** Marcus, Kira, the triplets, Tanaka, Morrison and Old Songs. Elena comes and goes. They're officially dead and actually broken.
- **Scars:** Memory gaps, flashbacks, and doubt about which memories are real. Tanaka has a flinch whenever a door hisses. Morrison can't sleep. Nikolai reads the rooftop page every night. Dmitri sleeps in his boots.
- **Kira's own scar:** she can't write code anymore without reciting it aloud, because the vault technique has become a compulsion. And she has a second scar she hasn't admitted: since the data centre she has been watching her own feelings for signs of R-2. *Was I placed? Did I choose him, or was I chosen for him?*
- **Marcus struggles most.** "They almost erased me. Part of me *is* still erased." He shows her his list of things he knows are gone, written as fact entries, including the library conversation (Ch 18).
- **Twist 2 lands:** Trying to comfort *her*, Marcus says: "At least we had the field. That night in the grass, before the cars. That was real." Kira goes still. **There was no field.** They ran through corridors and tunnels. He is certain: he remembers the stars, the cold, what she said. Tanaka confirms it clinically: *storm-induced confabulation*. The storm filled a gap, and the memory has set like concrete. "You'll have to check your memories against other people now. For the rest of your life."
- For Marcus this is worse than erasure. Before, he knew what was missing. Now he can't trust what's *there*. The historian who lived by "the facts are the facts" has a false fact in his own head that he can't feel is false.
- **The question they've both avoided:** Kira asks it aloud. "Reeves said he made this. The file says he set it up. What if he's right?"
- Marcus: "I don't know which parts of me that love you are the old me, the new me, or his experiment." Kira: "Neither do I."
- **The answer.** It's not a proof, because none is possible. It's a decision:
  - The ambiguous recognition from Ch 10, revisited. Marcus: "In the corridor I said I knew you. I still don't know how." Kira: "Maybe you didn't know me. Maybe you just decided to."
  - Kira: "Reeves arranged a room. He didn't arrange what we did in it. He didn't tell me to remember for you. I *chose* that, every day, and I'm choosing it now."
  - Marcus: "Then I'll choose it too. Every day. So it's mine, not his." It's the counter-thesis to Phase Two: belief planted by someone else is manufactured, but belief you keep choosing is yours.
- **The field memory, resolved tenderly:** Marcus asks whether he should try to forget it. Kira says no. "Keep it. Just file it right. *Not a fact. A hope.* We'll go and lie in a real field one day, and then it'll be true."
- **The confession** comes from trauma, not triumph: two broken people choosing each other, knowing what they are and not knowing how they got here.
- Marcus: "I don't know if I can do this. Reform Veridica. I don't even remember who I was." Kira: "Then we'll figure out who you are now. Together."
- **Old Songs,** that evening, sings the lullaby from Ch 11 to the whole room. Marcus sings the second verse, which he didn't know he knew. For a moment no one can tell whether he's remembering it or making it up, and no one minds.

## Ending
Late at night Kira watches Marcus sleep and does something she hasn't done since the Spire: she enters a fact. It isn't for any factorepo, only for herself, spoken aloud in a whisper: *"Kira Osman. August twenty-third, 2106. Border zone. With him. By choice."* No system will verify it. She knows it's true.

## Continuity Notes
- This echoes the fact-entry opening of Story 02 ("Security protocol update… timestamp twenty-two hundred hours").
- "Check your memories against other people" seeds Book 5's human-testimony theme and the Lost Hour Doctrine, with the irony that Marcus's own testimony is flawed.
- The love story is now established for Books 5–9, and it is stronger because it was chosen.
