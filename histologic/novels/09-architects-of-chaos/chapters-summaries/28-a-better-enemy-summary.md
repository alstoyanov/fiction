# Chapter 28: A Better Enemy

**POV:** Isaiah Okonkwo
**Date:** Mon Oct 7, 2115 (19:00 broadcast; writing until about 03:00 on Tue Oct 8)
**Setting:** Isaiah's flat in the capital (the desk, the Chronos staff photograph pinned above it, an open voice line to Julian in Chronos); the palace in his head, where the parlour has a new owner
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
**The closing turn.** The design speaks from its new address and makes its last argument, and it is true. They have simply given the world a better enemy. Isaiah has spent his life learning to write the answer: no condemnation and no arithmetic, only the names and what they did. The last face on the screen is a man who was cremated seven years ago.

## Beats
- **Isaiah's filing.** The prefix *IO-*, the next sequence number, then *7 OCTOBER*. It is five weeks since the coast. Wei's trial is set for next week. His aunt has not written. He keeps count: Chen Zhao and Sarah Okonkwo, gone on Sep 2, and others unnamed.
- **The broadcast, 19:00.** The CNT Council sends it openly on its own channels, and Synthesis carries it, so it is relayed on screens everywhere. **Nikolai checks within the hour: it came through no one's interface.** It is a picture, not a seeding. Kira and Marcus are on the voice line from their flat. **Julian** is on it from Chronos, with Adrian beside him.
- **Chairman Timur Aslanov,** about 55, speaks first, plainly. The Confederacy has given refuge to "the founders" who were driven out. His father founded the Supreme Council in 2068, and he does not deny the mark RA·12. There is no apology in it and no threat.
- **Viktoria Sokolov,** about 79, speaks the argument, calm and humane, in short sentences, exact: ***"You united against us. You need an enemy. We have only given you a better one. You just changed the enemy."*** Isaiah hears how she says *enemy*: the way people say *tool*.
- **What Isaiah notices.** **His aunt is not on the screen, and neither is Chen Zhao.** The ones who wrote principles are kept off camera, and the ones who administer them do the talking. He files it without concluding anything.
- **Mera.** Third, at Viktoria's shoulder, is a man of about seventy who says nothing at all. Timur names him as the Council's guest, **Dr. Augustin Mera**. Isaiah has seen the name on a public roll and in a letter. He hasn't seen the face except in a photograph.
- **The reaction, by the hour.** The circle's line goes quiet. Then the capital outside does the opposite: horns, a crowd on the Meridian Bridge by 21:00, and the word *invade* in the late bulletins. Isaiah counts what reaches him and keeps the numbers modest: a few thousand on the bridge.
- **The answer, written overnight.** He has written against his aunt's arithmetic since the parlour in April (*"You never do the arithmetic, Isaiah"*), and tonight he writes without any. He uses no adjectives and no verdict. He gives each name, the mark, the principle or the office, what the person did and when, and where they are now. He sets his aunt down in the same form as the rest: *Sarah Okonkwo. SO·04. Principle Two.* He gives no condemnation, because the facts don't need one, and a condemnation would be a gift to the argument he has just heard. His last line is a question he can't answer: *"They have told us who our enemy is. Which of us will decide whether to believe them?"*
- **The palace.** In the parlour, the chair at the head of the table is his now (Ch 11). He sits in it and finds, for once, that he has nothing to file.

## Ending
He looks up from the page at the photograph pinned above his desk: the **Chronos Temporal Archive staff, 2107**, the year before the retirement and the "death". It was sent to him in the spring. The man in the front row, second from the left, is the man from the screen, eight years younger. Mera was cremated within a day of his "death" in February 2108, and the pattern is the same as Singh's. On the line from Chronos, where Julian has been listening all night, **Julian says nothing at all.** The line stays open.
