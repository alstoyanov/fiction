# Epilogue: Twenty Years

**POV:** Rotating: Elena, Kira, Tamsin, Isaiah, Marcus
**Date:** Tue Nov 15, 2135
**Setting:** The farmhouse orchard at dawn; Kira's desk with Nikolai's open tools; a university seminar room in the capital; Isaiah's desk; the central plaza and the Lost Hour stone at 14:00
**Length:** ~3,600 words

## Purpose
Twenty years on, nobody keeps the one truth, because there isn't one to keep. There are many records, checked by many hands, and a country in the east opening slowly on paper. The singer is dead, and his song has more singers than he ever had listeners.

## Beats
- **The orchard, dawn (Elena, 71).** There are three graves under the plum trees: Alexei, Vance, and between them **Mikhail Petrov**. In 2110 Vance was buried beside Alexei with a grave's width of grass left between them; nobody said why, and in January 2116 that is where he was laid. He died at the farmhouse on **Mon Jan 20, 2116**, aged 77, two months after the plaza. Elena found him in the morning on the crate by the stove with the cracked door, the damper open two fingers. He was buried in the orchard between the two he had sung for. The Correction Registry has had him dead since 2106. **His name is on the stone** in the capital now with the right year, *2116*, cut by people who didn't need the record's permission. Elena still counts first and feels second. This morning she counts the three graves and lets the rest come.
  - **Her routes** are mostly the post now. **Timur Aslanov is dead.** A **provincial council has published the Register on its own noticeboards**, and others have copied it. **The CNT is opening**, slowly, on paper: letters, a library in a district town, a request for Isaiah's book. She sends a packet anyway, from habit.
- **The records (Kira, 56).** She is gray, and so is Marcus. At her desk Nikolai's open tools compare a dozen records that never decide anything. **The World Record still runs in Memoria, Axiom and Synthesis.** It is **quieter than it was, and watched**. Its reconciliation traffic is public, checked every night by strangers in six nations, and fewer people use it each year. Nikolai, 60, still sends her the night's comparisons at 06:00. She counts the discrepancies (there are always some) and logs them, and doesn't correct anyone.
- **The seminar (Tamsin, 22).** She keeps a journal. Its pages are **countersigned by strangers**, as her parents taught her; she met most of them once, and some never. This autumn a seminar on **the history of designed institutions** is being taught for the first time by a man released eight years ago after twelve years in prison. She goes to the first lecture and doesn't tell her father. **Thomas Wei**, 74, tall, gray, with a quiet voice and a thin old scar across the back of his left hand, is seen only from outside. He writes one sentence on the board with his right hand and turns to the room: ***"Nobody designs everyone."*** That is all Tamsin writes down. Her neighbour in the next seat, a stranger, countersigns it.
- **The last column (Isaiah, 68).** His final piece for the paper is filed with a prefix, a sequence number and the date. He sets down, in order: the stone, the three nations, the CNT's noticeboards, the seminar notice, and his aunt's name among the others, with no verdict. His last line is a question he admits he can't answer: ***"Did we break the cycle?"*** He doesn't know, and he says so. Somebody should find out.
- **The stone, 14:00 (Marcus, 58).** The monthly gathering has kept going for twenty-eight years. Marcus and Kira stand at the back. The names on the side of the stone are weathering: Alexei Volkov, Nora Chen, and Mikhail Petrov. **Children at the stone, who don't know who wrote it, sing his round "Many Hands"**, exact (`old-songs-character.md` §6):
  > *One hand writes and one hand checks,*
  > *one hand holds the light;*
  > *one voice fails and one voice mends,*
  > *and many make it right.*
  One voice starts, a second comes in, then a third. **It can't be sung by one voice.** Each phrase lifts at the end like a question, and the next child answers it by starting again. Marcus has no fact entry for it, and doesn't need one.

## Ending
The round goes on after the children have stopped listening to themselves, carried by voices that were never his and are no longer the singer's. **Final line, exact, as narration in Marcus's POV:**
***"There was never one truth to keep. There were only people, keeping it for each other."***

## Notes
- **The final line is exact** (plot v2). Render it as narration, not dialogue.
- **Old Songs' death** (README decision 17): Mon Jan 20, 2116, at the farmhouse, aged 77 (born March 2038), buried in the plum orchard between Vance and Alexei. **It is not shown.** The morning at the stove is Elena's memory in a few lines. **"Between" is settled:** Vance was buried "beside" Alexei (Book 8 Ch 29) with a strip of grass left between the graves (Ch 19), and Old Songs lies in that space. The Epilogue says so plainly.
- **Wei's line is exact** (README decision 19). He was sentenced in the autumn of 2115 to twelve years and released about 2127. The seminar is "taught for the first time" this autumn, which is how the plot's "first lecture" is read. He is seen only from outside, with no interior.
- **"Many Hands" lyrics are exact.** They're written out once in full.
- **Ages in 2135:** Marcus 58, Kira 56, Tamsin 22, Isaiah 68, Elena 71, Nikolai and Dmitri 60, Wei 74.
- **Timur Aslanov's death** has no date. **Chen Zhao, Viktoria, Sarah Okonkwo, Mera and Tobias:** their fates are deliberately left unstated. Isaiah's column names his aunt without saying whether she is alive.
- **The provincial council's noticeboards** pay off Ch 29 ("not one of theirs").
- **Marcus gives no fact entry,** which echoes the Book 8 Epilogue. His last entry was Ch 30.
- **Dmitri, Tanaka, Singh and Sokolov** may appear briefly at the stone. They aren't required.
