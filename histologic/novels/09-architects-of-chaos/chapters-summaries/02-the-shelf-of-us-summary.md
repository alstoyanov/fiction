# Chapter 2: The Shelf of Us

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Wed Mar 20, 2115 (dawn to dusk)
**Setting:** Elena's farmhouse in the border zone: the kitchen and the stove with the cracked door; the plum orchard behind the barn; the kitchen table at dusk
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
For five years he has kept Vance's riddle cold, the way he has kept everything, never looking at it. Now he lets one line of it lead him back to a shelf in 2063. He opens exactly one line, and no more.

## Beats
- **The stove.** He is 77, since earlier this month. The damper still has to be opened two fingers so that the crack glows and doesn't smoke, and this spring his right hand can't manage it. He does it with the left, badly, on the second try. He puts on barley and hums the **Lakeshore Air**. His hands are the first part of him to be old in a way he can't hide in a tune.
- **The news.** Elena came back on Monday night from the capital with Tanaka's message: the Deputy Minister who died in the lost hour is alive, and wants to see them all. Elena said it flatly, in her commander's voice. He hummed the rising phrase, *yes*, and went on stirring. He thinks about it now in one paragraph and doesn't follow it: the dead are coming back, and he is one of them himself.
- **The riddle, kept cold.** Since Wed Apr 9, 2110 he has carried Vance's four lines set to the Lakeshore Air. He has sung them with words only alone, in the yard, and hummed them without words everywhere else. For forty years the machines searched for heat, and what you look at gets hot, so he never looked. There has been no Judge in Veridica for five years, and the habit held anyway. Now his hands have told him how long he has. He remembers her words: *"For when I'm not here to be asked."*
- **The orchard.** Late morning, cold and bright. The plum trees are in tight bud, not yet in flower. Alexei and Elise lie side by side between the rows. He stands at the foot of both graves and sings the riddle with its words, once, under his breath:
  > *Look where they swept the shelf of us,*
  > *behind the number no one reads;*
  > *the old man peeled his apple there —*
  > *ask him the number. Ask him twice.*

  The last phrase lifts like a question. This time he doesn't turn away from it.
- **One line, and he lets it lead.** *Look where they swept the shelf of us.* This is the method he used as a boy for learning a long tune: a line at a time, and never the next one until the last has settled. Memory comes in the order Book 5 gives it:
  - the periodicals trolley in the spring of 2063, and the gray journal;
  - the shelf he gave it: *Periodicals, Governance*;
  - Thursdays, a young woman from the systems department with ink on her fingers, arguing across the reading-room table in whispers that rhyme is a compression algorithm while he says it is a prayer;
  - Kerr in his cotton gloves, pretending not to hear.

  And then **the morning in '66**: two men from a Ministry so new its letterhead still smelled of the printer, with a list, taking the issues away in a box. *Swept.*
- **"Us."** The word stops him, standing in the orchard. She didn't write *the shelf* or *the journal's shelf*. She wrote *the shelf of us.* It was the one place in the building where the three of them met: she took the journal down, he had catalogued it, and Kerr passed with his gloves on. It was also the shelf whose readers were then taken away, one after another, to be helped. He hears her voice from 2110: *"It scans. You always said mine didn't."* He laughs once, which he hasn't done in the orchard before.
- **He stops.** He can feel the second line waiting behind the first, and he doesn't let it open today. One line. He goes back to the stove before the barley catches.
- **Dusk, the kitchen table.** Fog comes up off the lake, real weather. He takes one of Elena's plain accounting ledgers and a pencil. His hand shakes, and he writes slowly. For the first time the riddle exists outside his head, and only one line of it:
  > *Look where they swept the shelf of us*

## Ending
He draws a line under ***us***, and then another, pressing hard enough to mark the page below. *She meant us: her, and me, and Kerr.*
