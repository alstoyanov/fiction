# Chapter 25: On Keeping

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Mon Aug 5, 2115 (the farmhouse at dawn; the reading at the stone from 14:00 until about 16:30)
**Setting:** Elena's farmhouse (the stove with the cracked door, the orchard); the road into the capital; the central plaza and the Lost Hour stone, with the names cut in its side
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This chapter opens Part Four. The city reads the Register aloud by many hands, one mark to each reader, so that no single voice owns the names. When the reading reaches the five who said no, the man who has kept silent all book stands up and gives his one speech. It is about his teacher. What the heroes have done for nine years was Anselm Kerr's idea, carried by a man who didn't know he was carrying it.

## Beats
- **Dawn at the farmhouse.** He is 77 and his hands are worse. He works the damper two fingers open, badly, and it takes him three tries. In the orchard he stands between the two graves under the plum trees, Vance and Alexei, and hums the ferry verse under his breath. The monthly gatherings go on without him now. The road into the capital tires him, and he comes only when something needs him there.
- **The Register is public now.** It has been since the heirs published it on Jul 8, and the heroes' hand copies went out on Jul 15. The World Record vote is ten days away. **Isaiah chose today's date.** On Aug 5, 2106 his witness account from Wing C came out through Synthesis, nine years ago. Isaiah says so to nobody but Old Songs, in the truck, filing it in his head the way he always does.
- **The reading.** It is organised by the journal networks, not the Commission. **Every reader is chosen by lot from the crowd**, and each reads one mark: the mark, the name, and the copy numeral where one exists. The families stand in the crowd and read nothing. Dmitri hears AV·05 read by a tram driver. Isaiah hears SO·04 read by a schoolgirl. Old Songs watches each of them hear it. The crowd is a few thousand. Kira has his elbow. Dmitri is on his other side, because Dmitri insisted on bringing him out once and has not stopped since.
- **CZ·11** is read by a woman in a Ministry clerk's coat, in a clear, level voice. Old Songs looks at Marcus, and Marcus looks at the stone.
- **The five.** When the lot-drawn reader for **SA·13** steps up, Old Songs stands. Nobody asked him to. SA·13 Dr. Sigrun Ahlgren and EO·14 Professor Emeka Obi are read, and the reader adds each time, as agreed, *struck through; unsigned*. The reader for **AK·15** is a boy of about sixteen. He reads *"AK·15, Anselm Kerr. Struck through. Unsigned."* Then he sees the old man standing, and he holds out the hand copy of Kerr's page without being told.
- **The one speech,** standing at the stone. It is plain and dry, with no riddle and no rhetoric, and runs about 600–800 words. Around the canonical core:
  - His own desk in the back room where they kept the periodicals nobody read: *"It had a lamp with a green shade and a drawer that stuck."* Kerr, his master, with cotton gloves for paper, taken off at eleven for the apple, peeled in one long curl with a pocketknife. *"We keep what people would rather lose, Mikhail. Anyone can keep what they want."* *"I thought he was a pompous old man. I was right. He was also right."*
  - What Kerr did: he asked where the issues had gone. Politely, in writing, twice. *"Two others asked with him. For forty-eight years I didn't know their names."* He says them: **Sigrun Ahlgren. Lorenzo Bassi.** *"The other two walked away. I don't know where to. I would like someone to find out."*
  - The correction in the spring of 2067, and the morning by the trolley: *"I said the shelf mark to him, and he said, very pleasantly, 'I'm afraid I don't know that one. Are you sure you have the number right?' He wasn't lying. They had taken it out of him so cleanly there was no hole."*
  - **The canonical core, exact (plot):** ***"The design had one flaw it could not see, because it was not in the design. My teacher said no. They took his memory. They didn't take the page. Everything we did, we did with his words and didn't know it."***
  - He says what he learned on the registry floor in April: that his own "many hands" in 2107 was Kerr's phrase. So was Vance's "Build by many hands" in 2066. *"She took her answer from him. I took mine from her, and from him, and I thought it was mine."* Then, dry: *"It's the only thing I ever said that I didn't make up, and I didn't know it."*
  - He doesn't say where Kerr is buried, because he never learned it and has never asked the record.

## Ending
He reads Kerr's last lines from the boy's copy, slowly, his hand shaking the paper: *"A record kept by one hand is a confession waiting for its torturer. Keep it by many hands."* He gives the page back to the boy. A thousand people say it back to the stone: ***"Keep it by many hands."*** He sits down. LB·16 and MR·17 are still to be read, and the reading goes on without him.
