# Chapter 27: The Checkpoint

**POV:** Col. Yevgenia Sokolov
**Date:** Mon Sep 2, 2115 (about 05:30 to 19:00)
**Setting:** Sokolov's coastal command on the Atlantic coast: the command post above the harbour, and the barrier on the coast road where it forks to the airfield and the harbour gate
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The designers leave the nation they built, and the only person who can stop them is the daughter of one of them. She doesn't stop them. She lets them pass formally, within her authority, and writes it down. Arrested, they would be martyrs. Gone, they are an address.

## Beats
- **Formal register throughout.** Her log is on paper, as it has been since Book 5, in short entries. *05:40. Coastal command. Visibility: fog, real, lifting by 08:00. Traffic: light.* No confession, and no speech about conscience.
- **The situation, as the log states it.** Since Aug 15 the heirs' remedy has been crippled and the succession is public. Singh's testimony is before a court. **The prosecutor's office will ask a court for warrants on Tue Sep 3.** On Friday it sent Border Security an informal request to "hold for questioning" certain named persons if they present at the coast. Sokolov replied in writing that she holds nobody without a warrant, and filed her reply.
- **The manifests.** A private aircraft is filed from the coastal airfield for 10:40, routed east over Memoria and Axiom, and a Synthesis-flagged vessel is to sail from the harbour on the evening tide. The passenger lists are lawful and dull. Among them: **Chen Zhao. Sarah Okonkwo.** Other names follow, which she reads once and does not write in the log (they stay unnamed on the page). **Thomas Wei** is on the Friday list of persons of interest, and he is not on any manifest.
- **Her authority, stated plainly.** Without a warrant she can still delay any departure for a documents check of up to six hours, and in six hours the warrants might exist. She knows it, and her deputy (unnamed) knows it and waits to be told.
- **Her reasoning, one log line, as close to interior as she comes:** *Arrested: martyrs. Gone: an address.* She writes it down. She doesn't explain it to her deputy.
- **The order.** At 08:15 she issues a standing written order for the day, signed, timed and copied to the First Minister's office: ***"No holds without warrant. Traffic normal."*** It is exactly what she is entitled to do, and it is mercy for nobody.
- **Her mother, briefly.** In a drawer is her mother's letter of May, and her own one-line reply is filed beside it: *"Mother. I am where I should be. It is not where you put me."* She doesn't take either out. She knows where the cars are going and who will be at the table when they get there.
- **09:50, the airfield fork.** A car comes on the coast road from the capital for the 10:40 aircraft. It carries **Sarah Okonkwo**, about 80. She looks at the colonel through the glass, gently and without apology, as if they had been introduced at a dinner long ago, which they may have been. The barrier lifts. The aircraft leaves at 10:41. Sokolov logs it.
- **Wei.** At 11:20 her radio reports from the capital that **Thomas Wei presented himself at the Justice Center** that morning and asked the duty clerk to record that he is available to the court. He has not asked for counsel. Sokolov logs it without comment.
- **The afternoon.** She drafts her report to Maitland: formal, in the first person, as she did after Farahani. It says who has passed, when, and under which order, and that the order was hers. She leaves the last line blank until the tide.
- **18:05, the harbour gate.** The vessel sails on the evening tide. The last car arrives at the barrier with twenty minutes to spare. Her deputy looks at her once. She walks down to the barrier herself.

## Ending
The rear window comes down. **Chen Zhao**, 74, courteous and unafraid, with a glass of tea on the seat beside him: ***"Colonel."*** Sokolov, formally: ***"Minister. Traffic normal."*** The barrier lifts. The vessel clears the harbour at 18:40, and she writes the time into the report's last line.

## Notes
- **The ending exchange is exact** (plot v2). **Chen Zhao goes by sea on the evening tide and Sarah Okonkwo by air in the morning.** That split is new, and it lets the day run in order and still close on the exchange.
- **The flight** (README decision 13): Mon Sep 2, by sea and air from the Atlantic coast, through her coastal command. Chen Zhao and Sarah Okonkwo go, and **the others stay unnamed**. Tobias stays in Chronos. Viktoria and Mera are already in the CNT. **The air route over Memoria and Axiom** is new; those two nations voted for the World Record and don't stop them.
- **Wei refuses to flee** (README decision 13). Presenting himself at the Justice Center is new. He is seen only through a radio report, with no lines (README decision 19).
- **Warrants on Tue Sep 3, the informal "hold" request, the six-hour documents check and the standing order** are new mechanics. They make her mercy deniable and lawful (her voice rule 4). **"Mercy for nobody"** is narration, not her words.
- **"By order"** (plot) is read as her own signed standing order.
- **Her mother's letter and her reply** are from Ch 12 and quoted exactly. Keep them identical if Ch 12 changes the wording.
- **Rank:** Colonel. She refused General in 2110 (Book 8). Her age isn't stated (44 or 45; her birthday falls between August and December).
- **Closing-turn clue:** *"an address"*. The design now has one, in the east.
- The fog is real weather. There are no storms.
