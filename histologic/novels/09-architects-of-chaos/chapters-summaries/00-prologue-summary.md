# Prologue: The Fifteenth

**POV:** Dr. Yuki Tanaka
**Date:** Fri Mar 15, 2115 (about 13:40 to 14:20)
**Setting:** The central plaza of the capital: the Lost Hour stone by the fountain, the monthly readings, the colonnade on the west side
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This opens the finale on the ritual the series built: the fifteenth of every month, the plain stone, ordinary people reading their ordinary days aloud. The first of the dead comes back. For eight years Tanaka has kept one unexplained sighting, and this time she doesn't file it. She walks across the plaza to it.

## Beats
- **Opening case note, in her italic clinical voice:** *15 March 2115. 13:41. Central plaza. Weather: cold, clear, wind from the west. Attendance: some hundreds. Notebook: the twelfth.* She began the first in the spring of 2107. She is 55, small and very straight, with the thin pale scar on her right temple. She doesn't live by the record any more, but she keeps her own.
- **The stone.** Pale gray and waist-high, a few metres from the fountain. The inscription is cut into the top: *TO THE HOUR THAT WAS LOST, AND THE TRUTH THAT WAS FOUND.* Since 2110 the war's dead have been cut into its side from the journals, from both shores and the SFW. She finds *Alexei Volkov* and *Nora Chen* by habit, without looking for them.
- **14:00, the readings.** It is the fifteenth, as it has been every month for eight years. Anyone may stand at the stone and say what they remember of one ordinary day. A Commission clerk writes each one down by hand, the speaker signs, and a second person who was there signs beside them. Give three short readings as texture, all harmless and specific: a tram driver's stops, a woman's list of what she bought on the ninth of February, a boy who remembers the weather wrongly and is corrected, kindly, by his grandmother. The crowd sings the refrain of "The Names" once, unled: *name them, and they stay.*
- **Old Songs isn't here.** He rarely comes into the capital now; the road tires him. Tanaka notes his absence the way she notes a missing instrument on a tray. She thinks of him standing at the back of these crowds, at the edge and not singing: officially dead, and there anyway.
- **14:12, the colonnade, west side.** That is where she saw the woman on Nov 15, 2107, at 16:12, and wrote *Witnesses: none*, and then went to find Kira so that it wouldn't be none. Now there is a woman by the same pillar. **About fifty-eight. Gray coat. Reading glasses on a cord.** She stands with her hands in her pockets, very still, and she isn't watching the stone. This time she is watching Tanaka. Tanaka thinks of Old Songs again: another person standing at the back of a crowd that has written her down as dead.
- **The case note she doesn't finish.** She writes: *14:12. Colonnade, west side. Female, about fifty-eight. Gray coat. Reading glasses on a cord.* She writes *Witnesses:*, and stops. She leaves the line unfinished, as she leaves the lines she can't bear to finish. This time it's for the opposite reason.
- **The walk.** No tram comes between them this time. She crosses the plaza at an ordinary pace and counts her steps from habit (Step one: confirm patient identity). She notes, clinically, what she can see as she gets closer: the coat is good, old and well mended at one cuff; the sleeves have been pushed up and then pulled down again; the face is thinner than the Ministry's official portrait and has the same set to the mouth. Tanaka doesn't let herself say the name, even in her head.
- **She reviews what she filed in 2106,** in one paragraph and no more: a cremation within a day; a family nobody could find; no photograph of the face; identification by staff who were all inside the seeded district. *Nobody who can't be seeded has seen her body.*

## Ending
The woman turns to face her fully, takes off her reading glasses and lets them drop on their cord. ***"Doctor. I think you've been keeping my file."***
