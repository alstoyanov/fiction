# Chapter 19: The Orchard

**POV:** Elena Volkov
**Date:** Mon Jul 1, 2115 (afternoon to dusk)
**Setting:** Elena's farmhouse in the border zone by the lakeshore: the kitchen table under a map; the barn; the old plum orchard behind it, where Alexei and Vance are buried side by side
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The quietest chapter of Part Three, and its heart. The woman who counts first and feels second has spent five years counting routes. Standing at a grave, she finally says the thing she didn't say. The old man doesn't answer her with words. He gives her the tune, and she sings her nephew's name herself.

## Beats
- **The kitchen table, afternoon. Elena counts.** She is 51. She has been out of hiding since the Judge ended: no machine is left to hold her record against her, and the enforcers who wanted "Vasquez" have nothing left to rule on. She still carries the notebook she never writes in. On the table is a map of the SFW–CNT border and the Black Sea coast. *Routes: four. Carriers: nine. Tested: two.*
- **The routes,** in her briefing voice:
  - across the mountain border from the SFW, on the old smugglers' paths, with Martinez's quiet leave (Ch 18);
  - by fishing boat along the Black Sea coast;
  - through unaligned territory in the south;
  - one route she won't name even to herself.
  The CNT's people **have no interfaces**. Nothing will seed them, and nothing will skim a page for them. They can read what they're handed, if they're allowed to keep it. *"History is what the Council says it is"* there, so paper is contraband, and paper is also the one thing that can't be un-said.
- **Her answer to Ch 17.** The routes won't bear the full run by the fifteenth. **On Jul 15 the first carriers take forty sets across, and no more.** After that she expects no word for months: *"Paper walks."* The forty are being copied by hand **in the CNT's own languages** by émigré volunteers, and checked by a second pair of hands, as Ch 29's copying room will do at scale. She tells Kira so by courier and doesn't soften it.
- **The quiet.** Her border contacts say the CNT side has been **quieter than in years** since spring. There have been no incidents and no arrests of carriers, and the patrols run to timetable. She doesn't like quiet borders. She has never met one that meant nothing. **(Twist 3 clue:** the heirs' silence, seen from the east.)
- **Old Songs** is at the stove, damper two fingers open, humming. He is 77 and his hands are worse: he holds the kettle with both. The farmhouse has been his home since Book 5. He doesn't ask about the routes.
- **Toward evening, the orchard.** The plums are setting, small and hard and green. Two graves under the trees: **Alexei**, buried in April 2110, and **Elise Vance** beside him, buried on Tue May 27, 2110, with a strip of grass left between the two. Old Songs walks out with her, slowly, and stands at the foot of Alexei's grave. Elena stands at the side, very straight, as she stood at the stove in 2106.
- **She tells him, for the first time, to anyone.** It comes out flat and in order, the way she briefs:
  - Isaiah gave her the first half of the message in the yard, two days after, on Fri Apr 25 (Book 8 Ch 20): ***"Tell Elena I love her."*** She didn't come in for the song that night.
  - What she said to Alexei last, on the radio, before he went for Isaiah on Apr 23, 2110, was a briefing line: *"Two of mine go with you. I want three back."* She got three back: the woman, the boy and Isaiah. She got exactly what she asked for.
  - She never said it back. She has never said the direct thing to anyone. She said it to his sheet in the barn, and he was already under it.
  Old Songs listens and says nothing. He doesn't touch her.
- **Singh, briefly.** Elena thinks once of the tip that herded her people into the Lost Hour, and of the credentials behind it, now back from the dead (Ch 5–6). She lets the thought pass. That reckoning is for another day, and she knows it will come.
- **"The Names."** Old Songs begins to hum **"The Names"**, low, the tune lifting at the end of each phrase like a question. He hums the first verse without words. At the third line he stops, and leaves the space.

## Ending
Elena sings it, hoarse, not a singer, into the space he left:
> *Who went down to the water?*
> *Name them, and they stay.*
> *Alexei went down to the water —*
> *name them, and they stay.*

Old Songs hums the refrain under her, *the record may forget them, / the song will not forget*, and the last phrase lifts and hangs. Neither of them finishes it.
