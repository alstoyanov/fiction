# Chapter 11: The Parlour

**POV:** Isaiah Okonkwo
**Date:** Mon Apr 29, 2115 (about 16:00 to night)
**Setting:** Carver Street, in the rebuilt eastern quarter of the capital: a new house, and inside it the old parlour; Isaiah's flat; his memory palace
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
For twenty years the parlour was the room he gave away: harmless, warm and true. He fed it to the machine so that the machine would leave the cellar alone. Now he sits in the real one, across the table from the woman who presided over it, and learns that it was never harmless. She doesn't deny anything. She has done the arithmetic.

## Beats
- **The walk.** He arrives at 16:00 exactly, as the letter asked (Ch 4): *"The house is where it always was."* The old eastern quarter was cleared and rebuilt years ago, and the Carver Street of his childhood survives only in his palace. The house at the old number is new, plain and well kept, on the same ground. So the record didn't lie about the quarter, and she didn't lie about the house. He has a hand copy of p. 39 in his coat lining, where he has always kept what matters.
- **The parlour.** She opens the door herself. **Sarah Okonkwo**, about eighty, upright, sharp and elegant, silver-haired. Inside, the parlour is **exact**: the long table, the chairs, the cups and the lamp. She moved every piece from the old house when the quarter was cleared. He walks in *as if he were arriving at a press conference*, and for a second the palace and the room are the same room. He files the difference: the window faces the other way.
- **Pepper soup** is on the stove, "because you always asked". He takes a bowl, because refusing would be a statement, and he doesn't make statements before he has the facts.
- **He asks, in order, like an editorial.** He lays the hand copy on the table. *SO·04. Ambassador Sarah Okonkwo.* Then Principle Two, from copy VII: *"…let there be enemies outside them all… Controlled conflict answers every question before it is asked."*
- **She doesn't deny it.** She reads her own line as though it were a good paragraph by someone she once admired. *"I wrote that at thirty. I'd write it again."*
- **Her case, calm and humane, in short sentences** (the Architects' voice rules):
  - The Fracture Years, which she remembers and he doesn't.
  - Wars "of facts and not of blood" for forty years.
  - The SFW–CNT border wars kept to a border.
  - Then, exact: ***"Controlled conflict saved more lives than it cost. I did the arithmetic. You never do the arithmetic, Isaiah."***
  - He hears his own sentence from Book 4 turned back on him: *It was arithmetic, in the end.*
- **What he asks and what she answers:**
  - **Did she call after his arrest in 2105?** No. *"You were exactly where a witness should be. Inside."*
  - **Did she arrange his career?** She smiles and doesn't answer. He files that as an answer he can't print.
  - **Why the invitation in March?** *"I'm eighty. And you were going to read my name by summer. I wanted you to have tea first."* He notes that she knew. He doesn't know how.
  - **Is she afraid?** *"Of you? You'll publish it, and they'll read it, and they'll be grateful someone kept the peace."* He files that too.
- **The Battle of Blood.** He names the dead he counted in Book 8: Alexei, Nora, Farahani, the siege. She says it was *"more than it should have been. Fewer than it would have been."* He doesn't write it down, and he notices that he doesn't.
- **The chair.** At the head of the table is her chair, the one in his palace. She sits in it now. He stays standing until the end.

## Ending
Evening, the walk home through the rebuilt quarter. That night he sits in his flat, closes his eyes, and goes in: the hall, the kitchen, the cellar door whose colour he lost in Unit 3. The parlour. Aunt Sarah sits at the head of the table, silent, as she has every night since 2108, with the good teacup in front of her that appeared in March (Ch 4). He asks her, courteously, to move. She rises and goes out, and the cup goes with her. For the first time he **takes the chair at the head of the table for himself**.
