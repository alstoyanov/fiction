# Chapter 7: Ask Him Twice

**POV:** Mikhail "Old Songs" Petrov
**Date:** Mon Apr 8, 2115 (about 10:15 to 15:00)
**Setting:** The capital's Historical Archive, for the first time since 2067: the entrance desk; the periodicals room and the Governance shelves; the reading room at eleven; the basement correspondence registry and its card index
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
**The solve.** An old man walks back into the building that took his teacher, carrying the riddle his friend left him. Every line of it points into these rooms, and the last line turns on the one thing he did only once. Bureaucracies don't destroy the inquiry about the thing they destroyed. They file it.

## Beats
- **Since Mar 20,** one line a week, the way he learns a tune. Line two opened at the stove: *the number no one reads* is **114**, the number that closed up so neatly the shelves run 113, 115. Line three opened in the orchard: *the old man peeled his apple there* is Kerr, at eleven, in the reading room, in one long curl with his pocketknife. Both lines are the Archive. On Thursday evening, before he went up to bed and before Singh began, he gave Kovač a slip of paper that said only: *Archive. Periodicals. — M.P.* She arranged the rest and didn't ask.
- **The desk.** Kovač, 61, in her dark coat, walks him in. The pass reads *VISITOR: PETROV, M. SPONSOR: KOVAČ, H.* The Civic Record still lists him as deceased, because he declined to be brought back to life in 2106. Nobody at the desk checks the dead any more. He is a dead man with a visitor's pass, and he finds that it suits him.
- **The building.** The same floor polish. The back room where the periodicals nobody reads were kept, and the lamp with the green shade. He doesn't go to the desk with the drawer that stuck. He hums the Lakeshore Air under his breath the whole way. Kovač hears it and doesn't mention it.
- **The Governance shelves.** The shelf labels read ***113***, then ***115***, closed up so well you can't see the join. That isn't how numbers run in a real archive. He does the obvious thing the riddle seems to ask. With Kovač's help he takes down the volumes on either side and looks *behind the number*. There is dust, the bare back of the shelf and fifty years of nothing. He puts everything back exactly.
- **The reading room, 11:00.** He sits at the long table where Elise argued with him on Thursdays for two years. A young duty archivist (unnamed) eats a sandwich at her desk at eleven. Nobody peels an apple. The room smells of polish and paper and not of apples, and that absence is the sharpest thing in the building.
- **The last line catches.** He hums the riddle under his breath to the Lakeshore Air, and at *ask him the number. Ask him twice.* the tune lifts, and stops.
  - **The memory, exact (Book 5 Ch 12 and 30):** one morning by the trolley, after Kerr came back from being "helped", he said the shelf mark to him, very quietly. Kerr smiled with perfect courtesy and no recognition at all: ***"I'm afraid I don't know that one. Are you sure you have the number right?"***
  - **He had only ever asked him once.**
  - **Kerr asked twice.** Politely, in writing, twice, where the issues had gone and on whose authority (Book 5: *"He asked politely. He asked in writing. He asked twice."*). Mikhail's one question got nothing back. Kerr's two might still answer, because the letters would have been kept.
  - *Behind the number no one reads* isn't a shelf. It is paper filed about a number that officially never existed: letters that nobody reads, because nobody reopens an inquiry into a thing that was never there.
  - His thought, in the plot's words: ***Bureaucracies never destroy the inquiry about the thing they destroyed; they file it.***
- **The riddle leaves his head.** He takes out the ledger page from Mar 20, still holding only line one underlined, and writes lines two, three and four beneath it in his shaking hand. Then he gives the page to Kovač. It is the first time the riddle has been in another person's hands. She reads it twice. He says the only words he speaks in the chapter: ***"He asked twice."***
- **The registry.** Folders are her element. She knows how registries work. In the 2060s the Archive's outgoing and incoming letters were logged on **paper index cards by correspondent**, and the files were **boxed by year** in the basement. The cards were never put into the catalogue, and the catalogue is what was purged. They go down. The card cabinet stands in the basement registry office, its steel drawers labelled by letter. She pulls **K**, 2060–2069, and walks the cards with one finger, the way she used to walk a roster.

## Ending
One card, in Kerr's own neat hand:
> ***Kerr, A. Inquiry re: Periodicals, Governance, 114. 1st letter. 2nd letter.***

He looks at the number in his teacher's hand, written in the weeks after the box went. It is the only place in Veridica where the number still exists.
