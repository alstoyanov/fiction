# Chapter 5: The Filed Woman

**POV:** Dr. Amara Singh
**Date:** Mon Apr 1, 2115 (about 07:00 to 18:30)
**Setting:** A rented room in the capital that Tanaka found for her on Mar 15; Tanaka's car on the lakeshore road; Elena's farmhouse kitchen at dusk
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
This is her only POV in the series after Book 5's Prologue. We hear nine years of being nobody from the inside: how precise she is, how wry, and how she keeps arguing herself out of what she sees. Her testimony is saved for Thursday. Today she only has to decide to walk through one door.

## Beats
- **Opening, in her written voice:** a note on the back of a tram ticket, in her small upright hand: *Apr 1. Farmhouse today. Tell it once, to all of them, Thursday. Not before. Why not before? A.S.* She is about 58. Her reading glasses hang on their cord, and her sleeves are rolled to the elbow by eight o'clock, as they always were. The **gray coat** on the door hook is the coat from her office stand in 2106. It has been mended at one cuff and she has never replaced it. She knows that is not a sensible reason to keep a coat.
- **Why only once.** She has told nobody the hour in nine years. She asked Tanaka for Thursday and all of them together, so that it would be told once, in front of many witnesses, and written down by more than one hand. She has learned what one person's account is worth. For today she keeps the hour shut like a door. Her POV approaches it and stops: the knock, the clock at *13:59:58*, and then nothing. **Nothing from inside the hour is shown in this chapter.**
- **What Tanaka established** (Mar 15–18, recalled): she examined her, courteously, as a case. The interface behind Singh's ears has been reporting zero since November 2106, **a spoofed flatline, the canon method**, and it still receives broadcasts. In nine years nobody has asked her about the zero behind her ears.
- **The nine years, in order, as she arranges things:**
  - **2106–2112, unaligned territory.** It was a disputed strip. For six years she kept a refugee registry **by hand**, in ledgers, for people no system counted: names, dates, who vouched for whom. She calls it "the only honest job I ever had in records".
  - **2112 onward, Chronos, under another name** (**never given on the page**). A lakeshore town, a room over a shop. After Chronos's Judge died, nobody there asked about an unlogged woman.
  - **Once, on Nov 15, 2107,** she came to the first Day of Testimony and stood by the colonnade. A small woman with a notebook saw her. She left before the tram had passed.
- **The watching.** She watched, as her margin note told her to (*"Who restores it? Watch who restores it."*). She wrote it for herself, at her desk, a minute before the knock (Book 5 Prologue). She watched through the newspapers, Isaiah's columns and the Chronos press:
  - the Historical Integrity Commission of 2107, its restorers appointed by the Minister;
  - the Concordance;
  - RESTORATION;
  - in 2110, the Commission of Rebuilding, again under the Minister.

  She has kept her own list of who restored what and when. It is a short list with one name recurring on it. **She doesn't write the name in this chapter.**
- **The file.** In 2112, in Chronos, she read *The Cost of Certainty* and found Tanaka's case note on her own body: *"Nobody who can't be seeded has seen her body."* She laughed aloud in a shop, and then sat down on the floor of it. From then on she knew who was keeping her file.
- **Why now.** She has read in the same book that the manuscript's contents list a **Register of Contributors** that nobody has found. "Somewhere there is a page with names on it," and she is 58. For nine years she has been evidence that nobody can use, like the torn page in her old safe. She is tired of being a thing in a folder.
- **The road.** Tanaka drives her to the lakeshore in the afternoon. They talk about tea, the road and the weather, and nothing else. As they near the lake the fog comes up, real weather. She checks the time on the car clock twice and notices that she does it.
- **The farmhouse.** It is the one place that isn't in any record. There is a yard of mud, a barn, and **Elena** in boots at the door, 51, who looks her up and down once, left to right, and says, "Right. Come in."

## Ending
She steps into the kitchen. The stove door has a crack that glows. An old man with a long white ponytail is standing at it with a wooden spoon. He looks up and nods to her, once, without surprise, ***as if she were late for a meeting***. Then he goes back to stirring, and hums.
