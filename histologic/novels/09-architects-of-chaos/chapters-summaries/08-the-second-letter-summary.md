# Chapter 8: The Second Letter

**POV:** Helena Kovač
**Date:** Thu Apr 11, 2115 (about 09:30 to 12:30)
**Setting:** The Historical Archive's basement correspondence registry: the retrieval table under a strip light, the steel card cabinet, and the floor between the stacks
**Length:** ~3,400 words

## Purpose
The woman who has kept folders all her life opens the one folder the design never thought to reopen. Behind a polite, unanswered letter is the thing five books have been chasing. It is almost whole, and it is missing exactly what its compiler gave away. Clipped to its front is a page by the man who said no first.

## Beats
- **Her voice, briefing herself:** *Thursday, 11 April. Registry, basement. Box: CORRESPONDENCE 2066, J–L. Requested Monday, 14:40. Delivered 09:30.* She is 61. She has rimless glasses and keeps her hands in her coat pockets. She trusts paper because nobody can adjust it. Mr. Petrov asked for Thursday. She didn't ask why, and she knows why: Vance read here on Thursdays.
- **She has carried the ledger page since Monday,** folded in her inside pocket: four lines in a shaking hand, line one underlined twice. She knows it by heart now, which she didn't intend.
- **The box.** The unnamed registry clerk brings it on a trolley and leaves them alone. The file is a buff registry folder: *KERR, A.* It is **thicker than two letters should be**, and she notices that before anything else.
- **The two letters.** They are Kerr's own **carbon copies** on Archive letterhead, each with a registry stamp, *RECEIVED — FILED*. They are addressed upstairs to the Director, and through him to the new Ministry whose men carried the box. The ministry isn't named on the page.
  - **1st letter, Mon Feb 8, 2066:** a courteous request to know where the issues at *Periodicals, Governance, 114* had been taken, and on whose authority, "so that the catalogue may record it".
  - **2nd letter, Mon Mar 1, 2066:** the same request, with no reproach, "since I have had no reply".
  - **No reply** is filed to either. They are polite and unanswered. She thinks of every form she has signed that nobody answered.
- **Behind the second letter.** It is a flat bundle folded inside one plain sheet, the way an archivist wraps a thing so that it will keep. A single typed sheet is clipped to the front. She unclips it and sets it aside unread, face up. The bundle beneath is typed. The **chipped *e*** takes the same little bite out of every *e*. There is **no copy numeral** in any lower right corner. *The Compiler's copy.* She says it once, aloud, and Mr. Petrov closes his eyes.
- **The inventory, by procedure.** She doesn't read it. It isn't hers to read first, and not on a basement table. She counts it the way she was trained to, **by the folio numbers at the foot**, turning corners:
  - **p. 1**, torn **lengthwise** down the right edge, where the contents strip came away;
  - **pp. 5 to 39**, continuous;
  - **missing: pp. 2–4** (the Introduction), **p. 40** (the handwritten Warning) and **the strip**. They reached the farmhouse in songbooks in 2108;
  - **between pp. 32 and 33,** a faint rust ghost of a clip that once held an extra leaf in place. The leaf is gone. It is the Seven page, which Kira has held since 2110.
  She understands it all at once: Vance gave away her own pages and one torn strip, and left the rest where only a question could find it.
- **What she lets herself see, and nothing more.** She sees marks at the foot of pages, at every even page of a principle and at its end (two capitals, a raised dot and a number), and doesn't read them. She sees that the last page, **39**, is a list, and **turns it face down**. The Annex and the Register are for the farmhouse table and for all of them.
- **The removal slip.** Before either of them reads a word further, she writes in the registry's paper removal ledger, in her own name, with the time: *Removed for examination. To be returned. H. Kovač.* She won't steal from an archive, not even this. She knows a paper ledger can be read by anyone who comes down here.
- **Kerr's page.** Now she turns to the sheet she set aside. Its steel paper clip has rusted a mark onto p. 1. It is one typed sheet on a **different machine: the *e*s are whole**, so it was never among the thirteen. It is titled ***ON KEEPING***. At its foot is a mark, **AK·15**, **struck through with a single line**, still legible. It is the first struck-through mark any of them has seen. She notices what the two letters could be and **doesn't let herself try them against any other mark**. Some things you don't do on a floor.
- **He reads it.** She gives it to Mr. Petrov and watches him read the whole page once, slowly, his lips moving. His face does what it did in the back room in January 2107, when he told them about Kerr. The page is plain, courteous prose, the voice of a man who thought an archive was a promise. Then he reads the last lines aloud:
  > ***"A record kept by one hand is a confession waiting for its torturer. Keep it by many hands."***
- **What she hears in it.** She was in the back room in January 2107 when he said "Write it down. Many hands." She has read the Warning's last words, *Build by many hands.* She understands that the Warning took its answer from this page, and that he has carried his teacher's phrase since the 2060s without knowing whose it was.

## Ending
He sits down on the registry floor, between the stacks, with the page on his knee. Kovač takes off her glasses, puts them back on, and sits down beside him on the concrete, in her dark coat, at 61. Neither of them speaks.
