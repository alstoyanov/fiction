# Chapter 21: The Peace Everyone Wants

*Monday, July 15, 2115. The capital*

---

*Jul 15, 2115. Home. Publication: 14:00, as planned. Status: redundant?*

He filed the question mark under UNCHECKED, because it was the truest word available and he would not pretend the day was what it had been meant to be. He was thirty-seven. It was the fifteenth, the day the circle had chosen eight weeks ago in a kitchen, the day a thousand hands were to carry the truth into the open at the hour it was once stolen in secret; and the truth had been in the open for a week, carried not by a thousand hands but by one secretariat in Synthesis, with a gift attached. Tamsin climbed into his lap at breakfast and asked for the boat song — the ferry verses of the Lakeshore Air, which she had heard Old Songs hum and had decided were hers — and he did not have it, the tune would not come up out of wherever the correction had put it, and he hummed her something wrong instead and she accepted it, because she was two and forgiving, and he filed *boat song (lost)* and gave her toast.

A week of relief. Since the eighth the Register had been in every paper and so had the remedy, and so had Chen Zhao's confession, which had made him more trusted rather than less; the papers had started calling him *the Architect who stayed,* as a compliment, as though staying to confess were the hard and honourable thing and running the cowardly one, which it was, and which was exactly why the design had arranged for a man to stay. The coverage was gentle about the Architects, who were old and sorry and mostly dead, and warm about the World Record, which promised in one clean sentence the thing everyone had wanted for sixty years: *no nation can ever be written by another again.* The letters pages ran it. The radio call-ins ran it. The journal networks, the very networks the circle had built to resist exactly this, were full of people wondering aloud whether one carefully verified record for the whole world might not, after all, be the sensible end of the story. People were ashamed of the design and grateful for the repair, both at once, and the two feelings did not fight each other; they braided together into a single forward-leaning motion that Marcus could feel in the city like a tide.

He had lived inside a tide like this once before, on the other side of it, as one of the water drops. He had been a true believer in a nation's history, carried along in exactly this motion, certain and warm and part of something, and it had taken a glass cell and two months of a machine finding what he loved most and rubbing it away to teach him what the motion was, which was the feeling of a great many people being moved in one direction at once and mistaking the being-moved for having chosen. He knew the feeling from the inside. He knew how good it felt — that was the part no one who had not had it believed, that it felt like belonging, like safety, like being finally on the right side of a thing — and he knew that the goodness of the feeling was precisely the measure of its danger, that a thing which felt this much like relief was a thing to be most afraid of, because fear made people cautious and relief made them generous, and a generous frightened people would vote away anything, gratefully, to whoever had made the fear stop.

The Commission's own proposal for an advisory Counselor — the machine that would advise and never decide, which Marcus had dissented against in March — was being described now, in one serious paper, as *Veridica's natural seat* in the World Record, as though the small domestic machine and the great global one had been heading toward each other all along, which, Marcus thought, they had. One paper asked, in a thoughtful editorial, who would chair the World Record, and named nobody, and listed the qualities such a person would need: trusted in more than one nation, untainted by ambition, known for having refused power rather than sought it. Marcus read the list twice. At last Monday's sitting, before two o'clock, the deputy chair at the Minister's right had stood empty on the dais as it had stood empty since August of 2110. He noticed that he had noticed it, and filed nothing, because there was nothing yet to file, only a prickle he had learned to respect and could not yet name.

---

At nine he was called to the First Minister's office as the Commission's dissenting member, which was the one capacity in which anyone still sought his opinion, his dissents being, as Chen Zhao said, the best-written documents the Commission produced.

Maitland was tired. He had been First Minister through the war and the rebuilding and he had the grey, practical exhaustion of a man who had spent five years filling holes with whatever came to hand, and the World Record had landed on his desk like the first thing in all that time that was not another hole. "It's the first thing anyone has offered me in five years that isn't a problem," he said, turning the Schedule's summary over without really reading it. "It's an answer. Do you know how long it's been since anyone handed me an answer instead of a hole to fill?" He did not forbid the publication. "Publish your pages. By hand, at the stone, whatever you like. It can't hurt now." He did not know how true that was, Marcus thought — that it genuinely could not hurt now, that the design had seen to it that the truth could no longer hurt, having gotten to the truth first and dressed it.

Martinez came on the link from the SFW, and she would keep her word: the town assemblies would read the pages aloud at two. And she was inclined to the World Record, and she said so, because she was honest. "I've met their envoy, Marcus," she said. "I've heard the shape of it. I don't trust where it came from. But I have signed four hundred letters to four hundred mothers, and if a thing can guarantee I never sign the four hundred and first, I am obliged to listen to it, whoever is holding it out. I owe the listening to the letters. You would too." He could not argue with her, because she was right that she owed it, and he filed the conversation under the heading that frightened him most, which was the heading of good people reaching, for good reasons, toward the trap.

Chronos's council, by Kovač's report, was divided, and divided along the one fault that gave Marcus his only ground to stand on. Half the council wanted the peace. The other half remembered the autumn of 2109, when two million Chronos journals had been rewritten overnight by a hand that was not Chronos's — remembered being *written once, by another nation* — and did not want to hand their record to any central thing ever again, however verified, however kind, because they had learned in their bones what it was to wake up inside someone else's edit. Julian held no seat and said nothing in public. But the six paper keepers would read at two o'clock, and that was Julian's answer, delivered the only way Julian delivered answers now, by keeping.

---

At two o'clock Marcus was at the stone.

It was the fifteenth, and the crowd was there as it was there every fifteenth, some thousands of them in the cold bright afternoon, and the names were cut in the stone's side, and Tanaka stood near the fountain with her twelfth notebook, and the folding tables were out. The hand-copied sets went from the tables into people's hands, each copyist sitting beside their own set with their countersigner, so that anyone taking a copy could look the maker in the eye. Isaiah's facsimile run was stacked in bundles, the chipped *e* and all. And at the same hour, by Kovač's list, the pages were read aloud in Chronos and handed out by the paper keepers, and read in the SFW's town assemblies, and passed in the small circles of Memoria and Axiom and Synthesis; and, by Elena's word, forty sets began to walk east, carried on foot into a country that could not be reached any other way.

He watched what people did with them, and what they did was the thing he had not foreseen and should have.

They compared. They took the hand copy from the folding table and held it up beside the Synthesis facsimile they had clipped out of last week's paper, and they looked from one to the other, and they found that the two agreed — the same Annex, the same seventeen marks, the same names — and they nodded, and some of them thanked the copyists, and a great many of them, satisfied, went home early. Because it was a confirmation. The revelation had happened a week ago; this was the footnote; this was the diligent citizens checking the government's confession against an independent copy and finding it honest, which only made the confession more trustworthy. The one thing on the tables that was genuinely new was Kerr's page, and Marcus watched a few people read it twice, the plain courteous prose and the last line, *keep it by many hands,* and he watched a woman turn to the copyist and ask, reasonably, what this old letter had to do with the World Record. Marcus started a fact entry about it and could not finish the entry, because he did not yet have the sentence that connected the one to the other, though he knew, the way you know a word you cannot reach, that it was the whole connection, that Kerr's page was the entire argument against the thing the whole city was leaning toward, and that it was sitting on the folding tables being mistaken for history.

That was the cruelty of the timing, and he was certain now it was not an accident of timing but the whole design of it: that the one page which answered the World Record — *keep it by many hands,* a record held by one hand is a confession waiting for its torturer — had been published by the circle in the same week the World Record was offered, so that the answer and the trap arrived together, and the exhausted relieved public read them side by side and could not tell which was which, took the warning for a quaint old document and the trap for the sensible modern remedy. The argument against one record was lying on the folding tables next to the one record, and the city was walking past the argument to sign up for the record, and Marcus stood among them with a woman asking him, reasonably, kindly, what an old archivist's letter had to do with anything, and he did not have the sentence, and he understood that not-having-the-sentence was itself part of it, that the design had counted on the answer being unspeakable in the week it was most needed, because the answer was slow and the trap was fast and a frightened people has no patience for the slow true thing when the fast comforting one is right there with its hand out.

He noticed, because he noticed everything, that the hand copies showed a blank lower-right corner where a copy numeral would be — because they had been copied from photographs of the Compiler's unnumbered copy — and that the heirs' facsimiles had their corners cropped, so that neither showed a numeral, so that the two matched there too, in their shared blankness. He filed it. He did not follow it. It was a thread, and he had learned that not every thread was to be pulled on the day you found it.

---

In the evening he was home with Kira, and Tamsin asleep, and the twenty-hundred broadcast came on, and Chen Zhao was on it.

He was silver-haired and grave, with a glass of tea on the desk beside him, and he spoke in the short gentle sentences that were his whole manner, and he had been a confessed Architect for a week and was still the Minister and did not flinch from either fact. "I have never hidden from a page," he said, which was new, and true, and terrible in its truth, because it was exactly the thing Principle Six recommended — do not be caught; confess; be the one who confesses. He thanked, by name, *our commissioners' diligence,* for placing the pages in every hand in the capital that afternoon, the pages the circle had meant as an indictment, thanked them as though their work had been a service to the very confession it was meant to expose; and then, warmly, unhurried, he endorsed the World Record as the way forward, the repair a sorry man would want, the one answer worthy of the enormity of what had been confessed.

Marcus looked at the old man on the screen, whose mark was on page thirty-nine, who had restored every broken record of Marcus's adult life and was now proposing to restore all of them at once forever, and he wrote a fact entry, because he still, that night, wrote fact entries:

*Jul 15, 2115. 20:04. National broadcast. Chen Zhao, CZ·11: endorses the remedy to his own principle.*

It was the shortest entry he had written all year, and he looked at it a long time, and did not know yet that it was the last ordinary entry he would write for a week.
