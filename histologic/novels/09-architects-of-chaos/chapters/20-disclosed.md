# Chapter 20: Disclosed

*Monday, July 8, 2115. The capital*

---

*IO. 8 JULY. PROOF 3.*

Isaiah Okonkwo initialled the third proof at eleven in the morning in the composing room of the printing house that ran his columns, where the air was warm with the smell of ink and hot metal and the presses on the floor below made the long slow heartbeat that he had come, over three years, to find more restful than silence. He was forty-eight. He checked the eight pages line by line against Kira's photographs — the Annex, the Register, Kerr's page — and initialled each sheet with his prefix and the date, because a proof was a document and a document got a prefix, and he had never in his life trusted a thing he had not checked against its source with his own eye.

Seven days to the fifteenth. Three hundred and twelve sets promised in Veridica, forty in Chronos, and Elena's forty for the east. His facsimile run on top of that, the seed crop, true facsimile, the chipped *e* reproduced so faithfully the pressman had asked him twice whether the broken letter was a fault he should fix. *Leave it,* Isaiah had said. *The broken letter is the point. The broken letter is how you know it's real.*

At two o'clock exactly, by the capital's clocks, the printers' radio broke into its hour.

He knew before he understood. He knew from the pressman's face across the room, from the way the man stopped with a form half-lifted and turned up the radio, and then Isaiah was standing too, with the proof still in his hand, listening to the announcer read, in the careful neutral voice they used for the things that mattered most, that the secretariat of the Council of Nations, in Synthesis, had that hour released a document *on behalf of the heirs of the contributors to the Foundation of Certainty.* The street screens, when he got to the window, had it already; and within the hour, he would learn, every nation's news had it, in every language, all at once, at the heroes' own hour, seven days before the heroes' own date.

What they had published was two things, and Isaiah made himself read both the way he read everything, once for the content and once for the shape, even with his hands not quite steady.

The first was facsimiles. The Annex, pages thirty-three through thirty-eight. The Register, page thirty-nine. The same pages he was holding in proof, the same chipped *e*, reproduced clean and large on the screens — and the lower right corner of every page cropped, squared off, so that no copy numeral showed, not an *X,* not an *XI,* nothing that would say which of thirteen copies this was photographed from. He noted the cropping and did not know what it meant yet and wrote it in his book anyway: *corners cropped. no numeral. why.* All seventeen marks were there, the twelve signed and the five struck through, *AK·15 Anselm Kerr* among them for the whole world to read. There was no Kerr's page. Of course there was not; only the Compiler's copy had carried *On Keeping,* clipped to its front; the heirs were publishing from a contributor's copy, and a contributor's copy did not have it. He noted that too.

The second was the *Statement of the Heirs.* Two pages, unsigned by any individual, in the calm humane register he had spent his life learning to distrust. He read it standing at the window.

*We are the children and students of the people who wrote these pages. We did not choose what we inherited. We have chosen what to do with it.*

*Four of the contributors are living. They will answer for themselves.*

*Even the enemy was drawn on the map. We are sorriest for that.*

And then the remedy. A thing called the World Record: every nation's rebuilt or surviving record, merged into one single record, under the authority of Synthesis's Meta-Judge, *so that no nation can ever be written by another again.* It would be funded, the Statement said, by the Liu Wei Endowment, and offered to the Council of Nations for an open vote, so that no nation would be compelled and every nation might choose the safety of a record that could never be tampered with because it would be verified against all the others at once. Attached was a Technical Schedule, sixty dense pages that Isaiah could not read, the architecture of the thing, and his eye snagged on one phrase that recurred through it, a phrase he circled every time it appeared: a *reference source.* He did not know what a reference source was. He marked the Schedule for Kira and sent it to her by runner at twenty to three.

---

He did the arithmetic, which his aunt had told him two months ago he never did, standing in the composing room while the pressmen looked at him and waited to be told whether to stop the run.

Every paper in the world had the Register today. His run would come off the presses on the fifteenth, seven days late to its own story, a reprint of a thing everyone had already read. Everything the circle had meant to reveal had been revealed — in words the heirs chose, on facsimiles the heirs cropped, with a repair already attached, offered as a gift, at the heroes' own hour, so that when the heroes published on the fifteenth it would look not like revelation but like confirmation, like the circle running to catch up with a confession already generously made. He had been scooped by the people he was about to name. It was, he thought, the most complete scoop in the history of the trade, and it had been arranged by the subjects of the story, and that had never happened before and would be studied for a hundred years, and he had a sick professional admiration for it that he was ashamed of and recorded anyway, in the margin, *admiration (noted, resisted).*

Because that was the move he had been watching the design make, in one form or another, his whole life, and had never seen executed at this scale or with this nerve. Confession was the oldest trap in the book — their book, literally, page thirty-one — and he had seen it work in miniature a hundred times, the official who got ahead of the story, the minister who apologised before he could be accused and thereby turned his accusers into the churlish ones, the ungracious ones, the people who would not let a sorry man make amends. He had never seen it done with the whole truth. That was the terrifying refinement of it. Lesser operators confessed to a lesser crime to hide a greater one; the heirs had confessed to the greatest crime imaginable, the design of the whole world, in full, in facsimile, holding nothing back — because they had understood, as only people who had studied humanity for sixty years could understand, that a total confession is more disarming than any partial one, that a man shown the entire horror and then offered a remedy does not go looking for what the confessor left out; he is too grateful, too relieved, too busy being moved by the courage of the confession to notice that the remedy is the crime continued by other means. They had told the truth, all of it, as a weapon. He wrote that in the margin too, and underlined it, because it was the thing his readers would least believe and most needed to.

"Run it," he told the pressman. "On the fifteenth, as planned. It's a reprint now. Reprints still matter. People keep the one with the broken letter."

---

At half past three he walked, because he thought with his feet. The street screens had it, and people stood under them with their hands over their mouths — and then he watched them read on, down past the Register to the remedy, and he watched their faces change, the horror softening into something he had to classify carefully because it was the whole of the thing: relief. He went through the crowd sorting them the way he sorted a hall at a reading. The frightened, who were steadied by the promise of a record that could never again be turned against them. The grateful, who were already grateful to whoever had confessed and offered to fix it. And the ones — more than he wanted — already saying *at last,* already saying that someone sensible had finally proposed the obvious thing, one true record for everyone, no more lying, no more wars of facts. He found almost no one angry. That was the finding. He walked for an hour through the capital on the day the whole design was exposed and found almost no one angry, because the design had exposed itself, gently, with a gift in its hand, and you do not rage at a man confessing with his hat off and a remedy held out.

The Register named Ambassador Sarah Okonkwo. He did not go to Carver Street. He noted, from a distance, walking the other way, that the Statement did not name her, or Chen Zhao, or anyone; it named no individual at all; it said only *four of the contributors are living,* and let the Register's own page do the naming, so that the heirs had the credit of total candour without any one of them standing up to be hated. He wrote that down. *They named no one. The page names them. It's cleaner that way. Everything they do is cleaner that way.*

At five o'clock Chen Zhao confessed first.

His name was on page thirty-nine in every paper in the world, *CZ·11,* and he did not hide from it. He came out onto the Ministry steps before the cameras, grave and fatherly, silver-haired, without notes, and made a short statement, and Isaiah watched it on the printers' screen. The Minister said that the mark CZ·11 was his, that he had written the principle it closed, that he had been a young man of twenty-two when he wrote the essay it grew from and had believed every word, and that he was sorry — he said *sorry* plainly, the way almost no official ever did, and let it sit. And then the turn came, warm, inevitable, the thing Isaiah had been waiting for since two o'clock: *I confess it. Now let us make sure it can never be done to anyone again.* And he commended the heirs' remedy, the World Record, as the way to do it, as the thing a repentant man would want, as the only decent answer to the thing he had helped build. He remained Minister. No one on the steps asked him to resign. One or two called out that he was brave. Isaiah counted it, flatly, on the screen: the second confession with a remedy attached since two o'clock, and the warmest, and the oldest hand.

---

In the evening he went to the kitchen, where Marcus and Kira and Nikolai were, and Kira had the Technical Schedule open across the table, dense and clean, and she said only that it was very well made, that it was the work of someone who understood the problem completely, and that she would need days with it before she could say what it actually did. Marcus asked how they had known the hour, two o'clock, the exact hour.

"A plan carried by many hands isn't a secret," Isaiah said. "We told three hundred and ninety-two households and a President and four borders. They heard the date the way anyone would. They didn't steal it; we gave it away; that was the plan." He set his book down. "Nobody betrayed us. That's the part I keep needing to say out loud. The leak isn't a person. The leak is the method. We chose a method that can't keep a secret, on purpose, because a method that can keep a secret can be bought, and they knew we'd choose it, because it's the only method that's left once you've read the Warning. They just published one week into our open window." He did not accuse anyone. There was no one to accuse.

Then he laid it out, the way he laid out an editorial, in order, because the shape was the whole of it and he needed them to see the shape. First the confession. Then the sorrow. Then the remedy. He had seen it three times now. Maitland, on the eighteenth of December, 2108, with the Concordance. Chen Zhao on the Justice Center steps on the twenty-eighth of May, 2110, with the Commission of Rebuilding — and again this afternoon, on the Ministry steps, with the World Record. And on paper, pages twenty-nine through thirty-two of the Compiler's copy, which the circle had read at the farmhouse table in April. Principle Six, its second sentence, which he said now, aloud, from memory, because he had read it enough times to have it:

*"When a truth cannot be hidden, disclose it first, in your own words, with your own remedy attached. The public forgives a system that confesses and repairs; it never forgives one that is caught."*

---

Late, at his own table, Isaiah wrote the entry.

*IO. 8 JULY. 14:00. Disclosed by: the named. Remedy: attached.*

He looked at it for a long time. The whole circle knew that page now; they had read it together under a lamp in April; they could all of them have recited the second sentence. But he was the one who said it aloud, because saying a thing aloud was his trade and because somebody had to be the one who named what had happened to them, plainly, for the record, so that it was written down by at least one hand that understood it.

*Disclose first, in your own words, with your own remedy attached.*

He underlined nothing. He did not need to. He put the book in his coat, over his heart, where he kept what mattered, and went to bed in a city that was, almost all of it, relieved.
