Chapter 15: Copy X

Monday, May 27, 2115. The capital

May 27, 2115. Commission chamber. Present: the Minister; the commissioners; the technical adviser. Status: sitting.

It was five weeks since Dmitri had read the names off page thirty-nine in a farmhouse kitchen, and Marcus Chen sat at the far end of the long table in the seat of the dissenting member and spent the whole morning in a room with CZ·11 and said nothing.

Chen Zhao chaired, as he chaired every Monday, silver-haired and warm, his tea in a glass in a metal holder, delighted. He called down the table to Marcus near the start, as he always did — "Cousin, how is Tamsin? Walking everyone off their feet, I'm told" — and Marcus said she was well, and it was true, and the word cousin went by in the room's affection the way it always did, and Marcus carried it differently now than he had in March. The Minister said, at the break, as he had said since March, that your chair was still waiting, with a tilt of his head toward the empty deputy chair at his right on the dais, and Marcus thanked him and did not sit in it. And once, presenting the spring figures for the Civic Record, the Minister used the word restore — the record had restored its backlog of winter testimony, he said, the clerks had caught up — and Marcus heard page thirty under it, arrange to be the one who restores it, and did not look up from his notes, because looking up would have been saying something, and he had not decided what saying something would be for.

That was the whole of what he did all morning: he did not say CZ·11. He had known the mark for five weeks. He had the proof of the man's principle and the mark beside his name in ink and a witness, Singh, who had watched him restore the record of a nation twice; and he sat ten feet from him across a table and ate the arithmetic of it in silence, and voted his dissent on the Counselor study for the record, as he always did, and wrote in his list, under UNCHECKED, in the middle of the sitting, the only line he wrote all morning:

What would saying it be for?

He could not answer it. That was the trouble. Saying it would feel like justice and would not be justice; it would be one broken man naming one old minister in a room, and the design had a place for that, had a place for everything, and he had learned in the war that the thing which felt most like striking a blow was most often the blow the design wanted struck. So he kept the mark in his list and the question under it and said nothing, and his prose had its old holes — he reached, during the long dull middle of the sitting, for the first words Chen Zhao had ever said to him, in 2106, when the Minister had appointed a young restorer to a Commission, and the words were not there, a clean socket where a sentence had been, and he wrote (Minister, first words: lost) in the margin and left it.

Wei was at the technical table. He took his notes with his right hand, as he always did; the old scar on the back of his left hand had faded over five years to a thin white line, nearly gone, the kind of mark you would not notice unless you had spent five years not looking at it. Marcus had known since the farmhouse exactly where that hand had been at twenty-one minutes past two on the fifteenth of November, 2106, bandaged, on a side door. At the end of the sitting Wei inclined his head to him, a few degrees, the acknowledgement he had given every Monday for five years, and Marcus returned it, and that was the whole of their exchange, as it had been the whole of their exchange for five years.

In the evening Kira was at the kitchen table with the hand copies of the Register spread out, cross-referencing marks to principles, and Tamsin was asleep down the hall, and at twenty to nine there was a knock at the door.

Marcus opened it. Thomas Wei stood on the landing in his coat.

He did not come in further than the table. He stepped inside enough for the door to close behind him and no further, and he set on the table a flat bundle, tied crosswise with cloth tape, worn at the folds.

"You'll want to check it against hers," Wei said. "Two copies are a pattern."

Marcus untied the tape. The pages inside were typed, and the es were bitten the same small bite, the chipped machine, and in the lower right corner of the first page, where the Compiler's copy had carried nothing, this one carried a small roman numeral: X. Copy ten. He turned the pages, and Kira came round the table and began to count them the way she counted everything.

Page one, intact — no torn strip; the contents entire, all the way down, IV, V, VI, the lines the farmhouse had reconstructed from a reunited strip, here whole on one unbroken sheet. The Introduction, pages two through four. Principles One through Six in their numbered runs. At the foot of page twenty-two, the end of Principle Four, a mark in the chipped type: WZ·10. The Annex. And page thirty-nine, the Register, the same seventeen marks, the same twelve signatures in brown ink — the same signatures, Marcus saw, the identical hands, because of course each of the thirteen copies had been signed by all twelve, so that the Register was countersigned thirteen times over and every copy carried every signatory's own ink.

"Twenty-seven pages," Kira said. "Missing pages thirteen to seventeen. Missing nineteen. Missing twenty-three to twenty-eight." She looked up. "Principle Three. Principle Five. And nineteen."

Marcus understood the gaps as she named them, and did not say so aloud, because Wei was standing by the door and some understandings were better kept off the air. Pages thirteen through seventeen and twenty-three through twenty-eight were the pages that had come through Helena Kovač's intake at Ashford in November of 2108 — deposited behind locked doors that had opened, on the camera, with no visitor standing at them, Book 6's small impossible miracle, the inside hand that fed the fragments out. And page nineteen was the page that had turned up in the Stabilisation Order's own file in 2110, the page Tanaka had read aloud in a linen room, the page that had reached Maya through a channel no one could trace. The inside route had always been one bundle, this bundle, copy X, Wei's father's copy, fed out of it a few pages at a time over seven years by the son, to the exact people who would need them, at the exact moments they would need them. The whole of the manuscript thread, from the other side.

Marcus had spent nine years assembling the story of how the fragments reached them — the deposit behind locked doors with no visitor on the camera, the page in the Stabilisation Order's file, each one a small impossibility he had filed under UNCHECKED and never solved — and the solution had just walked up his stairs and set itself on his table and tied the whole thread off in a single knot. One man. One copy. One patient hand, inside the design, feeding its own undoing out a few pages at a time, timed to the hour each piece would do the most good, for seven years, at a risk Marcus could not calculate because he did not know what the design did to the ones who betrayed it from inside and suspected it was worse than anything it did to its enemies. He looked at Wei standing by the door, courteous, graying, giving nothing away, and he understood that he was in the presence of either the most patient act of conscience he had ever encountered or the final move of a design that had a place even for its own apparent betrayal, and that he could not tell which, and that Wei had arranged it so that he could not tell which, because a man who gave you certainty was a man you could dismiss and a man who gave you only the facts was a man you had to carry.

"Why now?" Marcus said.

"Because it's finished," Wei said. "And I would like it to have been finished by you."

Marcus looked at him. "The others are going to run east. You know that. Will you go with them?"

Wei smiled, faintly, and shook his head, once. He did not say no and he did not explain; the single shake of the head was the whole answer, and it was not a thing done for effect, it was a man declining to dress a decision in words. He would not flee. He had brought the last of his father's copy up a flight of stairs to the one man he wanted to have it, and he was going to stand where he was and answer for the whole of it.

He put his hand on the doorframe.

"Singh is well?" he said.

"Yes," said Marcus.

"Good," said Wei, and went, and Marcus heard him go down the stairs lightly, taking the last low flight two steps at a time, the way a much younger man goes down stairs, the way a man goes down stairs who has set a thing down at last and feels the lack of its weight.

Marcus stood at the open door and listened to the footsteps go, and did not call after them, and thought — the thought arriving whole, the way the true ones did — that whatever Thomas Wei was, he had just become the one Architect's heir who would stand in the open and answer, the one who would not run east, the one who had carried his father's whole guilt up a flight of stairs and handed it to the man most entitled to use it against him and then gone down the stairs two at a time, lighter. Singh was well, and he had wanted to know it, and that was the most Marcus would ever understand of him: that somewhere in the design's own son there was a man who had spared the one witness it needed dead and had wanted, nine years later, to be told she was well. It was not enough to forgive. It was not meant to be enough to forgive. It was only true, and Marcus had learned to carry the true things whether or not they were enough, and he carried this one down the dark stairwell with his eyes, until the street door closed below and the footsteps were gone.

Marcus telephoned Kovač, and she came across the city at a quarter past eleven with the Compiler's copy in its card folder and, in a second folder, the intake pages — pages thirteen through seventeen and twenty-three through twenty-eight, copy X, in the chipped type with the X numeral, the pages she had held in her own custody since they came through the Ashford intake in 2108. For page nineteen Kira used the hand copy Tanaka had made for her in the war; the typed original was in Tanaka's case book across the lake. And then the three of them did the slow work, page by page, from page one to page thirty-nine, laying copy X's sheet beside the Compiler's copy's sheet under the lamp, reading them against each other a line at a time, the way the copies were made, two to a page.

They matched. Word for word, page for page, from the title to the Register. And the intake pages, slotted into copy X's own gaps, filled them exactly: the same numeral in the corner, the same weight of paper, the same bitten e, Principle Three sitting down into pages thirteen through seventeen as though they had never left, Principle Five into twenty-three through twenty-eight. Two copies, made on one machine in 1966, scattered across two custodians and sixty years, laid back together on a kitchen table, agreeing in every character. Kovač initialled each match in her register as they went, the copyist's discipline, so that the authentication itself was kept by many hands. When it was done, a little before two, she signed copy X into her own custody beside the Compiler's copy, with its intake pages; and Kira, before any of it was packed away, photographed every page of copy X, because they worked from photographs and let the originals rest in one trusted pair of hands.

In his list, late, Marcus wrote the heirs he could name. WZ·10 — Wei, T. TK·07 — Mera, A. And then, because he would not pretend to know what he did not, he wrote the other ten marks in a column and put a question mark after each one, in the same hand, giving no mark more weight than another — HV, YP, LW, SO, AV, MT, JM, VS, CZ, RA, each with its question mark, his cousin's among them, undistinguished, one query in a row of queries, which was the only honest way to write a thing he suspected and could not prove.

At two o'clock Kira was asleep at the table with her head on her folded arms, the photographs stacked and squared beside her, and Marcus sat with his list and wrote the entry he had not written at the sitting that morning, the plain fact of the day.

May 27, 2115. Home. Involved parties: Wei, T. Object: copy X. Verification: two independent copies, authenticated page for page. Status: pattern.

And below it, after a space, smaller, the thing he still could not decide, which was not a fact and so did not go in the entry, but under it, where he kept the things that were not yet anything:

CZ·11: not yet said.
