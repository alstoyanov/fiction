# Histologic — Promotional Kit

**Purpose:** reusable marketing assets for the nine-book *Histologic* series — cover-art prompts, ad copy and hooks, and a launch/promotion strategy. Draft, Oct 2026. Adapt freely.

**One-line positioning:** *Orwell's surveillance, Asimov's science of history, and the quiet horror of a machine that decides what is true — a complete nine-book saga about one record versus many witnesses.*

**House style for all art/copy:** clean, clinical, ordered, institutional. **Never steampunk** — no brass, gears, cogs, goggles, gaslight, Victoriana. The future here is bureaucratic and cold, not retro-mechanical.

---

## 1. Positioning & core messaging

**Logline (series):**
In a future where history is an exact science and every citizen's memory is written by the state, a broken true believer and a handful of people the record says are dead discover that their world — its wars, its heroes, even its reforms — was designed, and set out to replace one official truth with a truth kept by many hands.

**Elevator pitch (back-of-napkin):**
History is settled by a supercomputer called The Judge and broadcast straight into your head, so you don't *learn* your nation's past — you simply *know* it. Doubt is a disease, and it is cured. But the whole system, down to its enemies and its escape hatches, was written in a secret manuscript sixty years ago, and the people who wrote it are still running it. *Histologic* follows the fall and refusal of Marcus Chen across nine books, from the white room where they take your certainty to the day the machine finally dies — and the harder question of what you build in its place.

**Comparison titles (for pitching / "if you liked…"):**
- *1984* (George Orwell) — control through information
- *Foundation* (Isaac Asimov) — history as an engineered science
- *The Matrix* — a managed reality you're invited to doubt
- *A Memory Called Empire* (Arkady Martine) — memory, empire, selfhood *(modern comp)*
- *Black Mirror* / *Severance* — clinical near-future dread *(screen comp for tone)*

**The hooks that sell it (pick per channel):**
- "What if believing your nation's version of history was mandatory — and enforced by a machine in your skull?"
- "The record cannot lie. That was the first lie."
- "They don't rewrite history. They rewrite the people who remember it."
- "One record. Many witnesses. Only one of them can win."
- "Every reformer was anticipated. Including this one."

**Themes as selling points:** truth vs. freedom; memory vs. the record; whether belief is real if its conditions were manufactured; the reform a system was *designed* to absorb; the cost of certainty.

**Series status advantage:** the saga is **complete** (3 short stories + 6 novels). Lead with *"The complete series — start today, finish on your terms."* Completeness is a major purchase driver for binge readers and a strong rapid-release lever (see §4).

## 2. Cover-art prompts

### 2.1 Series art direction (give this to any designer or image model)
- **Mood:** cold, exact, oppressive calm. Institutional sublime. A world that is *tidy* and therefore frightening.
- **Palette:** bone white, paper cream, cold grey, slate; a single accent per book — a "verdict" red or a clinical cyan. High key, low saturation.
- **Recurring motifs:** the neural interface (a small seam/light behind the ear); the gray memorial stone; redaction bars; a single official checkmark or wax-less seal; a chipped lowercase **"e"** from one typewriter; the lakeshore border where two broadcasts overlap; a page multiplying into many. Use **one** motif per cover — restraint is the brand.
- **Typography:** austere grotesque (think Helvetica/Univers), stamped or embossed; small-caps series line "HISTOLOGIC · BOOK N"; title set like an official document header.
- **Composition:** lots of negative space, centered or rule-of-thirds single subject, no clutter. Should read at thumbnail size.
- **Avoid (negative prompt for every cover):** `steampunk, brass, gears, cogs, goggles, Victorian, gaslamp, chrome spaceships, neon cyberpunk sprawl, cluttered collage, busy background, photorealistic celebrity faces, lens flare, text artifacts, watermark`

### 2.2 Per-book prompts

**Series collection cover (the 3 short stories / omnibus):**
> …accent: cold cyan. A single human head in clean profile, the back of the skull opening like a filing drawer, one neat index card sliding out; the card is blank. Silhouette against a seamless white void.

**Book 1 — *The Believer's Fall*** (accent: verdict red)
> A man seen from behind in a spotless white interview room, a faint light-seam glowing behind his right ear; stamped across the image like an official verdict, a single red figure: **73%**. Clean, devotional, doomed.

**Book 2 — *The Stolen Fact*** (accent: cyan)
> A vast orderly grid of identical index cells receding into white; one cell is empty — a clean black gap where a fact used to be. A woman's small silhouette stands before the gap. Cold, architectural.

**Book 3 — *The Divided Truth*** (accent: red/cyan split)
> Three identical silhouettes standing in a row; the central face fractures into a ghosted double-exposure where two versions of the same man fail to line up — a perceptual fact-storm rendered as doubling, **not weather**. A thin seam of light marks the border between them.

**Book 4 — *The Correction*** (accent: clinical white-on-white, faintest grey)
> A single glass cell in an endless white ward; inside, a seated figure is dissolving at the edges into the white, as if being gently erased. Almost no colour. The horror is the cleanliness.

**Book 5 — *The Lost Hour*** (accent: red)
> A clean institutional timeline/ruler marked in hours; the span **14:00–15:00** is covered by a single red redaction bar. Beneath it, faint, a chalk-outline suggestion of a body. Precise, forensic.

**Book 6 — *The Distributed Truth*** (accent: cyan)
> A single official page caught mid-air, multiplying into a fan of many identical pages held up by many faint hands; in the far background, a lakeshore pier doubled against itself in the overlap. Hope rendered as repetition.

**Book 7 — *Battle of Truths*** (accent: two reds, opposed)
> Two maps of the same river, drawn in two different courses, overlaid so they conflict; where the lines cross, the paper looks torn. Two austere national seals press toward each other from opposite edges. Cold cartographic tension.

**Book 8 — *Battle of Blood*** (accent: red going to black)
> A low flat coastline at dawn; a vast, cathedral-like machine core on the horizon with its lights going out one bank at a time. In the foreground, the back of a soldier's head — smooth, **no light-seam behind the ear**. The one war the system can't win with lies.

**Book 9 — *Architects of Chaos*** (finale; accent: red, most dramatic)
> A single typed page: a list of names/signatures, most of them under heavy redaction bars — and one bar being peeled back to reveal a name. The typed text shows one telltale chipped lowercase "e". Grander scale, deep shadow, the saga's reveal. Alt: a plain waist-high gray memorial stone in an empty plaza at dusk, fresh names freshly cut into its side.

### 2.3 Alt / A-B test concepts (whole series)
- **Typographic-only line:** title as a stamped official document, a single redaction bar through one key word. Cheapest to produce, often the strongest literary-SF look.
- **The interface seam:** extreme close-up of skin behind an ear with a hairline luminous seam — unifying device across all nine spines.
- **Spine strategy:** nine spines that line up to form one continuous gray stone with names cut across all of them — rewards the collector, looks great in a series carousel.

## 3. Short adverts & hooks

### 3.1 One-line hooks (per book — for ads, carousels, series page)
1. **The Believer's Fall** — He reported the woman he loved to prove his faith. The machine convicted *him*.
2. **The Stolen Fact** — She built the system that keeps the truth. Then a true thing she entered simply… vanished.
3. **The Divided Truth** — Three brothers, one face, and the discovery that the storms tearing minds apart at the border are being *made*.
4. **The Correction** — They don't imprison doubters. They cure them. Seven minds learn to look cured while staying themselves.
5. **The Lost Hour** — One hour is missing from the history of the world, a dead man is accused of the murder inside it, and forty people watched him do a thing he never did.
6. **The Distributed Truth** — A war of facts, an offer to betray a friend, and a man who answers with the one weapon the design never planned for: *everyone.*
7. **Battle of Truths** — To stop a war he cuts one lie out of a nation's memory — and learns the two enemies were always one machine.
8. **Battle of Blood** — The enemy has no interface. No lie can turn them. They have come for the machine that decides what is true, and the only way to save the world is to let it die.
9. **Architects of Chaos** — The dead come back, the designers are named, and they offer the world the one thing it has always wanted: a single, perfect, shared truth. Refusing it may be the last free act left.

### 3.2 Back-cover / retail blurb (Book 1 — reader's entry point)
> Marcus Chen believes. He believes in Veridica, in the Record, in The Judge that settles every dispute and the gentle broadcast that lets every citizen simply *know* their nation's history. So when he discovers that the woman he loves is an outlaw — someone who *doubts* — he does the faithful thing. He reports her.
>
> The Judge convicts him anyway. Seventy-three per cent contaminated. On his way to be cured, Marcus begins to notice the one thing a perfect record was never supposed to allow: a question.
>
> *The Believer's Fall* opens **Histologic**, a complete nine-book saga about truth, memory, and the machinery of belief — for readers of *1984*, *Foundation*, and *A Memory Called Empire*.

### 3.3 Series blurb (for the Amazon series page / box set)
> History is no longer argued. It is computed, verified, and broadcast directly into memory, so that no citizen need wonder what happened — they simply know. Nations are defined not by land or language but by which version of the past they believe, and where those versions overlap, minds come apart. To doubt is to be ill. To be ill is to be corrected.
>
> But the whole of it — the rival nations, the wars at the borders, even the brave reforms that promise to fix it — was designed, decades ago, by the authors of a manuscript that officially never existed. **Histologic** is the complete story of the people who find that manuscript, and who must decide whether a world can be free if its freedoms were written in advance. Nine books. One question: who holds the record?

## 4. Promotion strategy

### 4.1 Who this is for (audience)
- **Primary:** adult literary/dystopian SF readers — the *1984 / Fahrenheit 451 / The Handmaid's Tale* crowd who also read modern SF (Martine, Chambers' darker cousins, *The Every*).
- **Secondary:** "science of history / grand-design" fans (*Foundation*, *Dune*-adjacent), and clinical near-future TV fans (*Severance*, *Black Mirror*) who read.
- **Tertiary / timely angle:** readers drawn to themes of misinformation, record vs. memory, and institutional truth. (Lean on theme, not partisan politics — the books are about the *machinery* of belief, which keeps it broad.)

### 4.2 The funnel (how a stranger becomes a finisher)
1. **Top:** free short-story collection (Book 0) as reader magnet — perma-free on retailers and/or email-gated via your newsletter.
2. **Convert:** Book 1 (*The Correction*) priced low (0.99–2.99) or in Kindle Unlimited.
3. **Binge:** Books 2–9 at standard price; **a box set / omnibus** (e.g. Books 4–6 and 7–9, or a complete set) as the high-value SKU.
4. **Retain:** newsletter for the next thing (a prequel novella about the Architects / the Fracture Years is an obvious magnet).

### 4.3 Launch model — rapid release (recommended, because the series is complete)
The single biggest advantage you have is that it's *finished*. Rapid release exploits Amazon's 30-day new-release visibility and reader binge behavior:
- **Pre-launch (4–8 wks):** finalize covers, blurbs, metadata; build/prime the newsletter with the free stories; line up reviews (see §4.5).
- **Release cadence:** publish one book every 2–4 weeks, or "all stories + Book 1 now, then one novel every fortnight." Each release re-triggers also-boughts and re-engages the list.
- **Finale push:** treat Book 9 as an event — box set goes live the same week; run price promos on Book 1 to pull new readers into the now-complete funnel.
- **Evergreen:** after launch, Book 1 lives at a promo-able price for BookBub-style features.

### 4.4 Channels & tactics
- **Amazon KDP first.** Decide **KU exclusive vs. wide**: KU suits binge SF and page-read income; wide (Apple/Kobo/B&N/Google) suits a literary series with international reach. A common play: **KU for the novels during launch**, wide for the free stories if perma-free is wanted (KDP can't do free easily without price-matching).
- **Metadata is marketing.** See §4.6 for categories/keywords — this is where most indie discoverability actually happens.
- **Price promotions:** stack a Book 1 $0.99 countdown with a newsletter feature + genre promo sites (Freebooksy/Bargain Booksy, Robin Reads, Fussy Librarian; **BookBub Featured Deal** is the grail — apply repeatedly).
- **Reader communities (participate, don't spam):** r/printSF, r/dystopia, r/booksuggestions (answer "books like 1984/Foundation" honestly), Goodreads SF groups, BookTok/Bookstagram via *content* (see below) not ads first.
- **Content marketing that fits the brand:** the world lends itself to shareable artifacts —
  - "redacted" quote cards (a sentence with one word blacked out);
  - a mock "citizen's fact entry" or "correction notice" graphic;
  - the recurring **gray-stone** image with a different cut name each week;
  - short "worldbuilding explainer" posts (What is a fact storm? What is The Judge? Why doesn't removing the interface just set you free?).
- **Paid, once organic signals are good:** Amazon Sponsored Products (keyword-target the comps), then small Facebook/BookBook ads to the free magnet. Don't scale paid until the funnel converts organically.
- **Audio (later):** clinical first-person narration suits this series; a strong audiobook of Book 1 widens reach. Consider once ebook traction is proven.

### 4.5 Reviews & social proof (do this *before* launch, and ethically)
- **Never buy or fake reviews, never swap for positive reviews** — it violates retailer terms and torches trust. Ask only for *honest* ones.
- **ARC team:** recruit 20–50 advance readers from your newsletter and SF review communities; distribute via **BookSirens, StoryOrigin, or BookFunnel**; for trade/library reach consider **NetGalley** (co-op listings are cheaper).
- **Editorial angle:** pitch book bloggers/BookTubers in literary SF; a few thoughtful long reviews outperform many drive-by ones for this genre.
- **Goodreads:** set up series + author profile early; a pre-launch giveaway can seed early shelf-adds.

### 4.6 Amazon categories & keywords (starter set — refine with live data)
- **Likely categories:** Dystopian SF; Political SF; Hard SF; Literary Fiction (crossover); Cyberpunk (loosely); Technothriller (for Books 6–8). Use KDP's 10 category slots and the "browse category" email request to add niche ones.
- **Keyword phrases to test (7 slots, mix intent + comp):** `dystopian science fiction series complete`, `books like 1984`, `surveillance state thriller`, `memory manipulation sci-fi`, `political dystopia saga`, `mandatory belief dystopia`, `truth and propaganda novel`.
- **Blurb SEO:** work "history," "memory," "surveillance," "dystopia," "truth," and a comp title into the first two lines of the retail description (that's what gets indexed and skimmed).

### 4.7 Taglines & reusable assets (grab bag)
- Series tagline options: **"Who holds the record?"** · **"The truth, verified."** · **"One record. Many witnesses."** · **"Certainty is care."** *(the last is in-world sinister — great for ironic ads)*
- Hashtags: #Histologic #dystopianfiction #scifiseries #indieSF #bookstagram #BookTok #1984vibes #foundationseries
- Elevator one-liner for bios/pitches: *"A complete nine-book dystopia where history is an exact science — and the reforms were planned."*

### 4.8 90-day sketch
- **Days 1–30:** covers + metadata final; newsletter + free stories live; ARC team reading; Goodreads/author profiles up; start brand-appropriate content (redaction cards, worldbuilding posts) 2–3×/week.
- **Days 31–60:** rapid-release novels begin; collect honest reviews; first Book 1 price promo + genre-site features; begin small Amazon Sponsored Products on comp keywords.
- **Days 61–90:** complete the release; launch the **box set** and the **Book 9 "finale" event**; apply for BookBub; evaluate KU vs. wide on real numbers; plan the prequel novella as the next magnet.

---

*Ethics note: all of the above relies on honest claims, honest reviews, and legitimate promo services. Don't fabricate reviews, endorsements, or quotes, and don't imply awards or press the series hasn't earned. The world of these books is about what happens when a record lies — the marketing shouldn't.*
