# Yomi: The Land of the Dead: Lore

## The Myth
At the beginning, when Heaven and Earth first separated, gods came into being one after another in the High Plain of Heaven (*Takamagahara*). The last pair were **Izanagi**, "he who invites", and **Izanami**, "she who invites". The heavenly gods gave them a jewelled spear, *Ame-no-nuboko*, and told them to make and fix the drifting land below. They stood on the **Floating Bridge of Heaven** (*Ame-no-Ukihashi*), lowered the spear, and stirred the brine until it curdled, *koworo, koworo*. When they drew it up, the brine that dripped from its point piled up into the first island, **Onogoro**.

They went down to the island, raised a great pillar and a hall, and agreed to marry by walking around the pillar from opposite sides. When they met, Izanami spoke first: *"How lovely, what a fine young man!"* Only then did Izanagi answer. The child of that union was **Hiruko**, a leech child, and they set him adrift in a boat of reeds. The heavenly gods divined the cause: the woman had spoken first. They walked the pillar again, and this time Izanagi spoke first. From that union Izanami gave birth to the eight great islands of Japan, and then to the gods of the sea, the wind, the trees, the mountains, the plains and the rivers.

Last she gave birth to **Kagutsuchi**, the god of fire, and his birth burned her. As she lay dying, gods were born even from her sickness. Then she left the world. Izanagi crawled weeping around her head and her feet, and from his tears another goddess was born. He buried her on Mount Hiba, between Izumo and Hōki. Then he drew his **ten-hand-length sword** and cut off the head of the fire child. From the blood on the sword more gods were born, among them **Takemikazuchi**, the thunder-sword god.

Izanagi longed for his wife, and followed her into **Yomi**. She met him at the door of its palace hall. He begged her to come back, because the lands they had made were not finished. She answered that he had come too late, because she had already **eaten at the hearth of Yomi** (*yomotsu-hegui*). Still, she would ask the gods of Yomi. *"Do not look at me,"* she said, and went inside. She was gone so long that he broke off the end tooth of the comb in his left hair-bunch, lit it, and went in. He saw her body **swarming with maggots**, with **eight thunder gods** seated on her head, breast, belly, loins, hands and feet.

He fled. *"You have shamed me,"* she cried, and sent the **Hags of Yomi** (*Yomotsu-shikome*) after him. He threw down his vine headdress, and it became **grapes**. While the hags ate, he ran. He broke and threw the comb from his right hair-bunch, and it became **bamboo shoots**, and again they stopped to eat. Then she sent the eight thunder gods with **fifteen hundred warriors of Yomi**. He ran, swinging his sword behind him. At the foot of the pass between the worlds, **Yomotsu Hirasaka**, he found a peach tree. He threw **three peaches** at them, and they all fled back.

Last of all came **Izanami herself**. Izanagi rolled a **boulder so great that a thousand men could not move it** (*chibiki-no-iwa*) across the pass. With the stone between them they broke their marriage. She said: *"My beloved husband, if you do this, I will strangle a thousand of the people of your land every day."* He answered: *"My beloved wife, if you do this, I will raise fifteen hundred birthing huts every day."* So every day a thousand people die, and fifteen hundred are born. Izanami became the great goddess of Yomi.

*"I have been to a foul and polluted land,"* said Izanagi, *"and I must purify myself."* At a river mouth in Tsukushi, at **Awagihara** by **Tachibana** in **Himuka**, he threw away his staff, belt, bag, robe, trousers, hat and bracelets, and each became a god. He went into the middle of the stream, because the upper current was too fast and the lower too slow. Gods of misfortune were born from the filth he washed off, and gods of setting things right after them. Last he washed his face. From his **left eye** was born **Amaterasu**, goddess of the sun. From his **right eye**, **Tsukuyomi**, god of the moon. From his **nose**, **Susanoo**, god of the storm. *"At the end of all my begetting I have three noble children,"* he said. He gave Amaterasu his necklace of jewels and the rule of Heaven, Tsukuyomi the realm of night, and Susanoo the plain of the sea.

## How the Album Tells It
- **Point of view:** Izanagi narrates most tracks. Izanami speaks in her own voice in tracks 2, 6 and 8. Track 2 is sung from the leech child's side.
- **Where we follow the sources, and where we take liberties:**
  - **The dialogue** in tracks 2, 5, 6 and 8 is closely based on the *Kojiki*.
  - **Track 2** gives Hiruko an interior voice. The sources only say he was set adrift. The medieval tradition that he washed ashore and became the god **Ebisu** is only hinted at, in the last line.
  - **Track 3** doesn't dwell on the physical detail of the burns. The *Kojiki* is explicit, and we keep it dignified.
  - **Track 5** invents the sensory detail of the road down to Yomi. The *Kojiki* says only that he followed her.
  - **Track 9** compresses the long list of gods born in the purification.
- **What we leave out:**
  - **The full lists of islands and gods.** Only a few are named, for the rhythm.
  - **Awashima,** the second failed child.

## Sensitivity Notes
- **Izanagi and Izanami are worshipped today.** Izanagi at Izanagi Jingū on Awaji and at Taga Taisha. Izanami at Hana-no-Iwaya shrine in Kumano, traditionally her burial place, and elsewhere. The album retells the *Kojiki*'s own account and doesn't mock or sexualise them.
- **Izanami's decay** is canonical and central to the myth. We keep it as horror and grief, not gore for its own sake. She is a goddess wronged by being looked at, not a monster.
- **The fire birth** is told with restraint (see "How the Album Tells It").
- **The purification (*misogi*)** is a living Shinto practice. Track 9 treats it as sacred.
