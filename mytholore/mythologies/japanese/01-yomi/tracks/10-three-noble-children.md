## 1. Story Behind the Song
Last of all, Izanagi washed his face. When he washed his **left eye**, there was born **Amaterasu Ōmikami**, the great goddess who shines in Heaven, the **sun**. When he washed his **right eye**, there was born **Tsukuyomi**, the god of the **moon**. When he washed his **nose**, there was born **Takehaya Susanoo**, the swift, impetuous god of the **storm**.

Izanagi rejoiced. *"I have been begetting child after child,"* he said, *"and at the end of my begetting I have gotten **three noble children**"* (*mihashira no uzu no miko*). He took the **necklace of jewels** from his neck, shook it so that it rang, and gave it to Amaterasu: *"You shall rule the High Plain of Heaven."* To Tsukuyomi he gave the **dominion of the night**. To Susanoo he gave the **plain of the sea**.

So the album's descent ends in light. From a death, a grief and a forbidden glance come the gods who will rule the sky, the night and the sea. Every later story of Japanese myth flows from these three. But the *Kojiki* goes straight on to say that Susanoo would not rule the sea. He wept and howled until his beard reached his chest, until the green mountains withered and the rivers dried. The song's last line points to that, and to the next album. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §§X–XI.)
