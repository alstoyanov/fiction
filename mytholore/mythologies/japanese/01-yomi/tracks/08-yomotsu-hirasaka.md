## 1. Story Behind the Song
At the foot of **Yomotsu Hirasaka**, the slope between the land of the living and the land of the dead, Izanagi found a **peach tree**. He took **three peaches** and threw them at the army of Yomi, and they all turned and fled. Grateful, he told the peaches that as they had helped him, they should help every living person in trouble, and he gave them a god's name, Ōkamuzumi. Peaches have been charms against evil ever since.

Last of all came **Izanami herself**. Izanagi took a **boulder so great that a thousand men would be needed to move it** (*chibiki-no-iwa*) and pulled it across the pass. Standing on either side of the stone, they broke their marriage. Izanami said: *"My beloved husband, if you do this, I will strangle to death **a thousand** of the people of your land every day."* Izanagi answered: *"My beloved wife, if you do this, I will set up **fifteen hundred** birthing huts every day."* **That is why, every day, a thousand people die and fifteen hundred are born.** Izanami was called the **Great Goddess of Yomi** from then on. The *Kojiki* adds that the pass is the Ifuya slope in the land of Izumo.

This is the heart of the album. Two lovers, who together made the world, now stand on either side of a stone and divide it between death and birth. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §IX.)
