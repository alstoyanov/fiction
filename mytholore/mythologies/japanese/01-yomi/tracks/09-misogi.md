## 1. Story Behind the Song
*"I have been to a hideous and polluted land,"* said Izanagi, *"so I must purify my body."* He went to a river mouth in the far west, at **Awagihara** by **Tachibana** in **Himuka**, in the land of Tsukushi, and there he performed the first **misogi**, the purification by water that is still a Shinto rite today. As he threw away everything he had carried out of Yomi, each thing became a god: his **staff**, his **belt**, his **bag**, his **robe**, his **trousers**, his **hat**, and the **bracelets** from his left and right arms. Twelve gods came from what he threw away.

Then he stepped into the river. *"The upper stream is too swift,"* he said, *"the lower stream is too sluggish,"* and he went down into the **middle**. From the filth of Yomi he washed off were born **gods of misfortune**, the gods of the great and the many calamities. Straight after them were born **gods who set things right**, to straighten what those calamities bend. As he washed at the bottom of the water, in its middle, and at its surface, the gods of the sea and the three gods of **Sumiyoshi** were born, who guard sailors to this day.

The song is the album's exhale after the horror: grief washed away in cold water, death becoming something that can be cleansed. It ends at the moment he bends to wash his face, and what that brings forth is the final track. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §X.)
