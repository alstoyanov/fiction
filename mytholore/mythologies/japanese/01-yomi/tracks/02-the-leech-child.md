## 1. Story Behind the Song
Izanagi and Izanami went down to Onogoro, raised a **heavenly pillar** and a great hall, and agreed to marry. Izanagi told his sister to walk around the pillar from the right while he walked from the left, and that they would join where they met. When they met, **Izanami spoke first**: *"Ana-ni-yashi, e-otoko-o!"* ("Ah, how lovely, what a fine young man!"). Only then did Izanagi answer, *"Ana-ni-yashi, e-otome-o!"* ("Ah, how lovely, what a fine young woman!"), though he said it was not right that the woman should speak first.

Their first child was **Hiruko**, the "leech child", who was born without bones and could not stand. They put him in a **boat of reeds** and let the sea take him away. The next child, the island Awashima, was not counted among their children either. They went back up to Heaven and asked the gods what had gone wrong. The gods divined the answer: because the woman had spoken first. So the two went down again, walked around the pillar again, and this time Izanagi spoke first. From that union Izanami gave birth to the **Great Eight Islands**: Awaji, Iyo, Oki, Tsukushi, Iki, Tsushima, Sado, and the great island of Yamato. After them came the gods of sea and wind, trees and mountains.

The *Kojiki* says nothing more of Hiruko. This song gives him a voice: the first child, abandoned because of words. Much later, medieval tradition said the reed boat washed ashore, and the child became **Ebisu**, the smiling god of fishermen and luck. The last line nods to that. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §§IV–V.)
