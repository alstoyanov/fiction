## 1. Story Behind the Song
With Izanami buried on Mount Hiba, Izanagi drew the sword he wore at his side, a **ten-hand-length sword** (*totsuka-no-tsurugi*) named **Ame-no-Ohabari**, and **cut off the head of his son Kagutsuchi**, the fire god whose birth had killed her. The *Kojiki* tells it in a single sentence, without comment. It is the first killing in the world, and a father kills his own child.

Even this death brought forth gods. The blood on the **point of the sword** spattered onto the rocks, and from it were born the gods of **rock-splitting** and **root-splitting**. From the blood on the **guard** came three more, among them **Takemikazuchi**, the thunder-sword god, who would one day win the land for Heaven. From the blood that ran down to the **hilt** and dripped through Izanagi's fingers came the gods of the **dragon-rain of the valleys**. From the fire god's body, from its head, chest, belly, genitals, hands and feet, rose **eight mountain gods**.

The song is Izanagi's grief turning into violence, and the strange law of this world: nothing dies without something new being born from it. It is the album's heaviest track before the descent. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §VIII.)
