## 1. Story Behind the Song
Before she went back into the palace to speak with the gods of Yomi, Izanami made one request: ***"Do not look at me."*** Then she went in, and she was gone so long that Izanagi could not wait any longer. He broke off the thick end tooth of the comb in the **left bunch of his hair**, lit it as a torch, and went inside.

What the light showed him was his wife as death had made her. Her body was **swarming with maggots**. On it sat **eight thunder gods** (*Ikazuchi*): Great Thunder on her head, Fire Thunder on her breast, Black Thunder on her belly, Crack Thunder on her loins, Young Thunder on her left hand, Earth Thunder on her right hand, Rumbling Thunder on her left foot, and Couchant Thunder on her right foot. Izanagi was terrified, and he fled.

*"You have put me to shame!"* cried Izanami. This is the first appearance of a motif that runs all through Japanese myth: a husband is forbidden to look at his wife in her true form, he looks, and he loses her forever. The song is Izanagi's impatience, the single light in the dark, and the end of their marriage in one glance. (*Kojiki*, Book 1, Chamberlain §IX.)
