# Masks and Mirrors

**Genre:** Dark cinematic orchestral hip hop

## Concept
Everyone wears a mask. Some wear it to be loved, some to avoid judgment, and some have worn it so long they've forgotten there's a person underneath. The album follows one man, the narrator, through a single long-running show: his own life, staged in a baroque theatre where every seat is full and the applause never stops. He's the funny one, the steady one, the son who's "doing great", the man in the meeting, the grin in the picture. Everybody loves the mask. Nobody knows what it costs.

Track by track, the performance comes apart. He meets a stranger in the dressing-room mirror, catches himself lying to an empty hallway, sits alone in a kitchen at three in the morning, and finds the jailers of his approval prison are only people who smiled. He splits into two faces, discovers that the critics in his head are cardboard cut-outs in his own handwriting, and builds a palace for the view with no room in it for himself. In a hall of mirrors he can no longer tell which version is real. Then he lies awake beside the one person he's afraid to be known by, and starts to talk.

He takes the mask off on the same stage, under the same lights, in front of the same crowd. Some of them leave. He lets them. The album ends the morning after, outside the stage door, on an ordinary grey street: no applause, no mirror, just a man with his own face on, walking out the door.

## Story Arc
1. **The stage (tracks 1–3):** the mask everyone loves; the stranger in the mirror; the lie so perfect he believes it too.
2. **The private room (tracks 4–5):** who he is with no audience; how much of himself he has sold for applause.
3. **The fracture (tracks 6–8):** the hypocrite with two faces; the audience that lives in his head; the gold frame on an empty wall.
4. **The breaking point (tracks 9–10):** a hundred reflections and no way to tell which one is him; the terror of letting one person see all of it.
5. **The unmasking (tracks 11–12):** the mask hits the floor; the morning after, with no more reflections.

## Sound
- **Reference artist:** none. The sound is described from its parts, not modelled on anyone: dark, theatrical, aggressive and emotionally intense, with orchestral hip hop production and **rap verses with huge sung choruses**.
- **Base style:**
  ```
  dark cinematic orchestral hip hop, sinister harpsichord, sharp staccato strings, eerie flute melody,
  deep 808 bass, hard-hitting drums, dramatic orchestral impacts, sudden silences, intense rapid-fire
  male rap, intricate internal rhymes, massive emotional sung chorus, theatrical, dark
  ```
- **Exclude styles (album default):** `pop, EDM, autotune, country, lo-fi, mumble rap, rock, distorted guitars, metal, growls, screamed vocals`
  - This album has **no guitars**. The weight comes from the 808s, the drums and the orchestral impacts.
  - **Shouted gang vocals are allowed** on tracks 5 and 11 (the two choruses meant to be shouted back). Screams and growls stay excluded everywhere.
- **Vocals:** one male voice throughout: whispered, spoken, rapped and sung. Track 6 splits him into two voices: the **Public Face** (smooth, warm, measured, like a man at a podium) and the **Hidden Face** (low, close, fast, venomous). Track 9 layers his voice over itself as the masks talk at once.
- **Tempo:** a rap pocket of 86–100 bpm with double-time verses. Tracks 4 and 10 are the slow ones (70–74 bpm). Track 12 sits in between (80 bpm).
- **Recurring motifs:**
  - **Sound:** the **harpsichord minuet** that opens track 1 is the mask's theme. It returns warped and out of tune in track 9, and in track 12 it's played once on a plain upright piano, with no harpsichord: the same melody, unmasked.
  - **Sound:** the **applause** opens track 1, turns into the murmuring crowd of track 7, and cuts out dead in track 11. Track 12 has none.
  - **Phrase:** *"Smile... wider"* (track 1 intro) is answered in track 12's bridge: *"Not wide. Just... true."*
  - **Image:** the **crack** in the mask: a hairline in track 3, down the middle in track 6, shattered mirrors in track 9, the porcelain splitting from forehead to jaw in track 11.
  - **Image:** the **mirror and the window**: the dressing-room mirror (track 2), the hall of mirrors (track 9), and in track 12 *"just a window, and the view."*
  - **Callbacks:** track 9's intro is a collage of lines from tracks 1–8. Track 11's second verse names one mask from each of tracks 2–10, a line each.
