# 08. Image Over Identity

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The most sardonic track on the album, introduced by a salesman: *"Ladies and gentlemen... the life. Look at it. Don't touch it. Whatever you do, don't look behind it."* The first verse is the showroom. A leased car he can't afford, which shines in the driveway so the neighbours know he scored. A flat with a view and an empty fridge. A watch worth a year's rent and no time to spare. A job title that grows longer every spring, and a brunch shot from above before he tastes it. He's the picture of success, framed and hung, *gold leaf on plaster, a ladder with no rungs*, and the inside of the dream is only a camera and a screen.

The second verse is comparison. A cousin on a beach, a friend with a promotion, a couple on a mountain with a ring the size of rent, a kitchen like a gallery. He measures every metre of his life against the feed like a tailor measuring for a suit he'll never wear. But he knows the angles too: the fight before the photo, the debt behind the name. Everyone is curating, a million tiny museums on display, and when you finally get the shot *you're still you in the picture, and the picture's all you've got.*

The fanfare cuts out for the bridge's confession: he got everything he wanted, but he never wanted any of it. He wanted them to see him have it. In verse 3 he eats dinner for one in his palace, with the lights on so the street can see it's fun, and remembers his grandfather in a shed with a lathe, making chairs nobody photographed. The old man never had a following, but every chair was honest, and so was he. The narrator has a thousand followers and nothing in his hands: *I built a perfect picture of a man, and never built the man.*
