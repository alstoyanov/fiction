## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The album opens with the curtain going up. A voice in the wings whispers *"Smile... wider,"* the house roars, and there he is: the version of the narrator that everyone loves. He's the life of the party, the shoulder to cry on, the son on the phone saying he's fine, the man in the meeting with all the answers, the grin in the picture. He has a different face for every voice that calls his name, and every one of them gets a standing ovation.

The second verse goes backstage. At two in the morning he peels it off and finds a ring around his jaw where the edge of the smile has cut in. He knows which version his father likes, which one his ex adored and which one the boss wants. He has been everyone they ordered: *a mask with a mask on*, with no idea where the core is. The bridge offers the audience a secret, then takes it back: *you'd stop clapping.*

The third verse sets up the album's central conflict. In the middle of a scene he feels the mask slip, and imagines taking it off in front of the whole house. Would they boo? Would they even recognise him? He presses it back on, lands the line, and the house goes up in thunder. *The mask says "thank you," every time.* The harpsichord minuet that opens this song is the mask's theme for the whole album; the applause that ends it is cut dead in track 11.
