# 04. Who Are You When Nobody's Watching?

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The first quiet on the album. No crowd, no camera, no one to be good for. It's three in the morning and the narrator is in his kitchen, shirtless, eating cereal out of the box, with his phone face-down on the counter so the light can't see him. The first verse is honest about who's standing there. He's petty, jealous when a friend wins, and types out a cruel comment before deleting it. He skips the dishes, the gym and the call to his mother. If you met him at a party you'd walk the other way. But he's the one who's here at three, so the question the crowd never asked has to be asked now: *is the real me the actor, or the guy without the mask?*

The second verse is the surprise. The man at three a.m. is not only the worst of him. He feeds the neighbour's cat and never mentions it. He leaves money for the man by the station without anyone seeing. He sings flat in the shower, cries at old cartoons, and writes honest poems in his notes app that he deletes by dawn. The worst of him is hiding, but so is the best, and the mask turns out to be neither: *just the middle that I polished up for you.*

In the bridge he half-remembers a saying, that you are only what you do when the lights are out (it's often attributed to D. L. Moody; the song paraphrases it). In verse 3 he sits down in the blue light from the street and looks at everything he's got, the petty and the gentle, the lazy and the kind, with nobody to sell it to. It's the closest he's come to himself. He isn't the hero or the villain, only a man without a crowd, and for the first time in a while he says his own name out loud.
