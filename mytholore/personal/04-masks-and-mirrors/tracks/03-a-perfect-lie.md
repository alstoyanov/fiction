# 03. A Perfect Lie

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The most deceptively cheerful track on the album. A mock-bright harpsichord plays under a voice that is far too sunny: *"Me? Oh, I'm good. Honestly, never better."* The first verse is the lie told to everyone else. "How's the job?" Amazing. "Are you sleeping?" Like a baby. He posts pictures from the beach while crying in the car, keeps "no worries" in his mouth like a mint he never chews, and fills his calendar so nobody can get through. He's a natural, a talent, a liar with a gift: *I could sell you your own name.*

The second verse is where it turns. The person he fools best isn't the audience; it's the man in bed at four in the morning telling himself it's just stress, just a phase, for eleven thousand days. He tells himself he's confident, that he didn't care when they laughed at him in school, that the money makes the emptiness alright. The verse lands on the song's thesis: *a lie you tell to others is a mask you put on, but a lie you tell yourself is the face, when the mask is gone.*

The bridge introduces the album's crack motif: a hairline, tiny, that nobody has noticed *yet*. In verse 3 he patches it with laughs and drinks and noise, but it spreads across his cheek. Then he catches himself alone in the hallway, saying "I'm fine" out loud to nobody at all. He realises he isn't lying for the audience anymore. He's lying to stay blind. The bright harpsichord returns slightly out of tune, and the last "I'm good" breaks.
