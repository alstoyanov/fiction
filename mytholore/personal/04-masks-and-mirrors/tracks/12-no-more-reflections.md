# 12. No More Reflections

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The conclusion, the morning after. The minuet from track 1 plays once more, this time on a plain, slightly worn upright piano with no harpsichord: the mask's theme, unmasked. The stage is empty, and there's a broken mask on the floor that turns out to be *just a mask. It never was anybody.* In the first verse the narrator walks out of the stage door into an ordinary grey day: no limousine, no flashbulbs, a bin truck, a pigeon, and a puddle with his face in it that he walks straight through. He orders a coffee without rehearsing it (the payoff of track 7), and the barista neither claps nor stares. He lost people last night, and for once he won't say that it's fine. But the person he told at four a.m., the one he was sure would leave, put the kettle on at sunrise and said: *"Thank you. Now I know."*

The second verse is an honest inventory, set against the list of masks in track 1. He still laughs too loud and talks too fast. He still gets jealous, petty and stuck in the past, still eats cereal at three, and still doesn't call his mother enough. The difference is that none of it is hidden in drawers or polished up. Some people will like him less, but at least the one they're judging is the one who's here. He used to be a hundred men and none of them were whole; now he's one, unfinished, and his own. *I'd rather be a rough sketch that's honest to the bone than a masterpiece in plaster that nobody's ever known.*

The bridge answers the first words of the album. Track 1 opened with *"Smile... wider."* Now he catches his reflection by accident in a shop window, and he's smiling: *not wide, just true.* Verse 3 claims no victory. There will be mornings he reaches for the mask, rooms where he goes quiet and days he wants the crowd, but he'll know when he's doing it, and say so. The theatre is dark behind him and the street is bright and grey. Nobody is keeping score, and he's just *a man with his own face on, walking out the door.* The album ends without applause and without a mirror: *"Just me. That's enough."*
