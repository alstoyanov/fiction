# 02. The Person in the Mirror

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
After the show, the dressing room. A ring of bulbs around the mirror, half of them dead. The narrator takes the mask off expecting relief, expecting the real him to be waiting underneath, and finds a stranger sitting in his chair instead. The stranger has his jaw, his jacket and the scar above his brow, but the eyes don't match the photos. In the first verse he leans in until his breath fogs the glass, and swears the reflection's lips move a beat late: *like he's dubbing my whole life, and he's a second out of date.*

The second verse is the inner critic. The man in the glass has a list and keeps it in order: the laugh's too loud, the voice is weak, remember that wedding, remember how she looked at you, you're a fraud and a fluke, too much and not enough. The narrator would fight anyone who spoke to him like that, but the voice is in his own mouth, so he nods and agrees. *There's no one in this city who's as cruel to me as me.*

The third verse is the first small softening on the album. He touches the glass and the reflection gives him back his old smile: not the stage one, but the crooked one from school, from before he learned which teeth to show. For a moment the stranger is a kid on the floor with crayons, before the roles and the audience. He doesn't hate him; he just hasn't seen him in years. He sits there waiting for the person to reappear, and the song ends with one more bulb going out.
