# 06. Two Faces

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The album's argument with itself. The narrator splits into two voices. The **Public Face** is smooth, warm and measured, a man at a podium who can't stand a hypocrite. The **Hidden Face** is low, close and fast, and only ever says one word at first: *liar.* In the first verse the Public Face preaches. He reads out the roster of everyone who ever cheated or lied, can't stand anyone fake, vain, jealous or gossiping, and grades the world in red pen: *bring me all your sins and I'll sort them into piles.*

The pre-chorus belongs to the Hidden Face, who knows what's in the drawer and who's on the phone. He knows the stones thrown at other people were all picked up at home. The second verse is his testimony. The man who hates liars told eleven lies today. The man who hates jealousy checked his rival's page at two in the morning. The man who preaches humility wants a statue on the hill, and the man who mocks a stranger's body has a stomach full of shame. *Every flaw he's ever mocked is a flaw that has his name.* Judging it in others is a lightning rod that keeps the spotlight off himself.

In verse 3 the two voices trade half-lines ("I'm a good man!" "You're a good show") until the crack from track 3 runs from forehead to chin and splits the mask down the middle: one half the sermon, the other half the sin. He is the villain of his own sermons, and every judgment he passed on others he should have passed on himself. The chorus never lets either face win: *two faces, and both of them are me.* The song ends with an apology that neither face is sure it made.
