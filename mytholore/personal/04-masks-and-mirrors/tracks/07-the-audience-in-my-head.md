# 07. The Audience in My Head

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The track 1 applause, turned inward. The narrator is alone in his flat, but he's never really alone: there's a theatre behind his temple, with somebody in every chair. The first verse is anxiety in concrete detail. He rehearses "a coffee, please" for eighteen blocks and has a script for every doorbell and every knock. When the waiter said "enjoy your meal", he said "you too", and he's been replaying it for two years. He writes a text fourteen times, adds a smiley, then deletes it for coming on too strong. He lives through Tuesday's meeting on Sunday in fifty versions, all of them bad. He's performing for a crowd that isn't even in the room.

The second verse names the faces in the gallery: his father in the front row with his arms crossed, the girl from tenth grade laughing at how he talks, the boss in the box seats taking notes on how he walks, and a stranger from the subway who once looked at him too long and bought a season ticket. He doesn't live inside his own life. He watches it from the rafters, like a camera on a crane, taking notes on his own funeral. Half of the audience don't even know his name, *but I built them all a theatre, and I'm the one who pays.*

In the bridge the whispering stops and he asks whether anyone is actually there, and the crowd answers all at once: *we're always here.* In verse 3 he turns on the house lights and finds that every critic is a cardboard cut-out holding a script in his own handwriting. The real people are too busy with the critics in their own heads; everyone on the subway has a balcony at home. Nobody is watching him as hard as he's afraid. In the outro he orders a coffee, and that's all he says.
