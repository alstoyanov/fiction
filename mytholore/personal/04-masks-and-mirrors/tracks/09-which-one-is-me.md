# 09. Which One Is Me?

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The psychological breaking point. The track 1 minuet returns warped and out of tune, while the narrator's own lines from earlier on the album talk over each other: *"Mom, I'm fine." "Honestly, never better." "I can't stand a hypocrite." "A coffee, please." "Ladies and gentlemen..."* He screams at them to stop, and in the sudden silence asks: *which one of you said that?* The first verse walks into a hall of mirrors where every glass reflects another glass. There's the son, the company man, the boyfriend and the clown, the saint at the podium and the sinner below, the one who's always fine and the one who's always funny, all of them in suits he remembers buying. He used to have a rule: *the real one is the one who takes the others off.* But now there are a hundred of them reaching for the mask.

The second verse is a roll call of the names other people use for him. "Sweetheart" for his mother, "the closer" for his boss, "a coward" for his ex, "the joker" for his friends, "legend" online and "fake" for his brother. He answers to all of them. He's a different height in every room and a different weight at home, a vending machine where you put in a coin and choose a face. He has played the parts so long that the seams have disappeared, *and the man who did the casting hasn't been seen in years.*

The bridge turns to the listener: close your eyes, picture your face, *which one did you see?* Then glass shatters. In verse 3 he smashes every mirror, but each shard holds another mask, so ten faces become a thousand. Bleeding from the knuckles, he sinks to the floor among the pieces and whispers: *please, just one of you be mine.* The minuet grinds to a stop. He doesn't know. This is the bottom of the album, and it leads straight into the four a.m. confession of track 10.
