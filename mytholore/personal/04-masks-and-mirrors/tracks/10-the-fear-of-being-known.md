# 10. The Fear of Being Known

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The deepest vulnerability on the album, and its quietest track. *"You asked me what I was thinking. I said 'nothing.' That was a lie too."* It's four in the morning and the narrator lies awake beside "you", the person who loves him, asleep with a hand on his chest. In the first verse he admits his worst fear: that what they love is only the better half, the funny one, the steady one, the man who calls on time. There's a box in the closet they've never seen him open, full of notebooks from the year he was quietly broken. Some nights the whole story is right behind his teeth. Then he pictures their face changing, *just a flicker, just a flinch*, and swallows it again.

The second verse explains where the fear comes from. He was loved for the performance from the day he learned to walk: a gold star for the smile, a sticker for the talk. He learned love as a prize for a show. He could survive them rejecting a role, or hating a costume he made. What he couldn't survive is them seeing what's underneath and quietly packing to leave. *The mask can take a beating; it was built for taking blows. But the face has never been outside, and it's softer than you know.*

The bridge is a whispered three-part idea, paraphrased from a thought often attributed to Timothy Keller: to be loved and never seen is safe; to be seen and never loved is the nightmare; to be seen all the way down and loved anyway is something he can't even imagine. In verse 3 he knows that if he doesn't say it now he never will, and he'll be the perfect stranger in their bed forever. He turns toward their shoulder, shaking like a kid on the high board. The mask is on the nightstand, smiling in the dark, and he takes a breath bigger than the room. The song ends on the first words of the confession, *"Are you awake?"*, and leaves what he says to track 11.
