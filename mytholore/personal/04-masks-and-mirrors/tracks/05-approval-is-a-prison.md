# 05. Approval Is a Prison

**Album:** Masks and Mirrors · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The most aggressive track in the first half, built on the sound of a phone. *Ding.* Somebody likes him. *Ding.* Do it again. The first verse is the addiction: he refreshes like a lab rat at a slot machine, and every heart is a pellet. It isn't only the phone, either. It's the nod across the table, the boss's "nice work", his father finally saying something kind. To earn a little praise he'd rearrange his skeleton. He laughs at what you laugh at, votes the way the room votes and orders what you ate. His opinions are a weathervane: *if you ask me what I think, I gotta check with you to know.*

The second verse counts what it's cost. He was going to be a painter, but "that doesn't pay". He was going to say he was hurting, but they said he was doing great. He folded the thing he wanted to say at dinner and left it under his seat. He dropped a friend in a week because the friend was bad for business, and the business was him. This is the song's central paradox: *the more of them that cheer for who I play, the less of me is left inside to hear a word they say.*

The bridge is cold and quiet: the guards of this prison don't even know they work here. They just smiled. In verse 3 he tries one honest "no, I disagree". The room drops thirty degrees and his heart runs like he's broken out of jail, with dogs and searchlights behind him. Nobody claps, nobody likes it, nobody shares it. It's awful, it's freezing, and it's his: *the first breath in days.* The song ends with a notification he doesn't look at.
