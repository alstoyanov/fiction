# 08. Too Small to Understand

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
Every discovery creates new questions. *"Ninety-five percent of the universe we can't see. We don't know what it is. We gave it a name so we'd feel better. 'Dark.'"* The first verse is a proud, breathless tour of what humanity has achieved. We split the atom and found a smaller box inside, then a thousand smaller locks. We found particles that are here and there until someone looks, and a thought-experiment cat both alive and dead. We wrote out the human genome three billion letters long, and can read the alphabet but not the rhyme. We pointed a telescope at a black, empty patch of night and found ten thousand galaxies. We've come so far so fast, *but every door we open, there's a hallway and a door.*

The pre-chorus gives the album's clearest image of knowledge: an island in an ocean of the dark, *and the bigger that the island grows, the longer is the coast.* The more we conquer, the more mystery we touch. The second verse turns to the strangest thing of all, the three pounds of grey matter behind his face, eighty-six billion neurons trying to understand the sky, which can't even understand themselves. An eye can't see itself and a blade can't cut its own edge, and no one can step outside the universe to look at it. A mathematician (Kurt Gödel, paraphrased) proved that some truths in any system of numbers can't be proved inside it. The greatest minds he has read all end up at the same place: at the edge of what they know, saying *"I don't know" like it's a door and not a wall.*

The bridge recalls the photograph a spacecraft took of Earth from beyond Neptune's orbit: home as a single pixel, with every war, love song and question inside it (a paraphrase of Carl Sagan's reflection on the image). In verse 3 he stands on the shoreline of the island and decides that smallness isn't shameful. It's where the wonder lives. If he understood everything, there'd be nothing left to find. *I'll be a kid in the sand, with a bucket and the ocean, and I'll love what I can't understand.*
