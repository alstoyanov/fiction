# 04. Dead Men Don't Explain

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The ultimate mystery, made personal. Last spring, room 412: a window, the hum of a vending machine, and the narrator's father getting smaller under the morphine. He holds the fingers that once held him steady on a bike. His father's eyes go somewhere distant, as if he's seeing past the wall, and the son asks, *"Dad, what's there?"* There's no answer. The breathing slows and stops, the nurse turns off the screen, and the vending machine hums on. For an hour he sits beside the only man who has finally crossed over and could tell him what remains. *Dead men don't explain.*

The second verse sets out every answer offered at the funeral. The preacher says he's home now, at rest. An aunt says he'll come back in another form, another breath. A cousin says it's lights out, the end. He nods at all of them because he doesn't want to pretend. He wants the gate and the garden, and his father on a porch with a coffee and the crossword. He's terrified of the nothing, *the universe forgetting that I ever knocked on the door.* He has read the old Greek argument (Epicurus, paraphrased) that death is nothing to fear because when it's here, you aren't. It's clever and logical, *and it doesn't help at four a.m. when I'm counting down my time.*

In the bridge he finds his father's matches in the kitchen drawer, the same box from track 1, and says out loud, *"I'm older now, Dad. I'm asking,"* to no answer. Verse 3 claims no afterlife and denies none. What he does know is that his father is in the way he holds a hammer, laughs before the punchline, and hums in the kitchen in a voice that isn't quite his own. It isn't heaven and it isn't proof, *but it's something that the dark can't take.* One day someone will ask the same question about him, and he hopes they'll hear him humming. The outro blows out a match: *"Goodnight, Dad. Wherever that is."*
