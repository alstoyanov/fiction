# 06. Saints Make Terrible People

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
Morality, justice and human judgment, and a warning about people who are sure they're good. The "saints" of the title aren't religious saints. They're the self-righteous of every age. *"Every century had its saints. Every century had its sinners. And every century swapped them."* The first verse walks through history. A woman burned in the square for knowing herbs and being strange, while the men who lit the fire went home to supper. People sold on the docks and called honest trade. Every crowd that ever cheered a hanging thought it was good, and every army believed it marched the way it should. The rules that make you holy in one century are the crimes read out in court in the next. So who decides what's right: the stone, the stars, God, culture, evolution, or the winners holding the pen?

The pre-chorus carries the album's match motif somewhere dark: the cruellest men in history weren't cartoon villains; *they were certain they were righteous when they lit the match.* The second verse moves from history to dilemmas where doing right causes suffering. It takes the runaway train and the lever that kills one to save five (Philippa Foot's trolley problem), and the philosopher who said never lie, even to a killer at the door asking where your neighbour hid (Kant, paraphrased). Then it turns personal. He has told the truth and watched a family break, and kept a secret for a frightened kid's sake, and doesn't know which was holy or wise. *The right thing's got a cost; sometimes somebody bleeds.*

The bridge is his confession: he doesn't trust people who never doubt they're good, he's trying to be good, and he doubts it every day. Verse 3 lands on what he can know, whether or not it's cosmic law: a crying child, a hungry stranger, a raised hand, the look of someone betrayed. He'd *rather doubt and show up than be certain and take aim.* His closing image is a compass whose needle is only useful if it's free to swing. The outro hopes the next century finds out our blind spots, as we found out theirs.
