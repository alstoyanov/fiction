# 10. Praying to an Empty Room

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
After all the questions, arguments and desperate searching, there is silence. *"I haven't done this since I was a kid. I don't even know if I'm doing it right. Hi."* The narrator kneels by the bed the way his grandmother taught him, in a room with one lamp on and his father's coat on the wall. He doesn't know who he's talking to: God, the ceiling, the cosmos, or the most honest part of himself. He has argued, doubted, said there's nothing there, and admits *I've left you on read too.* Tonight he isn't asking for answers, a miracle or a voice. He's just talking to the quiet *like you talk to someone asleep*, because some things are too heavy and too holy to keep.

The second verse is the honest list he has never said out loud. He hopes his father is somewhere, and not just gone. He hopes his daughter (first mentioned here) never has to sit in room 412 with him, and that when she asks him things he'll be honest. He hopes the kid from the children's ward made it to spring, and that the mother at the window (track 2) got to hear her baby sing. He hopes he's wrong about the nothing. He doesn't know whether hoping is prayer, or prayer is only hope, *or if it's just a drowning man who's talking to the rope.* But he feels lighter when he gives it to the air.

The bridge waits in a long silence. Nothing comes, and he answers *that's okay. I'll be here tomorrow.* Verse 3 changes how he hears the silence. It doesn't answer, but it doesn't send him away; it sits with him the way his grandmother did. He used to think silence meant nobody was home. Now he thinks it might be bigger than yes or no: *there are rooms you only enter if you're willing not to know.* The song is about continuing to hope without being given certainty. It ends with no answer and no verdict, just *"Goodnight. Whoever you are. Whatever you are."*
