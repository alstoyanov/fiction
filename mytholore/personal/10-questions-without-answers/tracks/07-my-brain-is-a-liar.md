# 07. My Brain Is a Liar

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
If our perception of reality is imperfect, how much can we really know? The song opens with an optical illusion (*"Look at the dot. Keep looking. Now tell me what colour the wall is. You sure?"*) and the first verse begins with a memory. He remembers his seventh birthday clearly: the red bike, the cake, his grandad by the lake. His mother says the bike was blue, and his grandad had been dead a year by then. Every time a memory is opened it's saved again, *like a photo of a photo, getting blurrier each day.* The brain is a painter, not a camera. There's even a blind spot in each eye where the nerve goes through, and the brain paints over it without saying so. *It's a rumour that my neurons told me, and I took it as read.*

The second verse is darker: self-deception. It recalls the split-brain experiments, in which patients whose brain halves had been separated would act on an instruction shown to one side, then confidently invent a reason for doing it. He wonders how much of his own day works like that. He's sure he chose the coffee, the girl, his politics and the world, but maybe he's a passenger narrating every turn as if he were at the wheel. And every dream feels real *until the second that I wake.*

The bridge asks the oldest sceptical question: what if this is the dream, and how would you know? In verse 3 he tears up every floorboard looking for one solid thing, and remembers a man in France (René Descartes, paraphrased) who did the same and doubted every sense until one thing was left that doubt couldn't kill: the doubter. His brain might be a liar and the world might be a guess, *but somebody is asking it, and somebody's me, I guess.* The outro returns to the dot. He still isn't sure what colour the wall is, but he's sure he's looking.
