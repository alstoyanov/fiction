# 01. Who Lit the First Match?

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The opening statement, and the album's first memory. The narrator is six. It's winter, the power has gone out, and the whole street is black. His father finds a matchbox at the back of a drawer and strikes one, and the kitchen comes alive in the glow. The boy asks the question he still can't answer: if you lit it with a match, who made the match? Who made the man who made it? Before there was a fire, who was holding up the flame? His father laughs, shakes it out and says *"ask me when you're older."* The question doesn't leave. It settles in his chest *like a pilot light that's burning when the rest of me's at rest.*

The second verse widens from one kitchen to all of human history. Every child of every century has asked it at the fire, from the painters in the caves to the builders of spires and telescopes. Priests, physicists, poets, sceptics and mystics each light their own match. The verse is even-handed on purpose: each answer just pushes the question back a step. If a god lit the match, who lit the god? If it all came out of nothing, why is nothing so strange? *Every answer that we lit just showed more dark behind.*

The bridge whispers the oldest version of the question, why there is something instead of nothing (a paraphrase of Leibniz), and says that nobody knows, and that this is where the question starts, not where it ends. In verse 3 he's grown, standing on a roof with a box of kitchen matches and *a lifetime with no proof*, about to go looking at every door on the album: God, truth, death and justice. The song ends with a match struck under the stars and his answer to his father: *I'm older now. I'm asking.* Its wordless choir chord returns, still unresolved, at the very end of track 12.
