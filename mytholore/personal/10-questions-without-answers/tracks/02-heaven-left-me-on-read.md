# 02. Heaven Left Me on Read

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
A conversation with God, or perhaps with an empty universe, told in the language of a phone: *message sent. Seen. No reply.* The narrator has been sending prayers since he was knee-high, on his knees by the bed, with the words his grandmother taught him. She told him *He always hears you, even when the silence is long.* So he prayed for the dog, for the rain, and for his mother when she couldn't stand the pain. The dog got better, the rain didn't come, and his mother kept on hurting. He never knew which was a yes, which was a no, and which was just the way things go. Every night the message says delivered, and the dots never come.

The second verse is the problem of suffering, told without abstractions. He's been to a children's ward, with crayon suns on the walls and beeping down the halls, and seen a mother with her forehead on the window asking for a sign, the way he once asked for his. He has read the books that explain why suffering is allowed (free will, the bigger picture) and found some of it beautiful and deep. *But none of it is something you can say to her while she weeps.* So he asks directly, not through the preacher or the page, and the verse breaks into the album's first shouted outburst: if you're there, say it, and if you're not, tell me that, *'cause the only thing worse than a "no" is not getting one.*

The bridge admits the hardest part: maybe the silence is the answer, maybe it's only silence, and he can't tell the difference. Verse 3 is about his grandmother, who died believing, hands folded, calm. He doesn't know where she went or who she met, but he has never seen a face that peaceful. The song respects her faith and his doubt equally, and doesn't choose between them. He keeps sending messages, *a message in a bottle on a black and endless shore*, and the outro is one more: *still here, in case you are.*
