# 03. Everybody's Got a Version

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
Everyone claims to know the truth, and everyone sees a different world. The song opens on voices arguing (*"That's not what happened." "I was there." "So was I."*) and the first verse gathers three kinds of disagreement. There are five witnesses to a crash on the corner: one says red, one says green, one says fast, one says slow, and every one of them is certain. There's a family Christmas where his uncle, his sister and his mum all grew up in the same house and remember a different father, a different childhood and a different reason for the fight. And there's the feed, where every scroll is a different planet and the algorithm keeps handing him back the version he already chose, *till the whole world looks like me, and that's the only world it shows.*

The second verse retells, in its own words, the old parable of the blind men and the elephant. One touches the side and says it's a wall, one the trunk and says it's a snake, one the leg and says it's a tree. They fight all night while the elephant stands there, bigger than their whole war. Maybe that's the truth about truth: we're all blind men with our hands against a wall. What frightens him most isn't people who don't know. It's the ones who'd burn the village down to prove it's just a rope. *Certainty's a fire, and it keeps you warm at night, but it's burned down every bridge that ever tried to cross the light.*

The bridge offers a gentler reading: maybe everyone's telling the truth about the small piece they can touch. In verse 3 he resolves to hold what he knows *like a candle, not a sword*, to let other versions shake his own, and to compare notes, because the only way anyone will learn the elephant's shape is by trading what they've felt. The outro brings back the opening argument with one change. *"That's not what happened"* is answered this time with *"Tell me what you saw."*
