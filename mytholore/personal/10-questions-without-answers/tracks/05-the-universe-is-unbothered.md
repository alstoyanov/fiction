# 05. The Universe Is Unbothered

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The universe doesn't promise fairness, explanations or meaning, and this track is the album's grimmest joke about that. *"Thirteen point eight billion years. Two trillion galaxies. And I'm upset about a parking ticket."* The first verse lists the unfairness: the sun will rise on the day he's buried, and the birds will sing as if nothing happened. There's a kid with a tumour and a crook with a yacht, a saint in the gutter and a liar on top. He keeps looking up as if the stars kept a ledger, as if the flood checked a list or the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs ever understood. The universe just shrugs, *a billion years of yawn*, and he's an ant demanding answers from the boot.

The second verse takes the old myth of Sisyphus, condemned to push a boulder up a mountain forever only to watch it roll back down. It also takes the French writer (Albert Camus, paraphrased) who looked at Sisyphus and said we should imagine him content, because the climb itself can fill a heart. The narrator laughed the first time he read it. Now he isn't laughing, because he's pushing his own rock every Monday. The universe won't clap and nobody's keeping score, *but the hands are mine, the hill is mine, and so's the view I saw.* The chorus turns the indifference around: the universe never made a promise, *so the meaning's mine to choose.*

The bridge is the hinge of the song: the stars don't know he's here, but he knows they're there, and that has to count for something. Verse 3 makes meaning out of small, defiant acts. He'll plant a tree he'll never sit under, cook for his friends, write a song for nobody, hold the door and hold the baby when she cries, not because anyone is watching but because he can. If he's only a spark that lasts a second in the night, *then I'm gonna burn like hell, and I'm gonna burn it right.* The song makes no claim about whether the universe has a purpose. It only refuses to wait for one.
