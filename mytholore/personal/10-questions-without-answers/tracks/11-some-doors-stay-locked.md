# 11. Some Doors Stay Locked

**Album:** Questions Without Answers · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The emotional turning point. *"I've tried every key I own. Some of these doors just don't open. Maybe they're not supposed to."* The first verse is a hallway in his head with a thousand doors, each marked with a question: *God*, *why*, *after*, *sky*, *truth* (scratched from his nails) and *Dad* (kicked until he failed). He has brought crowbars, scripture, theories, maths and every clever person who ever walked this way. Some doors opened easily, and he's grateful for the light. The big ones never moved an inch, and now he sits on the floor with his back against the wood and a keyring that never did him any good.

The pre-chorus starts the turn. He used to think a locked door was a failure, a test he wasn't smart or holy enough to pass. Maybe some rooms aren't built for us to enter, *and the hallway is the house.* The second verse recalls a poet's letter to a young man (Rainer Maria Rilke, paraphrased): be patient with the questions, don't kick at the door or hunt for the key, live inside the question, and one day you may live your way into an answer. A dog can't learn calculus, a fish can't know it's wet, and a moth can't read the moon it flies toward, and none of them are failures. Maybe a human mind has a ceiling too. *A mystery that fits inside my head would be a lie.* God or no God, an afterlife or an end: either might be bigger than any question he's built to understand. The verse says this without taking a side.

In the bridge he stops kicking the door, turns around, and finds the hallway had windows the whole time. Verse 3 is the album's warmest scene so far. The windows are enormous and the stars pour in, and the hallway is full of people: his daughter, his family, his father's coat, his grandmother's chair, friends around a fire, a table and a song. Nobody has the key and nobody's ashamed. They knock sometimes, listen, and call each other's names. The doors may stay locked for good, *but the hallway's full of life, and I'm living here for good.* He keeps the keys and will still try them sometimes. He's just not waiting at the door anymore.
