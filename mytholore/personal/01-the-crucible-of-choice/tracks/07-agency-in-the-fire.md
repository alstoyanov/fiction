# 07. Agency in the Fire

**Album:** The Crucible of Choice · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The heaviest track on the album, and the one where the crucible is most literal. The house is on fire, and it was always going to be. The phone rings at three in the morning; someone is sick, someone is gone; the job, the money, the news on the screen. In verse 1 the narrator tries to hold the walls up with his bare hands, as if worry could stop the rain. Then he understands that what he called control was only a fist clenched around everything.

Verse 2 recalls a man in a concentration camp who lost his wife, his work and his name. He wrote afterwards that everything could be taken from a person except one thing: the choice of how to meet each day. The narrator sets that against his own complaints about slow Wi-Fi and traffic, and turns it into the album's central image of metal in the fire: *I'm iron. Put me in it. I'll come out a blade.* He can't pick the weather or the flood, but he can pick the man still standing when they dig through the mud.

The chorus is the album's rawest vocal moment, a screamed *LET IT BURN*. It isn't nihilism; it's release. Verse 3 doesn't pretend he's fearless. He carries out the ones he can, mourns the ones he can't, and finds the only room the fire never reaches: *the space between what happens and the second I decide.*

The song's verse 2 paraphrases Viktor Frankl's *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946) without quoting it. The "space between" idea is popularly attributed to him too, and is also only paraphrased.
