# 02. Clay or Sculptor

**Album:** The Crucible of Choice · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
Having found that he built the cage, the narrator pushes back with the obvious objection: *did I, though?* He was six once. Before he could choose anything, other hands were already pressing into him: a house where love was weather you checked at the door, a father who taught "boys don't cry" without ever learning to cry himself, a school that rewarded the quiet ones, a screen that told him who to be and what it would cost. Verse 1 lays all of this out like evidence on a table. The mood is analytical rather than self-pitying.

Verse 2 cross-examines it. Yes, the early wiring is real; there's no shame in having been soft when they got to you first. But clay doesn't argue, doesn't read, doesn't wake at four in the morning trying to understand itself. *A vessel that can see its cracks* has stopped being only material. The bridge forgives the hands, but says that forgiving them isn't the same as letting them keep working.

Verse 3 is the turn. He picks up the tools they left on the floor. He'll never be unshaped, and the marks stay. But the clay is in his hands now, *and now it's my turn.* The final chorus answers the song's question by changing it: he is the sculptor **and** the clay.
