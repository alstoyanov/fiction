# 08. The Final Verdict

**Album:** The Crucible of Choice · **Type:** Personal · **Status:** Writing

---

## 1. Meaning Behind the Song
The reckoning. Three gavel strikes, and the ticking clock and staccato piano from track 1 return. Court is in session in the narrator's head. When he asks who the judge is, he's told to look up. It's him, thirty years from now, and he remembers everything. The song is a courtroom interrogation in two voices: the **defendant**, fast and frantic and full of reasons, and the **judge**, deep, slow and cold.

The evidence is small and devastating. Exhibit A is a notebook full of songs he never sang. Exhibit B is a ticket he bought and never used. Exhibit C is a phone call to his mother that he didn't make because he was busy, and then it was too late. His objection, *you don't know what I went through*, is overruled: *I'm the only one who does. I went through it with you.* In verse 2 he calls his witnesses (the city, the teacher who said "never", the girl, the boss, the bottle). They've all testified and admitted what they did. Then every one of them said the same thing: *after that... it was him.*

The bridge is the album's twist. The defendant begs to be sentenced and be done with it. The judge, softer, says he can't, because he isn't here yet. The trial is a warning, and the defendant decides which version of the judge walks into the room. Verse 3 turns the defendant into a man with thirty years to change the evidence. The final chorus rewrites the verdict: the ink is still wet; *not guilty... not yet.*
