# Lyrics Guide

## Voice
- **Tell the myth, don't explain it.** Put the listener inside the scene: the storm, the oath, the descent. The background belongs in `lore.md` and the release notes, not in the verses.
- **One point of view per track.** State it in the track file (narrator, a god, a mortal, a chorus of the dead).
- **Concrete over abstract.** "The wolf breaks the chain of Gleipnir" works better than "destiny unfolds".
- **Let names land.** Put the key names on stressed beats and at the ends of lines, so Suno sings them clearly.

## Structure
- **House style: three verses per song.** A typical track runs **Intro → Verse 1 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → Solo → Verse 3 → Chorus → Outro**. Each verse moves the story on, and Verse 3 usually carries the turn or the consequence. Change the order when the story needs to, but keep all three verses.
- **Choruses are anthems.** They should be short, repeatable and singable, and usually carry the album's central image.
- **Epics** (7+ minutes) should be built in movements, using section tags such as `[Part I: …]`, plus extends in Suno.
- **Length:** with three verses, a five-to-six-minute track holds roughly 300–400 words. Above that, Suno tends to rush or cut; use Extend, or trim the repeated choruses in the text.

## Craft
- Keep the line lengths within each section even. Suno phrases more cleanly when lines have similar syllable counts.
- Rhyme is optional, but rhythm isn't. Read every line aloud against a pulse.
- Words in the original language work best in choruses and chants, where repetition helps pronunciation. Always check the meaning (`lore.md` › Words and Phrases).
- Put harsh and clean vocal cues in tags (see `suno.md`), not in the lyrics themselves.

## Sensitivity
Some mythologies belong to **living religions and communities**: for example Yoruba (Ifá, Òrìṣà), Vodou, Hindu, Shinto, Aboriginal Australian, Māori, and many Indigenous nations of the Americas. For these:
- Research **from sources written by people inside the tradition** where possible, and list them in `lore.md`.
- **Don't** present sacred rites, prayers or restricted stories as entertainment, and don't put words in the mouth of a deity that is actively worshipped in a way the tradition would find offensive.
- Prefer stories that are already told publicly, and frame the album as a tribute or retelling.
- Record your choices in `lore.md` › Sensitivity Notes.

Historical traditions with no living practice (for example the Mesopotamian or Aztec state religion as recorded) still deserve accuracy. For them, the main risk is cliché, not offence.
